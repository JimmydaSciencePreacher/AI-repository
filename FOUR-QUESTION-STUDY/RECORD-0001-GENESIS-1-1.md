# Study Record 0001 — Genesis 1:1 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:1 (KJV)**

> In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.

Opening subject: the first verse of the KJV Bible. Genesis 1:1 is selected because this study begins with the Scriptural text at the beginning of the Bible, not because a previous study prescribed this placement.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:1 state at the outset, what distinctions does its wording establish, and what questions does it give the study reason to investigate next?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:1 (KJV): “In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.”

This is the opening declaration of Genesis. The verse names:
- a beginning;
- God as the subject of the action;
- the action “created”;
- “the heaven and the earth” as the objects of that action.

The verse itself does not, in this single sentence, explain the process, duration, material means, or detailed ordering of creation. Those questions must be examined from the surrounding KJV text rather than supplied by assumption.

## Direct observations

1. The sentence begins with “In the beginning.”
2. “God” is the grammatical subject.
3. “Created” is the action attributed to God.
4. “The heaven and the earth” are named as what God created.
5. The verse does not name a human witness or describe a human act of observation.
6. The verse does not yet detail the subsequent events of the creation account.

These are observations about the wording. Further theological or architectural descriptions must be tested against the broader Scriptural context.

## Four-Question Study Method

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

The first necessary question is:

**What does GUA-W identify as the relationship between God, the act of creating, and the created heaven and earth—and which parts of that relationship are stated directly in Genesis 1:1 versus established elsewhere in Scripture or GUA-W?**

This question preserves the distinction between the verse's explicit wording and any framework used to examine it. The next investigation must not treat an explanatory label as though it were part of the verse.

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

At this opening point, the verse supplies the primary distinction for examination: God is the One who creates; heaven and earth are what He creates.

The study must now investigate whether and how the rest of the KJV text identifies the Word, the manner of creation, the ordering of creation, and the relation between the Creator and the created. Genesis 1:1 alone does not answer all of those questions.

No additional GUA-W mechanism or scientific explanation is declared established by this verse alone.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

I am witnessing a Scriptural declaration of creation. Its wording places God as the acting subject and heaven and earth as the created objects.

The verse establishes the starting distinction between Creator and creation. It does not present creation as the author of itself, nor does it identify a created witness as the source of the creative act.

The verse's concise statement invites examination of the account that follows, while its unstated details remain open for examination rather than conjecture.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

The verse is a written testimony received and examined by a reader. The reader can report what the text says, distinguish that wording from interpretation, and acknowledge what the verse has not yet specified.

The reader is not the Creator described in the verse and does not acquire authorship or authority over the event by examining its testimony.

This record therefore distinguishes:
- **Author / acting subject in the verse:** God.
- **What is created:** the heaven and the earth.
- **Present witness in this study:** the KJV text as read and the reader's registration of its wording.
- **Limit of this record:** it examines the declaration in Genesis 1:1; it does not claim to have exhausted the full creation account.

### 1. Biblical compliance

The direct findings above follow the wording of Genesis 1:1 (KJV). Broader claims about the creation account must be checked against Genesis 1–2 and relevant Scripture in context.

### 2. Scientific coherence

No scientific claim is required to state what Genesis 1:1 says. No scientific mechanism, measurement, or empirical conclusion is asserted in this record. Scientific coherence is therefore not used to manufacture an explanation that the present subject has not raised.

### 3. GUA-W consistency

The record preserves the distinction between the Creator and what is created, and does not assign authorship to the witness or reader. No further GUA-W structure is declared from this verse without supporting source material.

### Joint admissibility result

The direct textual claims are admitted as observations of the verse. Claims that the verse alone supplies a complete process, scientific mechanism, or complete GUA-W architecture are not admitted. No conflict among the three constraints is asserted where no scientific claim is being made.

## Admitted findings

1. Genesis 1:1 declares that God created the heaven and the earth.
2. The verse distinguishes God, the Creator, from the heaven and earth, which are created.
3. The verse is the opening declaration; its details must be read alongside the remainder of the KJV creation account.

## Not admitted

- Any detailed creation mechanism not stated in the verse or yet examined in its wider context.
- Any claim that this single verse contains every later distinction or operation in GUA-W.
- Any scientific conclusion attributed to Genesis 1:1 without a defined scientific question and evidence.
