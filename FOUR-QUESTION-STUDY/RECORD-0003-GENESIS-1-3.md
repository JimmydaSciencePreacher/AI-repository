# Study Record 0003 — Genesis 1:3 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:3 (KJV)**

> And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:3 state about God speaking, the command “Let there be light,” and the recorded appearance of light? What relationship does the verse establish between what God says and what follows?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:2 (KJV):
> And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.

Genesis 1:3 (KJV):
> And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.

Genesis 1:4 (KJV):
> And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness.

Genesis 1:5 (KJV):
> And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first day.

The immediate account therefore records: the condition described in verse 2; God's speaking and the appearance of light in verse 3; God's seeing and dividing the light from darkness in verse 4; and God's naming of light and darkness in verse 5. This record examines verse 3. Verses 4–5 are context for the next examination, not conclusions already established here.

## Direct observations

1. The verse identifies God as the speaker: “And God said”.
2. The words spoken are: “Let there be light”.
3. The verse then records: “and there was light”.
4. The wording places God's speech before the statement that light was present.
5. The verse does not name a created intermediary that issues the command.
6. The verse does not specify a physical mechanism, material process, duration, or scientific model by which light appeared.
7. The verse does not yet state that God saw the light was good or that He divided light from darkness; those statements occur in verse 4.
8. The verse does not yet name the light Day; that naming occurs in verse 5.

The text's sequence is directly observable. Further claims about the nature of the light or the physical process must be examined separately and must not be inserted into the wording.
