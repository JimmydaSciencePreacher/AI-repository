# Study Record 0004 — Genesis 1:4 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:4 (KJV)**

> And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:4 state about God's seeing the light, His declaration that it was good, and His dividing the light from the darkness? What distinctions and relationships does the verse establish?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:2 (KJV):
> And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.

Genesis 1:3 (KJV):
> And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.

Genesis 1:4 (KJV):
> And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness.

Genesis 1:5 (KJV):
> And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first day.

The immediate context records God's command and the appearance of light in verse 3; God's seeing, the declaration that the light was good, and the division of light from darkness in verse 4; and the naming of light and darkness in verse 5. This record examines verse 4. The naming in verse 5 is noted as the next statement, not treated as part of verse 4.

## Direct observations

1. The verse identifies God as the One who saw the light.
2. The verse records God's assessment: “that it was good”.
3. The verse then records that God divided the light from the darkness.
4. Both seeing and dividing are actions attributed to God in the verse.
5. The light being assessed is the light whose appearance was recorded in Genesis 1:3.
6. Darkness was already mentioned in Genesis 1:2 as being “upon the face of the deep”.
7. The verse does not specify a physical mechanism for the division.
8. The verse does not define “good” as a technical scientific term, nor does it provide a complete philosophical definition of goodness.
9. The verse does not yet name the light Day or the darkness Night; that statement occurs in Genesis 1:5.

The order of the clauses is recorded as written. The verse connects God's seeing and assessment with His act of dividing, but it does not explain every physical or temporal detail of that division.
