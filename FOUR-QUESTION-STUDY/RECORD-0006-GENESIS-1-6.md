# Study Record 0006 — Genesis 1:6 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:6 (KJV)**

> And God said, Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:6 state when God commands a firmament in the midst of the waters and commands that it divide the waters from the waters? What relationship does the verse establish among God's speech, the firmament, and the division?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:6 (KJV):
> And God said, Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters.

Genesis 1:7 (KJV):

Genesis 1:8 (KJV):
> And God called the firmament Heaven. And the evening and the morning were the second day.

Genesis 1:9 (KJV):
> And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so.

The immediate sequence matters. Verse 6 records God's command. Verse 7 records God making the firmament and the division of waters above and below it. Verse 8 records God's naming of the firmament Heaven and the second-day statement. Verse 9 begins another command concerning the waters under the heaven and the appearance of dry land. This record examines verse 6 and uses the following verses to clarify the immediate fulfillment without collapsing separate statements.

## Direct observations

1. God is the speaker: “And God said”.
2. The command is: “Let there be a firmament”.
3. The firmament is commanded to be “in the midst of the waters”.
4. A second command is stated: “let it divide the waters from the waters”.
5. The verse therefore explicitly relates the commanded firmament to a dividing function.
6. Verse 6 is a command; verse 7 subsequently records God making the firmament and the division of waters under and above it.
7. Verse 8 subsequently records that God called the firmament Heaven.
8. Verse 6 does not itself define the material composition, dimensions, physical mechanism, or modern scientific identity of the firmament.
9. Verse 6 does not say that the waters divide themselves or that the firmament originates its own command.
10. The text's order is clear, but the verse alone does not provide a complete physical model of the waters or the firmament.

The KJV word “firmament” is retained as written. This record does not silently substitute a modern scientific term or treat a proposed translation or model as though it were the wording of the KJV.
