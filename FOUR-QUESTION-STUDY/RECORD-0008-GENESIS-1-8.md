# Study Record 0008 — Genesis 1:8 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:8 (KJV)**

> And God called the firmament Heaven. And the evening and the morning were the second day.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:8 state when God calls the firmament Heaven and the evening and morning are identified as the second day? How does this naming relate to the making and division recorded in Genesis 1:7?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:6 (KJV):
> And God said, Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters.

Genesis 1:7 (KJV):
> And God made the firmament, and divided the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above the firmament: and it was so.

Genesis 1:8 (KJV):
> And God called the firmament Heaven. And the evening and the morning were the second day.

Genesis 1:9 (KJV):
> And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so.

The immediate sequence distinguishes the command in verse 6, the making and division in verse 7, the naming and second-day statement in verse 8, and the next command in verse 9. This record examines verse 8 while preserving those distinctions.

## Direct observations

1. God is the subject of the naming: “And God called”.
2. The object named is “the firmament”.
3. The name given in the verse is “Heaven”.
4. The verse then states: “And the evening and the morning were the second day.”
5. The naming follows the making and division described in verse 7.
6. Verse 8 does not repeat the making or dividing actions; it records naming and the second-day designation.
7. Verse 9 begins another command, concerning the gathering of the waters under the heaven and the appearance of dry land.
8. The verse does not give a scientific definition of “Heaven” or a physical description of the firmament's composition.
9. The verse does not state that the firmament names itself; the naming is attributed to God.
10. The verse does not specify a modern clock measurement or independently explain the physical chronology represented by evening and morning.

The wording “God called the firmament Heaven” is retained. This record does not silently replace “Heaven” with a modern astronomical or atmospheric term, nor does it assume that all uses of “heaven” throughout Scripture refer to precisely the same scope. Those questions require wider contextual examination.
