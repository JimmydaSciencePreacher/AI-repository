# Study Record 0009 — Genesis 1:9 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:9 (KJV)**

> And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:9 establish when God commands the waters under the heaven to be gathered together unto one place and dry land to appear? How does this command relate to the firmament named Heaven in verse 8 and to the distinction between waters above and waters under the firmament recorded in verse 7?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:7 (KJV):
> And God made the firmament, and divided the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above the firmament: and it was so.

Genesis 1:8 (KJV):
> And God called the firmament Heaven. And the evening and the morning were the second day.

Genesis 1:9 (KJV):
> And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so.

Genesis 1:10 (KJV):
> And God called the dry land Earth; and the gathering together of the waters called he Seas: and God saw that it was good.

Genesis 1:11 (KJV):
> And God said, Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself, upon the earth: and it was so.

The sequence is important. Verse 7 records the division of waters above and below the firmament. Verse 8 records God calling the firmament Heaven. Verse 9 then specifically addresses “the waters under the heaven” and commands their gathering together and the appearance of dry land. Verse 10 records the naming of the dry land Earth and the gathered waters Seas, followed by God's assessment that it was good. Verse 11 begins the next command concerning vegetation.

This record examines verse 9. Verse 10 is used as immediate context, while its naming and assessment are kept distinct from the command in verse 9.

## Direct observations

1. God is the speaker: “And God said”.
2. The first command concerns “the waters under the heaven”.
3. Those waters are commanded to “be gathered together unto one place”.
4. The second command is: “let the dry land appear”.
5. The verse ends: “and it was so”.
6. The verse's two commanded outcomes are related in one sentence: the gathering of the waters and the appearance of dry land.
7. The verse refers specifically to waters under the heaven. It does not say in this verse that the waters above the heaven are gathered.
8. The command follows the earlier account of waters divided above and below the firmament, which God called Heaven (verses 7–8).
9. Verse 9 does not itself name the dry land Earth or the gathered waters Seas; those names are given in verse 10.
10. Verse 9 does not specify the physical mechanism, rate, geography, depth, or material processes by which the waters are gathered or dry land appears.
11. The verse does not state that the waters gather themselves as the originating cause. God is the speaker issuing the command.
12. The phrase “and it was so” is part of the verse's report. It does not, by itself, provide a complete physical explanation of the event.

The KJV wording is retained. “Gathered together unto one place” is not silently converted into a modern geological theory, and “dry land appear” is not replaced with an assumed mechanism.
