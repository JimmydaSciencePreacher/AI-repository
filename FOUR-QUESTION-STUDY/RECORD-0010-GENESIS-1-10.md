# Study Record 0010 — Genesis 1:10 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:10 (KJV)**

> And God called the dry land Earth; and the gathering together of the waters called he Seas: and God saw that it was good.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:10 establish when God calls the dry land Earth, calls the gathering together of the waters Seas, and sees that it was good? How do naming and God's assessment relate to the command and result recorded in Genesis 1:9?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:7 (KJV):
> And God made the firmament, and divided the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above the firmament: and it was so.

Genesis 1:8 (KJV):
> And God called the firmament Heaven. And the evening and the morning were the second day.

Genesis 1:9 (KJV):
> And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so.

Genesis 1:10 (KJV):
> And God called the dry land Earth; and the gathering together of the waters called he Seas: and God saw that it was good.

Genesis 1:11 (KJV):
> And God said, Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself, upon the earth: and it was so.

Verse 9 records God's command concerning the waters under the heaven and the appearance of dry land. Verse 10 names the dry land Earth and the gathering together of the waters Seas, then records God's assessment that it was good. Verse 11 begins a new command concerning what the earth is to bring forth.

This record examines verse 10. The command in verse 9 and the next command in verse 11 provide immediate context but are not merged with the statements of verse 10.

## Direct observations

1. God is the subject of both naming actions: “And God called”.
2. God calls “the dry land” Earth.
3. God calls “the gathering together of the waters” Seas.
4. The verse then states: “and God saw that it was good”.
5. The dry land and the gathering together of the waters are the referents being named in the sentence.
6. Verse 9 had already recorded the command for the waters under the heaven to be gathered together unto one place and for dry land to appear.
7. Verse 10 does not repeat the command to gather the waters or to make dry land appear. It records the naming of what verse 9 described, followed by God's assessment.
8. The text attributes the naming and the assessment to God. It does not attribute either action to the land, the waters, or the Witness.
9. The verse does not specify the physical dimensions, geography, number, boundaries, or geological formation of the Earth or Seas.
10. The verse does not provide a technical scientific definition of “good”.
11. The verse does not state that naming is the same action as gathering the waters or making dry land appear.
12. Verse 11 begins a distinct command concerning vegetation; it is not part of verse 10.

The textual sequence is preserved: command and stated result in verse 9; naming and assessment in verse 10; a new command in verse 11. This is a description of the order in the passage, not a claim that every physical intermediate event is specified.
