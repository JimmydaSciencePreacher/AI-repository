# Study Record 0011 — Genesis 1:11 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

## Question under examination

What distinctions does the verse make concerning the earth, what is brought forth, seed, kind, and fruit?

## Direct observations

1. God is the speaker: “And God said”.
2. The command is: “Let the earth bring forth”.
3. The earth is the grammatical subject of the commanded bringing forth.
4. The verse names three descriptions: “grass”, “the herb yielding seed”, and “the fruit tree yielding fruit”.
5. The fruit tree is described as yielding fruit “after his kind”.
6. The phrase “whose seed is in itself” follows the description of the fruit tree. In the sentence, it is associated with the fruit tree; the verse does not explicitly extend that exact phrase to every item previously named.
7. The phrase “upon the earth” is included in the command.
8. The verse concludes: “and it was so”.
9. The command is spoken by God. The verse does not identify the earth as the originating authority or source of the command.
10. The verse does not describe the biological or physical mechanism by which the earth brings forth the vegetation.
11. The verse does not specify species, genetic mechanisms, rates of growth, soil chemistry, pollination, or ecological conditions.
12. Verse 12 reports the earth bringing forth the vegetation and repeats seed and kind language, with some wording differences. Those details belong to verse 12 when quoting or examining that verse.
13. Verse 11 does not say that the earth independently decides what to bring forth; it records God's command.

The direct wording and grammatical relationships are retained. No modern biological explanation is inserted into the KJV sentence.
