# Study Record 0013 — Genesis 1:13 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:13 (KJV)**

> And the evening and the morning were the third day.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:13 establish when it states, “And the evening and the morning were the third day,” and how does that day designation close the sequence of Genesis 1:9–12?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:9 (KJV):
> And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so.

Genesis 1:10 (KJV):
> And God called the dry land Earth; and the gathering together of the waters called he Seas: and God saw that it was good.

Genesis 1:11 (KJV):
> And God said, Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself, upon the earth: and it was so.

Genesis 1:12 (KJV):
> And the earth brought forth grass, and herb yielding seed after his kind, and the tree yielding fruit, whose seed was in itself, after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

Genesis 1:13 (KJV):
> And the evening and the morning were the third day.

Genesis 1:13 follows the account of the gathering of waters, appearance and naming of dry land, and the bringing forth of vegetation. It identifies the evening and morning as the third day. The verse does not repeat the creative commands or the descriptions of the results; it gives the day designation.

This record examines that designation and its role in the immediate sequence. It does not attempt to settle questions about modern clock time, the length of a day by external measurement, or a physical chronology not stated in this verse.
