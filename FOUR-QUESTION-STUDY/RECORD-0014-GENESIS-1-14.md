# Study Record 0014 — Genesis 1:14 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:14 (KJV)**

> And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:14 establish when God commands lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night, and assigns them for signs, seasons, days, and years?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:13 (KJV):
> And the evening and the morning were the third day.

Genesis 1:14 (KJV):
> And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:

Genesis 1:15 (KJV):
> And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth: and it was so.

Genesis 1:16 (KJV):
> And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also.

Genesis 1:17 (KJV):
> And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth,

Genesis 1:18 (KJV):
> And to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness: and God saw that it was good.

Genesis 1:19 (KJV):
> And the evening and the morning were the fourth day.

Verse 14 records God speaking and stating that lights are to be in the firmament of the heaven. It gives a dividing function—day from night—and says that they are to be for signs, seasons, days, and years. Verse 15 adds the purpose of giving light upon the earth. Verses 16–18 record further details about the lights, their making, setting, and functions. Verse 19 identifies the fourth day.

This record examines the command and assigned purposes stated in verse 14. The following verses are included as immediate context, but their additional statements are not silently treated as if they were all contained in verse 14.

## Direct observations

1. God is the speaker: “And God said”.
2. The command is: “Let there be lights”.
3. The stated location is “in the firmament of the heaven”.
4. One stated function is “to divide the day from the night”.
5. The verse then states: “and let them be for signs”.
6. It also names “seasons”, “days”, and “years”.
7. The verse uses plural “lights” and plural pronouns “them”.
8. Verse 14 does not name the lights as the sun and moon. Those names or descriptions must not be inserted into this verse; verse 16 gives further descriptions as “two great lights” and “the stars also”.
9. Verse 14 does not state the physical mechanism by which the lights divide day from night.
10. Verse 14 does not define the meaning or full range of “signs” or “seasons”.
11. Verse 14 does not specify a calendar system, the length of a year in days, or a modern astronomical measurement.
12. Verse 14 does not itself say “to give light upon the earth”; that wording appears in verse 15.
13. Verse 14 does not itself say that the lights rule over day and night; that wording appears in verses 16 and 18.
14. Verse 14 does not contain the assessment “good”; that is recorded in verse 18.
15. Verse 14 records an appointed purpose for the lights. The verse does not say that the lights originate their own purpose or authority.
16. The conjunction “and” connects the stated purposes in the sentence. The verse presents dividing day from night and being for signs, seasons, days, and years as purposes assigned to the lights.
17. The verse does not define each listed purpose in technical detail. Their exact scope requires further textual examination rather than assumption.

The direct finding is that God speaks of lights in the firmament and assigns them the stated functions and purposes. This record preserves the verse's own wording and does not import details from verses 15–18 or modern astronomy into it.
