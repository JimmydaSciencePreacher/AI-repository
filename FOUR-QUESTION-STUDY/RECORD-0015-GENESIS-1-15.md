# Study Record 0015 — Genesis 1:15 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:15 (KJV)**

> And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth: and it was so.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:15 add when God says that the lights are to be in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth, and the text records, “and it was so”? How does this added purpose relate to the command in verse 14?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:14 (KJV):
> And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:

Genesis 1:15 (KJV):
> And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth: and it was so.

Genesis 1:16 (KJV):
> And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also.

Genesis 1:17 (KJV):
> And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth,

Genesis 1:18 (KJV):
> And to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness: and God saw that it was good.

Genesis 1:19 (KJV):
> And the evening and the morning were the fourth day.

Verse 14 records God's command concerning lights in the firmament and their purposes in dividing day from night and serving for signs, seasons, days, and years. Verse 15 continues the sentence with a further purpose: the lights are to give light upon the earth. The verse concludes, “and it was so.” Verse 16 then records that God made the two great lights and the stars; verses 17–18 add that God set them in the firmament, describe their functions, and record God's assessment.

This record examines verse 15 and its relation to verse 14. The following verses are immediate context, but their additional statements must remain attributed to their own verses. The wording “and it was so” is recorded as part of verse 15 and should not be ignored or assigned a mechanism the text does not state.

## Direct observations

1. Verse 15 begins with “And”, continuing the statement in verse 14.
2. The verse uses “them”, referring back to the lights mentioned in verse 14.
3. It says the lights are to be “for lights”.
4. Their stated location is “in the firmament of the heaven”.
5. Their stated purpose in this verse is “to give light upon the earth”.
6. The verse concludes: “and it was so”.
7. The speaker and source of the command are established by the preceding words in verse 14: “And God said”.
8. Verse 15 does not introduce a new named speaker.
9. Verse 15 adds the purpose of giving light upon the earth to the purposes stated in verse 14.
10. The phrase “and it was so” affirms the command's stated outcome in the narrative. The verse does not explain the physical process or mechanism by which it was so.
11. Verse 15 does not itself name the lights as the sun and moon; verse 16 supplies the descriptions “two great lights” and “the stars also”.
12. Verse 15 does not itself describe God making the lights; that statement appears in verse 16.
13. Verse 15 does not itself state that God set the lights in the firmament; that statement appears in verse 17.
14. Verse 15 does not itself state that the lights rule over day and night; that wording appears in verses 16 and 18.
15. Verse 15 does not itself record the assessment “good”; that appears in verse 18.
16. The wording “to give light upon the earth” states an assigned function. It does not say that the lights originate their own purpose or authority.
17. The verse does not specify intensity, spectrum, distance, physical composition, or a modern astronomical mechanism.
18. The verse does not define “light upon the earth” in terms of modern measurements or units.
19. The verse does not, by itself, resolve all questions about the relation between the command, the affirmation “and it was so”, and the further description of making and setting in verses 16–17.
20. The verse must be read in sequence with verse 14 and not detached from its antecedent “them”.

The direct finding is that verse 15 continues God's command and adds the stated function of giving light upon the earth, followed by “and it was so”. The text then proceeds in verse 16 to describe God's making of the lights. This order must be preserved without forcing an explanation of the relationship beyond what the wording supports.
