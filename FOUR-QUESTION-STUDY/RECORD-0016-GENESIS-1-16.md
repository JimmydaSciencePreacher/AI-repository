# Study Record 0016 — Genesis 1:16 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:16 (KJV)**

> And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:16 establish when it says God made two great lights—the greater light to rule the day and the lesser light to rule the night—and made the stars also? How must the Witness distinguish the making of these lights, their stated differences, and their assigned rule?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:14 (KJV):
> And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:

Genesis 1:15 (KJV):
> And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth: and it was so.

Genesis 1:16 (KJV):
> And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also.

Genesis 1:17 (KJV):
> And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth,

Genesis 1:18 (KJV):
> And to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness: and God saw that it was good.

Genesis 1:19 (KJV):
> And the evening and the morning were the fourth day.

Verse 14 records God's command concerning lights and their purposes. Verse 15 continues the command, assigning the lights to give light upon the earth and concluding, “and it was so.” Verse 16 then explicitly says that God made two great lights, distinguishes them as greater and lesser, assigns their rule in relation to day and night, and adds that He made the stars also. Verse 17 says God set them in the firmament of heaven. Verse 18 continues the description of their functions and records God's assessment that it was good. Verse 19 identifies the fourth day.

This record examines what verse 16 itself says. The surrounding verses are context, not permission to transfer every detail into verse 16. In particular, verse 16 does not use the words “sun” or “moon”; it says “the greater light” and “the lesser light”. The familiar identifications may be considered in a broader interpretive inquiry, but this record preserves the verse's own terminology.

## Direct observations

1. God is the grammatical subject of “made”: “And God made”.
2. The verse says God made “two great lights”.
3. It distinguishes the two lights comparatively: “the greater light” and “the lesser light”.
4. The greater light is associated with ruling “the day”.
5. The lesser light is associated with ruling “the night”.
6. The verse then repeats the subject and verb: “he made”.
7. The object of this second clause is “the stars also”.
8. The word “also” adds the stars to the account of what God made.
9. The verse attributes the making of both the two great lights and the stars to God.
10. The verse does not call the greater light “the sun” or the lesser light “the moon”.
11. The verse does not specify the physical composition, distance, mass, size, temperature, luminosity, or orbital properties of the lights or stars.
12. “Greater” and “lesser” are the comparative terms used in this verse. The verse does not specify a measurement scale for that comparison.
13. The verse uses “rule” for the relationship of the greater light to day and the lesser light to night. It does not define a political, conscious, or independent authority for the lights.
14. Verse 16 does not itself say that God set the lights in the firmament; that statement appears in verse 17.
15. Verse 16 does not itself repeat the phrase “to give light upon the earth”; that phrase appears in verses 15 and 17.
16. Verse 16 does not itself contain the assessment “good”; that is recorded in verse 18.
17. Verse 16 does not specify how the stars relate individually to the day-and-night rule assigned to the two great lights.
18. The verse does not say that the stars are lesser lights, nor does it assign them a separate ruling function here.
19. The verse does not explain how the lights were made or the physical process by which they perform their stated functions.
20. The verse does not say that the lights or stars made themselves, assigned their own purposes, or originated their authority.
21. The verse must be read with verses 14–18, while keeping the distinct statements and sequence of each verse intact.

The direct finding is that God made two great lights, distinguished them as greater and lesser, associated their rule with day and night respectively, and made the stars also. The verse attributes their making to God. It does not supply a modern physical description or detailed mechanism.
