# Study Record 0017 — Genesis 1:17 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:17 (KJV)**

> And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth,

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:17 establish when it says, “And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth”? How must the Witness distinguish God's making of the lights in verse 16 from His setting them in verse 17, while preserving the stated purpose?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:14 (KJV):
> And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:

Genesis 1:15 (KJV):
> And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth: and it was so.

Genesis 1:16 (KJV):
> And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also.

Genesis 1:17 (KJV):
> And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth,

Genesis 1:18 (KJV):
> And to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness: and God saw that it was good.

Genesis 1:19 (KJV):
> And the evening and the morning were the fourth day.

Verse 14 records God's command concerning lights and their purposes. Verse 15 continues the command, saying that they are to give light upon the earth, and concludes, “and it was so.” Verse 16 says God made two great lights, distinguishes the greater and lesser in relation to day and night, and adds that He made the stars also. Verse 17 then says God set “them” in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth. Verse 18 continues the description of their rule and division and records God's assessment that it was good. Verse 19 identifies the fourth day.

This record examines the statement in verse 17. The preceding and following verses supply necessary context, but their statements must remain distinct. Verse 16 says “made”; verse 17 says “set”. Verse 17 also repeats the purpose “to give light upon the earth”, already stated in verse 15. The verse does not explain a physical mechanism or specify a modern astronomical coordinate system.

## Direct observations

1. God is the subject of the action: “And God set”.
2. The object is expressed by the pronoun “them”.
3. The pronoun points back to the heavenly lights described in the immediately preceding context, especially the two great lights and the stars in verse 16.
4. The precise scope of “them” should be read from the sentence and its context; the verse itself does not separately enumerate the objects again.
5. The action in verse 17 is “set”, whereas the action in verse 16 is “made”.
6. The stated location is “in the firmament of the heaven”.
7. The stated purpose is “to give light upon the earth”.
8. The purpose repeats the wording of Genesis 1:15.
9. Verse 17 does not introduce a new speaker; it continues the account of God's actions.
10. Verse 17 does not repeat the command “Let there be lights”; it reports that God set them.
11. Verse 17 does not itself repeat the greater/lesser distinction or the day/night rule; those are stated in verse 16 and further described in verse 18.
12. Verse 17 does not itself say “and it was so”; that phrase concludes verse 15.
13. Verse 17 does not itself record the assessment “good”; that appears in verse 18.
14. The verse does not explain the physical process by which God set the lights.
15. The verse does not give distances, orbital paths, altitudes, coordinates, or other modern astronomical measurements.
16. The verse does not define “firmament” by reference to a modern physical model.
17. The verse does not state that the lights set themselves or chose their location.
18. The verse attributes the setting to God.
19. The verse's purpose clause links their setting in the stated location with giving light upon the earth.
20. The repeated purpose in verses 15 and 17 should be preserved; the record should not erase the repetition or assume without evidence that the two occurrences have different meanings.
21. The distinction between making and setting is explicit at the level of the verbs used, but the verse does not provide a technical explanation of the relationship between those actions.
22. The verse must be read as part of the sequence in Genesis 1:14–19 without collapsing command, affirmation, making, setting, function, and assessment into one statement.

The direct finding is that God set the lights referred to in the immediate context in the firmament of the heaven for the stated purpose of giving light upon the earth. The verse distinguishes this setting from the making recorded in verse 16. The precise physical mechanism and any modern spatial interpretation are not supplied by this verse.
