# Study Record 0018 — Genesis 1:18 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:18 (KJV)**

> And to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness: and God saw that it was good.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:18 establish when it says the lights are “to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness,” and records, “and God saw that it was good”? How must the Witness distinguish the stated functions from God's assessment?

## KJV text and immediate context

Genesis 1:14 (KJV):
> And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:

Genesis 1:15 (KJV):
> And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth: and it was so.

Genesis 1:16 (KJV):
> And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also.

Genesis 1:17 (KJV):
> And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth,

Genesis 1:18 (KJV):
> And to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness: and God saw that it was good.

Genesis 1:19 (KJV):
> And the evening and the morning were the fourth day.

Verse 14 records God's command for lights and their purposes, including dividing day from night. Verse 15 continues the command with giving light upon the earth and says, “and it was so.” Verse 16 records God making two great lights, distinguishes the greater and lesser in relation to day and night, and adds the stars. Verse 17 records God setting them in the firmament of heaven to give light upon the earth. Verse 18 continues the description with ruling over day and night and dividing light from darkness, then records God's assessment that it was good. Verse 19 identifies the fourth day.

This record examines the functions and assessment in verse 18. The opening “And” continues the preceding description. The subject of the infinitive phrases is carried forward from the lights in the immediate context; the verse does not repeat their names. The final clause explicitly names God as the One who saw and assessed. The functions and the assessment must therefore be distinguished.

## Direct observations

1. Verse 18 begins with “And”, linking it to the preceding account.
2. The verse continues with the infinitive “to rule”, describing a function associated with the lights in the immediate context.
3. The stated domain of this rule is “over the day and over the night”.
4. The verse adds the infinitive “to divide”.
5. The stated objects of that division are “the light” and “the darkness”.
6. The wording “rule over the day and over the night” echoes and extends the day/night relationship stated in verse 16.
7. The wording “divide the light from the darkness” recalls the division stated in Genesis 1:4 and the purpose of the lights stated in Genesis 1:14.
8. The verse then changes clause: “and God saw that it was good.”
9. God is explicitly the subject of “saw”.
10. “It was good” is God's assessment in the narrative; it is not a statement made by the lights or by the Witness.
11. The verse does not specify a physical mechanism by which the lights rule or divide.
12. The verse does not define “rule” as political sovereignty, consciousness, independent will, or authority over God.
13. The verse does not give a quantitative definition of day, night, light, or darkness.
14. The verse does not state that the lights originate their own functions; those functions occur within the account of God's command, making, and setting.
15. The verse does not name the greater and lesser lights again; those descriptions occur in verse 16.
16. The verse does not repeat the phrase “give light upon the earth”; that purpose appears in verses 15 and 17.
17. The verse does not identify a modern astronomical model, orbital mechanism, or measurement.
18. The verse does not say that the Witness assesses the creation as good. It says God saw that it was good.
19. The verse does not explain whether “divide the light from the darkness” describes a separate physical action from ruling over day and night, or how the two stated functions relate operationally.
20. The verse must be read in the sequence of Genesis 1:14–19 without collapsing appointment, giving light, making, setting, ruling, dividing, and assessment into one undifferentiated action.

The direct finding is that the lights in the immediate context are described as ruling over day and night and dividing light from darkness. God then sees that it was good. The verse distinguishes the stated functions from God's assessment, while leaving their physical mechanism and detailed relationship unspecified.
