# Study Record 0021 — Genesis 1:21 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:21 (KJV)**

> And God created great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which the waters brought forth abundantly, after their kind, and every winged fowl after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:21 establish when it records God's creation of the great whales, every living creature that moveth, and every winged fowl? How does this verse distinguish God's creative act, the waters' bringing forth, the creatures after their kind, and God's assessment that it was good?

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 1:20 (KJV):**

> And God said, Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life, and fowl that may fly above the earth in the open firmament of heaven.

**Genesis 1:21 (KJV):**

> And God created great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which the waters brought forth abundantly, after their kind, and every winged fowl after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

**Genesis 1:22 (KJV):**

> And God blessed them, saying, Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let fowl multiply in the earth.

**Genesis 1:23 (KJV):**

> And the evening and the morning were the fifth day.

The immediate sequence is command (verse 20), creation and reported bringing forth (verse 21), blessing and instruction to multiply (verse 22), and fifth-day designation (verse 23). These statements must remain distinct.

## Direct observations

1. The verse begins, “And God created”; God is explicitly identified as Creator.
2. “Great whales” are named specifically.
3. “Every living creature that moveth” is also included.
4. The clause “which the waters brought forth abundantly” appears in the description of the moving living creatures.
5. The phrase “after their kind” is applied in the description of the creatures.
6. “Every winged fowl” is separately included in the creation statement.
7. The winged fowl are described as being “after his kind”.
8. The verse ends with “and God saw that it was good”.
9. The governing creative declaration is “God created”.
10. The waters are described as having brought forth abundantly; the verse does not say that the waters created the creatures.
11. The verse does not remove the waters' participation from the account.
12. The verse does not explain a biological mechanism by which the creatures came forth.
13. The verse does not define “after their kind” using modern biological taxonomy.
14. The verse does not state that “kind” is identical to the modern scientific category of species.
15. The verse does not specify the limits of variation within each kind.
16. The verse does not give a scientific account of the origin or diversification of biological life.
17. The verse includes aquatic creatures and winged fowl under God's creative act, while its wording describes the waters' bringing forth in connection with the moving living creatures.
18. The verse records God's assessment that what He created was good.
19. Verse 22 subsequently records God's blessing and command concerning fruitfulness and multiplication.
20. The direct finding is that Genesis 1:21 identifies God as Creator, reports the waters' abundant bringing forth of living moving creatures, includes winged fowl after his kind, and records God's assessment that it was good.

### Grammatical caution

The sentence has one governing creative declaration: “And God created.” Its objects include great whales, every living creature that moveth, and every winged fowl. The relative clause “which the waters brought forth abundantly” is part of the description of the moving living creatures. The verse does not say that the waters created them.

The wording must be preserved without claiming more grammatical precision than the sentence supplies. In particular, the exact scope of the relative clause and the detailed relationship between the waters' bringing forth and God's creative act should not be made to carry an unstated biological mechanism.
