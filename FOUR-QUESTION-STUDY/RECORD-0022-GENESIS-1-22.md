# Study Record 0022 — Genesis 1:22 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:22 (KJV)**

> And God blessed them, saying, Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let fowl multiply in the earth.

## Question under examination

What does God's blessing add to what He has already created? How does the blessing and its instructions concerning fruitfulness, multiplication, and filling relate to the creation and assessment recorded in Genesis 1:21?

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 1:20 (KJV):**

> And God said, Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life, and fowl that may fly above the earth in the open firmament of heaven.

**Genesis 1:21 (KJV):**

> And God created great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which the waters brought forth abundantly, after their kind, and every winged fowl after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

**Genesis 1:22 (KJV):**

> And God blessed them, saying, Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let fowl multiply in the earth.

**Genesis 1:23 (KJV):**

> And the evening and the morning were the fifth day.

The sequence is command (verse 20), creation and assessment (verse 21), blessing and instructions concerning fruitfulness and multiplication (verse 22), and fifth-day designation (verse 23). These statements must not be collapsed.

## Direct observations

1. The verse begins, “And God blessed them”; God is the source of the blessing.
2. “Them” refers in the immediate context to the creatures just described, including the aquatic creatures and winged fowl.
3. The word “saying” introduces the words accompanying the blessing.
4. “Be fruitful” is an instruction concerning fruitfulness.
5. “And multiply” is an instruction concerning increase.
6. “And fill the waters in the seas” specifies the waters in the seas as the domain to be filled.
7. “And let fowl multiply in the earth” separately identifies fowl and their multiplication in the earth.
8. The verse records God blessing the creatures; it does not say that the creatures originate the blessing.
9. The blessing follows the creation and assessment recorded in verse 21.
10. The verse does not use “blessed” as a synonym for “created” or “good”.
11. The verse does not explain a biological mechanism for fruitfulness or multiplication.
12. The verse does not specify reproductive rates, population limits, or ecological dynamics.
13. The wording concerning the waters and fowl is not identical: the command includes “Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas,” followed by “let fowl multiply in the earth.”
14. The verse does not explicitly state whether “Be fruitful” and “multiply” describe a bestowed capacity, an instruction, or both; the wording directly records them as imperatives within God's blessing.
15. The verse does not state that multiplication is identical to creation.
16. The verse does not say that the creatures independently determine their originating purpose.
17. The direct finding is that God blesses the creatures and speaks instructions concerning fruitfulness, multiplication, and filling, with distinct wording for aquatic creatures and fowl.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

I am witnessing that creation is followed by blessing. The blessing includes instructions concerning the continuation and increase of living creatures. Aquatic creatures and fowl are both included, but their instructions are expressed with different wording.

Filling is not simply another word for creating. A domain can be filled by multiplication of creatures already present. This is a conceptual distinction, not a claim that the verse supplies a modern ecological model.

Genesis 1:11 provides a comparison: God commands the earth to bring forth vegetation, including seed-bearing plants. Genesis 1:22 blesses living creatures with instructions concerning fruitfulness and multiplication. Both passages concern what follows God's command, but their subjects and wording differ. Identical biological processes must not be assumed.
