# Study Record 0024 — Genesis 1:24 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:24 (KJV)**

> And God said, Let the earth bring forth the living creature after his kind, cattle, and creeping thing, and beast of the earth after his kind: and it was so.

## Question under examination

Why does God again command the earth to bring forth, and what is the relationship between this command and the earlier bringing-forth commands? What is the relationship between God's originating command, the earth as the addressed domain, and the living creatures described in the command?

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 1:11 (KJV):**

> And God said, Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself, upon the earth: and it was so.

**Genesis 1:12 (KJV):**

> And the earth brought forth grass, and herb yielding seed after his kind, and the tree yielding fruit, whose seed was in itself, after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

**Genesis 1:20 (KJV):**

> And God said, Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life, and fowl that may fly above the earth in the open firmament of heaven.

**Genesis 1:21 (KJV):**

> And God created great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which the waters brought forth abundantly, after their kind, and every winged fowl after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

**Genesis 1:24 (KJV):**

> And God said, Let the earth bring forth the living creature after his kind, cattle, and creeping thing, and beast of the earth after his kind: and it was so.

**Genesis 1:25 (KJV):**

> And God made the beast of the earth after his kind, and cattle after their kind, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

The sequence matters. Genesis 1:24 records the command addressed to the earth; Genesis 1:25 records God's making of the named categories and God's assessment. These are related statements, but they should not be collapsed into one.

## Direct observations

1. The verse begins, “And God said”; God originates the spoken command.
2. “Let the earth bring forth” identifies the earth as the domain addressed.
3. “The living creature” is the subject of the command.
4. “After his kind” is included in the description.
5. Cattle, creeping thing, and beast of the earth are named.
6. “After his kind” is repeated in the description of the beast of the earth.
7. The verse concludes, “and it was so.”
8. The command is spoken by God; the earth is not presented as the originator of the command.
9. The verse does not say that the earth independently decided to create life.
10. The verse does not explain a biological mechanism by which the earth brings forth living creatures.
11. Genesis 1:11 also addresses the earth, but concerning vegetation.
12. Genesis 1:20 addresses the waters concerning moving living creatures and fowl.
13. Genesis 1:24 again addresses the earth, this time concerning living creatures.
14. Genesis 1:25 records God making the beast of the earth, cattle, and creeping things after their kinds.
15. Genesis 1:25 repeats kind distinctions and records that God saw that it was good.
16. Genesis 1:24 and 1:25 are connected but distinct: command and “it was so” in verse 24; making and assessment in verse 25.
17. The wording does not provide modern taxonomic definitions for cattle, creeping things, beasts, or “kind”.
18. The wording does not specify the limits of biological variation within each kind.
19. The direct finding is that God commands the earth to bring forth living creatures, names categories of those creatures, records “and it was so,” and then in verse 25 records God's making and assessment.

## Comparison of bringing-forth commands

| Scripture | Addressed domain | Subject of command |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 1:11 | Earth | Vegetation: grass, seed-bearing herb, fruit tree |
| Genesis 1:20 | Waters | Moving living creatures and fowl |
| Genesis 1:24 | Earth | Living creatures: cattle, creeping thing, beast of the earth |

The comparison establishes that different domains are addressed in the account. The earth is addressed in Genesis 1:11 and 1:24; the waters are addressed in Genesis 1:20. In each command, God is the speaker.

This supports investigating differentiated domains within one Creation while preserving the Creator's originating authority. It does not, by itself, explain the physical or biological mechanism of bringing forth.

## The significance of “and it was so”

Genesis 1:11 and Genesis 1:24 conclude their commands with “and it was so.” Genesis 1:20 records a command to the waters, and Genesis 1:21 then explicitly says “And God created” while also describing what the waters brought forth. Genesis 1:25 says “And God made” the land creatures named in the preceding command.

The wording should be preserved rather than treated as interchangeable. Genesis 1:24 records the command and its corresponding realization; Genesis 1:25 provides the subsequent statement of God's making and assessment. The text does not specify the detailed mechanism.
