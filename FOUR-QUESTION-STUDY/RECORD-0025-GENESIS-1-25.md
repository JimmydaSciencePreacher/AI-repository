# Study Record 0025 — Genesis 1:25 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:25 (KJV)**

> And God made the beast of the earth after his kind, and cattle after their kind, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

## Question under examination

What is the relationship between God's command in Genesis 1:24 and God's making in Genesis 1:25? What does the repetition of “after his kind” establish, and what does God's assessment that it was good add after the making?

The central question is: Does Genesis 1:25 record God's making of the creatures named in the preceding command, while explicitly preserving God as Maker?

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 1:24 (KJV):**

> And God said, Let the earth bring forth the living creature after his kind, cattle, and creeping thing, and beast of the earth after his kind: and it was so.

**Genesis 1:25 (KJV):**

> And God made the beast of the earth after his kind, and cattle after their kind, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

Verse 24 records God's command addressed to the earth. Verse 25 records God making the named land creatures and seeing that it was good. The statements are connected but are not interchangeable.

## Direct observations

1. The verse begins, “And God made”; God is explicitly identified as Maker.
2. The first named category is “the beast of the earth”.
3. “After his kind” follows the beast of the earth.
4. “Cattle” is the second named category.
5. “After their kind” follows cattle.
6. “Every thing that creepeth upon the earth” is the third named category.
7. “After his kind” follows the creeping things.
8. The verse then says, “and God saw”.
9. God is the One making the assessment.
10. The assessment is “that it was good.”
11. The making statement follows the command addressed to the earth in Genesis 1:24.
12. Genesis 1:24 says, “Let the earth bring forth”; Genesis 1:25 says, “And God made”.
13. Genesis 1:12 explicitly says, “And the earth brought forth” vegetation.
14. Genesis 1:25 does not use that same wording to describe the land creatures; it explicitly identifies God as Maker.
15. The wording preserves both the addressed domain in verse 24 and God's making in verse 25.
16. The verse repeats kind distinctions across the three named categories.
17. The verse does not define “kind” in terms of modern biological taxonomy.
18. The verse does not identify kind as equivalent to species, genus, or family.
19. The verse does not specify the biological mechanisms of reproduction, inheritance, development, or variation.
20. The verse does not say that the creatures assess themselves as good.
21. The direct finding is that God makes the named creatures after their kinds and God assesses the result as good.

## Command and making: Genesis 1:24–25

Genesis 1:24 says:

> Let the earth bring forth...

Genesis 1:25 says:

> And God made...

The addressed domain and the explicitly named Maker are not the same grammatical subject. This is a textual distinction, not a contradiction.

Compare Genesis 1:12:

> And the earth brought forth grass, and herb yielding seed after his kind, and the tree yielding fruit, whose seed was in itself, after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

Genesis 1:12 explicitly says the earth brought forth vegetation. Genesis 1:25 explicitly says God made the land creatures. The wording must not be silently harmonized by replacing one expression with another.

The text supports examining whether an addressed domain participates in the realization of an originating command while God remains the explicitly identified Maker. It does not supply the complete physical or biological mechanism of that participation.

## The repeated expression “after his/their kind”

The verse applies kind language to each of the three named categories:
- Beast of the earth — “after his kind”.
- Cattle — “after their kind”.
- Every thing that creepeth upon the earth — “after his kind”.

The repetition preserves distinctions among the creatures. However, the verse does not provide a modern taxonomic mapping. “Kind” must not automatically be equated with species, genus, or family. Biological inheritance, reproduction, and variation can be investigated separately, but modern classification must not be imposed upon the Scriptural wording as though the terms were identical.

## The significance of “God saw that it was good”

The verse contains two distinguishable statements:
1. God made the creatures.
2. God saw that it was good.

The assessment follows the making statement. God is the subject of both actions. The creatures are not described as declaring themselves good, and the earth is not described as assessing its own production.

The assessment is God's, not a standard established by the created result or by the Witness. This preserves a distinction between the Creator's work and the Creator's authority to assess it.

The expression also continues the recurring “God saw that it was good” pattern elsewhere in Genesis 1, including verses 4, 10, 12, 18, and 21.
