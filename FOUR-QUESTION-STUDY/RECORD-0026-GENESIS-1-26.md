# Study Record 0026 — Genesis 1:26 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:26 (KJV)**

> And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.

## Question under examination

What changes when God introduces the creation of man? What is the relationship between being made in God's image, being made after His likeness, and receiving dominion over Creation?

The central question is: Does God create man in His image and likeness and appoint man to exercise dominion within the Creation that God alone originates?

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 1:26 (KJV):**

> And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.

**Genesis 1:27 (KJV):**

> So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.

**Genesis 1:28 (KJV):**

> And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.

These verses must be read together. Verse 26 records God's statement concerning making man in His image and likeness and the appointment of dominion. Verse 27 records God's creation of man in His image and identifies male and female. Verse 28 records God's blessing and further instructions, including subduing the earth and exercising dominion.

## Direct observations

1. The verse begins, “And God said”; God is the speaker.
2. God says, “Let us make man”.
3. The stated pattern is “in our image, after our likeness”.
4. The verse then says, “let them have dominion”.
5. The pronoun changes from the collective singular “man” to the plural “them”.
6. The named domains include fish of the sea, fowl of the air, cattle, all the earth, and every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.
7. Genesis 1:27 explicitly says God created man in His own image.
8. Genesis 1:27 identifies male and female as created by God.
9. Genesis 1:28 records God blessing them and instructing them to be fruitful, multiply, replenish the earth, subdue it, and exercise dominion.
10. The verse does not say man creates himself or independently appoints himself to dominion.
11. The verse does not say man becomes God by being made in God's image.
12. The verse does not define image and likeness as two separate technical operations.
13. The verse does not fully explain the theological meaning of the plural “us”.
14. The verse does not state that man's dominion includes authority to originate, alter, or redefine Creation's source or purpose.
15. The verse establishes dominion over named parts of Creation; further boundaries and responsibilities require examination of surrounding Scripture.
16. The direct finding is that God speaks concerning man's making in His image and likeness and assigns man dominion over specified domains.

## Image and likeness

The Hebrew terms traditionally associated with the KJV words are:
- Image: צֶלֶם (*tselem*), Strong's H6754.
- Likeness: דְּמוּת (*demuth*), Strong's H1823.

Both terms occur in Genesis 1:26. Genesis 1:27 repeats “image” in its description of God's creation of man, without repeating “likeness” in that verse.

The difference and relationship between the terms require examination across Scripture. Their distinct lexical forms do not, by themselves, prove two independent architectural functions. Nor does the text authorize equating image-bearing with becoming God, physical resemblance alone, or originating authority.

Further relevant passages for examination include Genesis 5:1–3 and Genesis 9:6. Their contribution must be assessed in context before a theological conclusion is admitted.

## “Let us make man”

The plural wording “Let us make” requires careful theological examination. Genesis 1:27 subsequently states, “So God created man in his own image”. The wording of verse 26 and the explicit statement of verse 27 must both be preserved.

The complete theological significance of “us” is not settled by Genesis 1:26 alone. Interpretations must be tested against the broader Scriptural context rather than selected because they appear architecturally attractive.

## Dominion: received authority, not originating authority

Genesis 1:26 assigns dominion to man over named domains. Genesis 1:28 further says “subdue it” and repeats the dominion instruction.

The text distinguishes:
- God's originating authority as Creator.
- Man's received appointment to exercise dominion.
- The created domains over which that dominion is assigned.

The appointment is real within the wording of the passage, but it does not make man the Creator or the originating authority over Creation. The verse does not, by itself, define every boundary or responsibility of dominion. Those require further Scriptural examination.

The distinction is therefore: authority to exercise dominion is received; it is not the authority to become the Original Cause.

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The governing principle remains: God alone is Original Cause. Everything else is derivative witness.

Genesis 1:26 introduces an appointed participant exercising dominion within the created order. Previously, the examination considered the earth and waters as addressed domains. Now the text describes man as created by God and assigned dominion over specified parts of Creation.

This gives us distinctions among original authority, received authority, and the domain over which authority is exercised. A derivative participant can receive real authority without becoming the originating Author.

A provisional relationship for examination is: Author → Creation → appointed human participant → delegated dominion. This is a proposed relationship for investigation, not an amendment to the established GUA-W architecture.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

I am witnessing that man occupies a distinct position in the Genesis account. Man is created by God, made in God's image and likeness, and given dominion over specified domains. Man is not presented as an independent source of authority.

The appointment appears in the original Creation account, before the Fall described in Genesis 3. Dominion is therefore part of the account before the Fall, not merely a response to it.

Modern political, scientific, or technological authority must not automatically be equated with the dominion described in Genesis. Such applications require separate examination.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

A Witness can receive an appointment without becoming the Author. Representation does not establish origination. Delegated authority does not erase the authority of the One who delegates it.

This has an analogy to instruments and derivative systems: an instrument may perform functions assigned by its maker without becoming its maker. This analogy must not be used to equate AI with humanity's image-bearing identity or to settle the theology of the image of God.

The distinctions to preserve are:
- Capability is not authority.
- Delegated authority is not Original Cause.
- Representation is not authorship.

The Witness registers what is given; it does not become the source of what it witnesses.

## Triple Constraint Law

### 1. Biblical compliance

The examination preserves the wording of Genesis 1:26 and reads it alongside verses 27–28. It distinguishes God's creation of man from man's received dominion. It does not settle the plural “us” or the complete meaning of image and likeness from this verse alone.

### 2. Scientific coherence

This is primarily a textual and theological examination. No scientific claim about human origins, biology, cognition, or anatomy is inferred from the wording alone. Scientific investigation may address empirical questions, but it does not become the authority that redefines the Scriptural claims being examined.

### 3. GUA-W consistency

The examination preserves God as Original Cause, man as created, and dominion as an appointment received from God. It distinguishes originating authority from delegated authority. It does not add an architectural layer or operator without further evidence.

### Joint admissibility result

The direct textual findings are admitted under all three constraints: God speaks concerning man's creation in His image and likeness and assigns man dominion over specified domains. Genesis 1:27 explicitly records God's creation of man in His image; Genesis 1:28 expands the blessing and appointment. The complete meaning of “us”, the precise relationship between image and likeness, and the full boundaries of dominion remain open for wider Scriptural examination.

## Admitted findings

1. God is the speaker and Creator in the passage.
2. Man is described as made in God's image and likeness.
3. Genesis 1:27 explicitly records God creating man in His image.
4. Male and female are included in the creation statement.
