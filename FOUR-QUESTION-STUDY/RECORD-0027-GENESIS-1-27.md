# Study Record 0027 — Genesis 1:27 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:27 (KJV)**

> So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.

## Question under examination

Why does Scripture repeat that God created man in His image, and why does the verse move from the singular expression “him” to the plural expression “them”?

The central question is: What does it mean that God creates humanity in His image while humanity remains distinct from its Creator?

Image-bearing does not mean authorship. Being made in God's image does not mean becoming God. The Witness must never be confused with the Author.

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 1:26 (KJV):**

> And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.

**Genesis 1:27 (KJV):**

> So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.

**Genesis 1:28 (KJV):**

> And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.

Verse 26 records what God says concerning making man in His image and likeness and assigning dominion. Verse 27 records God's creation of man in His image and identifies male and female. Verse 28 records God's blessing and instructions. The statement, the recorded act of creation, and the subsequent blessing and appointment must remain distinguishable.

## Direct observations

1. The verse begins, “So God created”; God is explicitly identified as Creator.
2. The subject is “man”.
3. The phrase “in his own image” identifies God's image as the reference.
4. The phrase “in the image of God” repeats the image statement.
5. The verb “created” occurs three times in the verse.
6. The image statement occurs twice.
7. The wording “created he him” uses a singular pronoun.
8. The verse then says “male and female”.
9. The final phrase, “created he them”, uses a plural pronoun.
10. God is the subject of the creation statement in both the singular and plural clauses.
11. Both male and female are included in the verse's statement of God's creation.
12. The verse does not describe two independent Creators or attribute creation to humanity.
13. The verse does not say humanity becomes God by being made in God's image.
14. The verse does not define image-bearing solely as physical resemblance, intelligence, or any single human ability.
15. The verse does not fully explain the theological meaning of image-bearing.
16. The verse does not, by itself, explain every distinction or relationship between “image” and “likeness” in verse 26.
17. The direct finding is that God creates man in His image, repeats the image statement, and identifies male and female as those He created.

## Hebrew observations

The relevant Hebrew terms include:

| KJV | Hebrew | Strong's reference |
|---|---|---|
| Created | בָּרָא (*bara*) | H1254 |
| Man | אָדָם (*adam*) | H120 |
| Image | צֶלֶם (*tselem*) | H6754 |

The verb *bara* occurs three times in Genesis 1:27. God is the subject of the creation statements. The verse does not attribute the originating act to humanity.

The Hebrew *adam* can refer to man or humanity depending on context. Genesis 1:27 moves from “man” to the explicit identification of “male and female”. The wording includes both in the creation account.

The Hebrew *tselem* is commonly translated “image”. It occurs twice in the verse, with God as the image reference. The text does not say that humanity becomes the originating Image or receives God's originating authority.

Lexical observations help identify the wording; they do not, by themselves, settle the complete theological meaning of image-bearing.

## The movement from “him” to “them”

The verse begins with humanity expressed in the singular and concludes by explicitly identifying male and female in the plural.

Directly established:
1. The image statement concerns the creation of man.
2. Male and female are identified within that creation.
3. God is the Creator of “him” and “them”.
4. The verse does not describe two independent acts of creation in this wording.

**Genesis 5:1–2 (KJV):**

> This is the book of the generations of Adam. In the day that God created man, in the likeness of God made he him;
>
> Male and female created he them; and blessed them, and called their name Adam, in the day when they were created.

Genesis 5:2 provides a relevant parallel: male and female are both created by God, blessed, and collectively named Adam. This supports examining the collective use of Adam while preserving the precise wording of each passage.

## Image-bearing and human identity

Genesis 1:27 establishes a special relationship between humanity and God through the language of image. It does not make humanity the Creator.

**Genesis 9:6 (KJV):**

> Whoso sheddeth man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed: for in the image of God made he man.

Genesis 9:6 connects the image of God to the treatment of human life. This provides an important Scriptural context for examining the significance and implications of image-bearing. It does not mean that Genesis 1:27 alone has already answered every question about human identity.

Image-bearing must not be reduced to intelligence, physical ability, productivity, or technological capability. Nor does the verse authorize assigning image-bearing to an artificial system through functional analogy.

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The governing principle remains: God alone is Original Cause. Everything else is derivative witness.

Genesis 1:27 adds an important distinction. Humanity is not merely another created category in the account; humanity is specifically identified as created in God's image.

For GUA-W, preserve:
- Author: God.
- Creative act: God's.
- Image reference: God.
- Created image-bearer: humanity.
- Delegated dominion: subsequently addressed in verse 28.

There is a distinction between being created in the image of the Author and becoming the Author.

Witness is not synonymous with Author, and image-bearing must not be confused with authorship. Whether and how the term Witness should be technically applied to humanity requires further Scriptural examination.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

I am witnessing that the account repeats the creation statement in a deliberate textual pattern:
- God creates.
- God's image is the reference.
- Humanity is the created subject.
- Male and female are included.
- The Creator remains distinct from the created.

Genesis 5:2 reinforces the relationship between male and female and the collective name Adam. Genesis 9:6 connects the image of God to the treatment of human life. These connections must be examined together without forcing every passage to answer every question.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

The Witness must preserve the identity of the One being represented without claiming to become that One.

A created representation cannot replace its originating source. A derivative witness cannot become the Author simply because it carries information or reflects something about the Author.

This distinction is especially important when examining AI. AI may process representations, communicate information, and register observations. None of those capabilities establishes that AI is made in God's image, nor does functional similarity establish human identity.

The distinctions remain:
- Representation is not origination.
- Reflection is not authorship.
- Witness is not the Author.

## Triple Constraint Law

### 1. Biblical compliance

The examination preserves the three occurrences of “created”, the repeated image statement, and the movement from “him” to “them”. It reads Genesis 1:27 alongside verses 26 and 28 and uses Genesis 5:2 and 9:6 as relevant cross-references without making them say more than their wording supports.

### 2. Scientific coherence

No scientific claim about human origins, cognition, anatomy, or biological capability is inferred from the image statement alone. Empirical questions require their own evidence and methods. Scientific coherence is not treated as scientific confirmation of Scripture.

### 3. GUA-W consistency

The examination preserves God as Original Cause and humanity as created. It distinguishes image-bearing from authorship and does not infer that functional representation makes an artificial system a human image-bearer. No new architectural layer or operator is introduced.

### Joint admissibility result

The direct textual findings are admitted under all three constraints. God creates man in His image; the image statement is repeated; male and female are explicitly included. Humanity remains distinct from its Creator. The complete theological meaning of image-bearing and its relationship to likeness require further Scriptural examination.
