# Study Record 0030 — Genesis 1:30 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:30 (KJV)**

> And to every beast of the earth, and to every fowl of the air, and to every thing that creepeth upon the earth, wherein there is life, I have given every green herb for meat: and it was so.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:30 establish when God gives every green herb for food to the beasts of the earth, the fowl of the air, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth wherein there is life? How does this provision relate to the provision given to humanity in verse 29?

The central question is: What does the verse establish about the scope, giver, recipients, and stated use of this provision, while preserving the distinction between the provision given to humanity and the provision given to the living creatures?

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 1:28 (KJV):**

> And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.

**Genesis 1:29 (KJV):**

> And God said, Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree, in the which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat.

**Genesis 1:30 (KJV):**

> And to every beast of the earth, and to every fowl of the air, and to every thing that creepeth upon the earth, wherein there is life, I have given every green herb for meat: and it was so.

**Genesis 1:31 (KJV):**

> And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

The immediate sequence distinguishes humanity's appointment (verse 28), provision for humanity (verse 29), provision for the named living creatures (verse 30), and God's assessment of everything He had made (verse 31). These statements are related in the account but are not interchangeable.

Verse 29 identifies seed-bearing vegetation and fruit-bearing trees as provision for humanity. Verse 30 identifies every green herb as provision for the named animal groups. The text gives these provisions in separate statements with different recipients and wording.

## Direct observations

1. Verse 30 continues the account with “And”.
2. The verse identifies three recipient groups: every beast of the earth, every fowl of the air, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth.
3. The phrase “wherein there is life” occurs in connection with the creeping things in the sentence.
4. God is identified as the giver by the words “I have given”.
5. The provision is “every green herb”.
6. The stated use is “for meat”.
7. The verse ends with “and it was so”.
8. Verse 29 addresses humanity with “I have given you”.
9. Verse 29 names every herb bearing seed and every tree whose fruit yields seed as food for humanity.
10. Verse 30 names every green herb as food for the animal groups specified in that verse.
11. Both verses identify God as the giver.
12. The recipients named in verses 29 and 30 are distinguishable.
13. The provisions are described with different wording: verse 29 identifies seed-bearing herbs and fruit-bearing trees; verse 30 identifies every green herb.
14. Verse 30 does not repeat the blessing and dominion instructions of verse 28.
15. Verse 30 does not describe the process by which the animals obtain, consume, or digest the green herb.
16. Verse 30 does not provide a modern biological classification of the named groups.
17. The verse does not state that the animal recipients independently originate their provision.
18. “And it was so” affirms the stated provision in the narrative; the verse does not specify a separate physical mechanism by that phrase.
19. Verse 31 records God's assessment of everything He had made as “very good”.
20. The direct finding is that God gives every green herb for food to the named animal groups, and the account affirms, “and it was so”.

The direct observations establish the giver, recipients, provision, and stated use. They do not establish a complete account of animal diets, ecological relationships, or biological mechanisms.
