# Study Record 0031 — Genesis 1:31 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 1:31 (KJV)**

> And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:31 establish when God sees everything that He has made and declares it “very good”? What is included in “every thing that he had made,” how does this assessment relate to the preceding assessments, and what does the closing evening-and-morning formula establish about the sixth day?

The central question is: What does the collective assessment establish about the created work described in Genesis 1, while preserving the distinction between God's seeing, God's assessment, and the designation of the sixth day?

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 1:25 (KJV):**

> And God made the beast of the earth after his kind, and cattle after their kind, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

**Genesis 1:27 (KJV):**

> So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.

**Genesis 1:28 (KJV):**

> And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.

**Genesis 1:29–30 (KJV):** These verses record God's provision of food for humanity and for the animal groups named.

**Genesis 1:31 (KJV):**

> And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

**Genesis 2:1 (KJV):**

> Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them.

**Genesis 2:2 (KJV):**

> And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.

The sequence matters. Genesis 1:31 records God's assessment of everything He had made and closes with the designation of the sixth day. Genesis 2:1–2 then records the finishing of the heavens and earth and all their host, and God's ending of His work. The assessment in 1:31 and the completion statements in 2:1–2 must be read together without treating their wording as interchangeable.

## Direct observations

1. The verse begins with “And God saw”.
2. God is the subject of the seeing.
3. The object of the seeing is “every thing that he had made”.
4. The verse does not say that God saw only the last thing made.
5. The verse uses “every thing” to refer collectively to the work under consideration in the Creation account.
6. The expression “that he had made” identifies God as the maker of the things being assessed.
7. “And, behold” introduces the declaration that follows.
8. The assessment is “it was very good”.
9. The verse uses “very good”, whereas earlier assessments in Genesis 1 repeatedly use “good”.
10. The assessment is stated collectively in relation to everything God had made.
11. The verse does not enumerate every created thing again; it refers to the work already narrated.
12. The verse does not explain the complete meaning or every possible implication of “very good”.
13. The verse does not say that the created work had become its own author or source.
14. The verse next records, “And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.”
15. The evening-and-morning formula designates the sixth day in the recurring structure of Genesis 1.
16. The verse does not call the sixth day the seventh day.
17. The verse does not itself state that God rested on the sixth day.
18. Genesis 2:1–2 separately records that the heavens, earth, and all their host were finished and that God ended His work on the seventh day.
19. The direct finding is that God sees everything He has made, declares it very good, and the account identifies the evening and morning as the sixth day.

These observations distinguish the wording of the verse from subsequent explanations of the meaning of the assessment.

## The collective assessment: “every thing that he had made”

The wording is broader than the individual assessments recorded during the six days.

Earlier in Genesis 1, the account records particular assessments:
- Genesis 1:4 — God saw the light, that it was good.
- Genesis 1:10 — God saw that the separation and naming described there were good.
- Genesis 1:12 — God saw the vegetation was good.
- Genesis 1:18 — God saw that the lights' appointed functions and division were good.
- Genesis 1:21 — God saw the created living creatures were good.
- Genesis 1:25 — God saw the land creatures were good.

Genesis 1:31 now says:

> And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.

The difference in scope is important. The earlier assessments occur within particular parts of the account. Verse 31 presents a collective assessment of everything God had made.

The text does not say that the earlier things were defective and became good only in verse 31. Nor does it say that the phrase “very good” is merely a repetition with no difference in scope. The immediate wording supports a distinction between particular assessments and the collective assessment.

**Directly established:** God assesses everything He had made as very good.

**Not established by this verse alone:** A complete technical definition of “very good”, or a detailed explanation of every relationship among all created things.

## “God saw” and “it was very good”

The verse contains two distinguishable statements:

1. “God saw every thing that he had made.”
2. “And, behold, it was very good.”

God's seeing is the first stated action; the declaration of goodness follows. The verse does not identify these as two unrelated events, nor does it provide a technical account of the relationship between seeing and assessment.

The assessment belongs to God. The created work does not declare itself very good, and the Witness does not become the authority that establishes the assessment.

This distinction must be preserved:
- God is the Maker.
- God sees what He has made.
- God gives the assessment.
- The created work is what is assessed.

The verse does not transfer the authority of assessment from God to the created things or to a later observer.

## “Very good” compared with “good”

Genesis 1 uses “good” in several earlier assessments. Genesis 1:31 uses “very good” in the collective assessment.

The KJV wording therefore preserves a difference:
- Earlier assessments: “it was good”.
- Collective assessment: “it was very good”.

The text supports observing this difference in wording and scope. It does not, by itself, define “very” as a mathematical quantity, a scientific measurement, or a technical GUA-W operator.

The collective assessment must not be reduced to a numerical score or a scientific measurement that the text does not provide.

## The sixth day and the transition to Genesis 2

Genesis 1:31 closes:

> And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

This is the recurring day-designation formula also found at the close of the first five days. It identifies the sixth day in the sequence.

Genesis 2:1 then begins:

> Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them.

Genesis 2:2 records God ending His work and resting on the seventh day.

These statements must remain distinguishable:

| Passage | What it states |
|---|---|
| Genesis 1:31 | God sees everything He made, declares it very good, and the sixth day is designated. |
| Genesis 2:1 | The heavens, earth, and all their host are finished. |
| Genesis 2:2 | God ends His work and rests on the seventh day. |

The relationship among assessment, finishing, and rest is a legitimate subject for the next examination. But Genesis 1:31 must not be made to say that the seventh day has already occurred or that the rest described in Genesis 2:2 is the same statement as the assessment in Genesis 1:31.

## Triple Constraint Law

### 1. Biblical compliance

The examination preserves Genesis 1:31 in the KJV and reads it in the context of the earlier assessments and the following statements in Genesis 2:1–2. It distinguishes the collective assessment, the sixth-day designation, the finishing of the heavens and earth, and the seventh-day rest. It does not make the verse say more than its wording establishes.

### 2. Scientific coherence

No scientific measurement or empirical test of “very good” is inferred. The verse is making a Scriptural assessment, not presenting a modern scientific metric. Scientific descriptions of created systems, their interactions, or their condition would require separate evidence and must not be substituted for the theological wording of the passage.

Scientific coherence is not treated as scientific confirmation of Scripture.

### 3. GUA-W consistency

The examination preserves God as Original Cause, Maker, and the One who gives the assessment. The created work remains distinct from its Author. The Witness remains a recipient and reporter of what is given, not the authority that originates the work or establishes God's assessment.

No new GUA-W layer, operator, or technical sequence is introduced merely from the phrase “very good”.

### Joint admissibility result

The direct textual findings are admitted under all three constraints: God sees everything He has made, declares it very good, and the evening and morning designate the sixth day. The collective scope of the assessment is distinguishable from earlier particular assessments. Genesis 2:1–2 then gives distinct statements concerning the finishing of the heavens, earth, and all their host, and God's ending of His work and rest.

Claims that “very good” is a scientific measurement, that the sixth day is the seventh-day rest, or that this verse alone establishes a new GUA-W operator are not admitted.
