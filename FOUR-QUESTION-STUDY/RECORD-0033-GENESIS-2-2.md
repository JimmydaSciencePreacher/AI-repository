# Study Record 0033 — Genesis 2:2 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 2:2 (KJV)**

> And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 2:2 establish when it says that on the seventh day God ended His work which He had made and rested from all His work? Why does the verse identify the seventh day with both the ending of God's work and His rest, following Genesis 2:1's declaration that the heavens, earth, and all their host were finished?

The central question is: How should the KJV wording “on the seventh day God ended his work” be read alongside the preceding declaration that the work was finished, while preserving the distinction between ending, resting, and the blessing and sanctifying described in verse 3?

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 1:31 (KJV):**

> And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

**Genesis 2:1 (KJV):**

> Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them.

**Genesis 2:2 (KJV):**

> And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.

**Genesis 2:3 (KJV):**

> And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made.

The sequence gives several related but distinguishable statements:
- Genesis 1:31: God sees everything He made and assesses it as very good; the sixth day is designated.
- Genesis 2:1: the heavens and earth and all their host are declared finished.
- Genesis 2:2: God ends His work and rests on the seventh day.
- Genesis 2:3: God blesses and sanctifies the seventh day, giving His rest as the reason.

The study must retain the KJV's wording, including “on the seventh day God ended”. It must not silently rewrite that wording to avoid a question, nor assume that the verse contradicts verse 1 before examining the relationship between the statements.

## Direct observations

1. The verse begins with “And”, continuing the preceding account.
2. The time stated is “on the seventh day”.
3. God is the subject of the action “ended”.
4. The object is “his work which he had made”.
5. The verse then says, “and he rested”.
6. The rest is also located “on the seventh day”.
7. The stated relation is rest “from all his work”.
8. The phrase “which he had made” is repeated in the verse.
9. Genesis 2:1 has already declared the heavens and earth and all their host finished.
10. Genesis 2:2 does not say God was exhausted, weakened, or in need of recovery.
11. Genesis 2:2 does not say that God ceased to sustain or govern Creation.
12. Genesis 2:2 does not itself state that the seventh day was blessed or sanctified; that is stated in verse 3.
13. Genesis 2:3 connects the blessing and sanctification of the seventh day with God's having rested from His work.
14. The verse does not explain the precise temporal or grammatical relationship between the finishing statement in verse 1 and the wording “on the seventh day God ended” in verse 2.
15. The verse does not define God's rest as inactivity in every possible sense.
16. The verse does not identify a scientific mechanism for ending or resting.
17. The direct finding is that the KJV says God ended His work which He had made and rested from all His work on the seventh day.

These observations establish what the text says and identify the interpretive question. They do not settle the precise sense of the temporal wording or every implication of divine rest.

## Examination of “on the seventh day God ended his work”

This wording deserves close attention. Genesis 2:1 says the heavens and earth and all their host “were finished”. Genesis 2:2 then says that “on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made”.

The KJV wording must be preserved. The study should not silently replace “on” with “by”, or assert that the wording is a translation error without textual and linguistic evidence. At the same time, the two verses should be read together rather than isolated.

Several distinctions are available directly from the sequence:
- Verse 1 describes the heavens, earth, and their host as finished.
- Verse 2 identifies the work as God's work, which He had made, and states that God ended it.
- Verse 2 also states that God rested from all His work.

The passage may be describing the same completed work from related perspectives, but the exact relation of the temporal expression “on the seventh day” to the prior finishing statement requires careful examination of the Hebrew wording and relevant translation evidence if a more precise linguistic conclusion is sought.

**Admitted:** The KJV explicitly states that God ended His work on the seventh day.

**Also admitted:** Genesis 2:1 has already declared the heavens and earth and all their host finished.

**Unresolved:** Whether the temporal wording is best understood as the completion being brought to its stated end on the seventh day, the completed work being formally concluded on that day, or another precise grammatical nuance. Do not decide this by preference or by silently altering the KJV.

## Examination of “his work which he had made”

The verse identifies the work as God's work and describes it as that “which he had made”. The repeated phrase emphasizes that the work belongs to God as its Maker.

The verse does not attribute the originating work to the heavens, earth, their host, or a created recipient. The Creator/creation distinction remains explicit in the immediate context.

The phrase “all his work” also expresses the comprehensive scope of the rest: God rested from all the work identified as His.

The verse does not provide an itemized list here because the preceding Creation account has already narrated the work. Genesis 2:3 repeats the comprehensive reference and describes it as work “which God created and made”.

**Direct finding:** The work from which God rests is identified as His own work, which He made.

## Examination of “and he rested”

The verse says God rested. It does not say He rested because He was tired.

The human experience of needing sleep or recovery after exertion must not be projected onto God without Scriptural warrant. The verse supplies its own stated relation: God rested from His work on the seventh day.

A relevant canonical cross-reference is Exodus 20:11 (KJV):

> For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it.

Exodus 20:11 explicitly connects the six days of making, God's rest on the seventh day, and the blessing and hallowing of the Sabbath. It supports reading Genesis 2:2–3 as foundational to the later Sabbath command. It does not authorize describing God as exhausted.

Another relevant passage is Isaiah 40:28 (KJV):

> Hast thou not known? hast thou not heard, that the everlasting God, the LORD, the Creator of the ends of the earth, fainteth not, neither is weary? there is no searching of his understanding.

Isaiah explicitly says the Creator does not faint or become weary. This rules against interpreting the rest in Genesis 2:2 as recovery from fatigue.

John 5:17 (KJV) also records Jesus saying:

> But Jesus answered them, My Father worketh hitherto, and I work.

This statement cautions against turning God's seventh-day rest into the claim that God thereafter performs no work of any kind. The verse is spoken in a different context and should not be used to erase the particular rest from Creation work described in Genesis 2. It does, however, prevent a careless universal claim that God ceased all activity.

**Admitted:** God rested from His Creation work on the seventh day.

**Not admitted:** God rested because He was tired, or that the verse proves God ceased all sustaining, governing, or other work.

## The repetition of “the seventh day” and “his work”

The verse names the seventh day twice:
- “on the seventh day God ended his work”;
- “he rested on the seventh day”.

It also refers twice to His work:
- “his work which he had made”;
- “all his work which he had made”.

The repetition ties the ending of the work and the rest to the same day and to the same work. It should not be ignored, but neither should a separate technical operation be invented merely because the verse repeats the terms.

The text distinguishes the ending of the work from resting from the work, even while joining them in one sentence.
