# Study Record 0034 — Genesis 2:3 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 2:3 (KJV)**

> And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 2:3 establish when God blesses and sanctifies the seventh day, and why does the verse give as its reason that God rested from all His work which He created and made?

The central question is: What does God's blessing and sanctifying of the seventh day establish in the account, and how does the stated reason connect those actions to His rest from the work of Creation?

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 2:1 (KJV):**

> Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them.

**Genesis 2:2 (KJV):**

> And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.

**Genesis 2:3 (KJV):**

> And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made.

The sequence contains distinct statements:
- Verse 1 declares the heavens and earth and all their host finished.
- Verse 2 says God ended His work and rested on the seventh day.
- Verse 3 says God blessed the seventh day and sanctified it, giving His rest from all His created and made work as the reason.

The three verses are connected, but their words must not be treated as interchangeable. Verse 3 adds the blessing and sanctification of the seventh day to the account.

## Direct observations

1. God is the subject of the actions “blessed” and “sanctified”.
2. The object of both actions is “the seventh day”.
3. The verse gives a reason for these actions: “because that in it he had rested”.
4. The pronoun “it” refers to the seventh day in the sentence.
5. The rest is described as rest “from all his work”.
6. The work is described as work “which God created and made”.
7. The verse identifies God as the Creator and Maker of the work.
8. The verse does not say that the seventh day was created as a physical object.
9. The verse does not say that God created another material component of the heavens or earth on the seventh day.
10. The verse does not say that God blessed or sanctified the other six days in this same statement.
11. The verse does not itself command humanity to observe a weekly Sabbath.
12. The verse does not specify a human Sabbath schedule or give the later legal instructions concerning Sabbath observance.
13. The verse does not say God sanctified Himself; it says He sanctified the seventh day.
14. The verse does not describe God's rest as fatigue or recovery.
15. The verse does not state that God ceased all work of every kind.
16. The verse does not define every later application of the seventh day.
17. The direct finding is that God blessed and sanctified the seventh day because He had rested in it from all His work which He created and made.

These observations distinguish the actions and the reason given in the verse from later applications and theological conclusions.

## Examination of “God blessed the seventh day”

The verse explicitly says God blessed the seventh day.

The blessing is directed to the day. The verse does not state in this sentence every consequence or effect of that blessing. It is therefore appropriate to record that God blessed the day, but not to invent a detailed list of effects that the text does not provide.

The wording is significant because the preceding verses have described God's work and His rest. Verse 3 now records God's blessing of the seventh day in connection with that rest.

A later passage provides further context:

**Exodus 20:8–11 (KJV):**

> Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy.
>
> Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work:
>
> But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, thy manservant, nor thy maidservant, nor thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates:
>
> For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it.

Exodus explicitly connects the Creation account, God's seventh-day rest, and the Sabbath command given to Israel. It confirms that the blessing of the seventh day is relevant to the later Sabbath instruction.

However, Genesis 2:3 itself is not phrased as a command to Adam or to all humanity to observe a weekly Sabbath. The later command must be read in its own context.

**Direct finding:** God blesses the seventh day.

**Canonical context:** Exodus 20:8–11 connects the blessed seventh day with the Sabbath command.

**Not established by Genesis 2:3 alone:** Every detail of the later Sabbath law or its application to every person and covenant.

## Examination of “and sanctified it”

The verse says God sanctified the seventh day.

The object of sanctification is again the day. The statement distinguishes the seventh day as the day God sanctified.

The Hebrew verb commonly represented by “sanctified” is from the root *qadash* (קָדַשׁ), associated with holiness or setting apart as holy. This lexical observation helps identify the word's semantic range; it does not, by itself, supply every theological implication of the verse.

The verse itself establishes the act: God sanctified the seventh day. The following question is what this means in the immediate account and how later Scripture develops the Sabbath.

Exodus 20:11 uses “hallowed” in its explanation of the Sabbath. This provides a canonical connection to the sanctification described in Genesis.

The study must preserve the direction of the action:
- God is the One who sanctifies.
- The seventh day is what He sanctifies.

The day does not sanctify itself, and humanity is not identified here as the authority that makes the day holy.

**Direct finding:** God sanctifies the seventh day.

**Supported contextual understanding:** The day is set apart as holy by God's action, with later Sabbath instruction providing further context.

**Not established by this verse alone:** A complete account of all the obligations, restrictions, and applications associated with Sabbath observance.

## Examination of “because that in it he had rested”

The word “because” supplies the reason stated by the verse for God's blessing and sanctifying of the seventh day.

The reason is that God had rested in it from all His work.

This makes the relationship explicit:
- God rested on the seventh day.
- God blessed the seventh day.
- God sanctified the seventh day.
- The verse connects the blessing and sanctification to His rest.

The verse does not say that God needed rest because He was tired. Isaiah 40:28 (KJV) states:

> Hast thou not known? hast thou not heard, that the everlasting God, the LORD, the Creator of the ends of the earth, fainteth not, neither is weary? there is no searching of his understanding.

This canonical statement rules out attributing human exhaustion or weakness to the Creator as the explanation for Genesis 2:2–3.

The verse also does not establish that God ceased every form of activity. John 5:17 (KJV) records Jesus saying:

> But Jesus answered them, My Father worketh hitherto, and I work.

This passage cautions against a universal claim that God performs no work after the Creation account. It does not erase the particular rest from Creation work described in Genesis 2.

**Direct finding:** God's rest from His created and made work is the stated reason for blessing and sanctifying the seventh day.

**Not admitted:** Rest as recovery from fatigue, or rest as proof that God ceased all activity of every kind.

## Examination of “all his work which God created and made”

The verse describes the work comprehensively as “all his work” and explicitly identifies it as work “which God created and made”.

The wording preserves God as the Creator and Maker. The work belongs to Him; the created things do not become their own originating source.

The use of both “created” and “made” should be preserved. Genesis 2:3 does not provide a technical definition of two separate creation systems or authorize the construction of a new GUA-W operation from the two English verbs alone.

The phrase also ties the seventh-day rest to the Creation work described in the preceding account. It is not a statement that God rested from only one isolated task.

**Direct finding:** The rest is from all the work identified as God's created and made work.

**Unresolved:** Whether the two verbs in this verse carry a specific technical distinction beyond their wording and context. That question requires examination of the Hebrew terms and their use elsewhere, not an assumed architectural mapping.

## The sequence of Genesis 2:1–3

| Verse | Stated action or condition | Object / scope |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 2:1 | “were finished” | The heavens, earth, and all their host |
| Genesis 2:2 | God “ended his work” | His work which He had made |
| Genesis 2:2 | God “rested” | From all His work, on the seventh day |
| Genesis 2:3 | God “blessed” | The seventh day |
| Genesis 2:3 | God “sanctified” | The seventh day |
| Genesis 2:3 | “because” | His rest from all His created and made work is the stated reason |

This sequence is connected, but the expressions remain distinguishable. The blessing and sanctification are not merely alternative words for finishing or resting. They are additional actions expressly attributed to God.

A concise textual summary is:

**Finished work → God's ending and rest → God's blessing and sanctifying of the seventh day.**

This is a summary of the order and relationships stated in Genesis 2:1–3, not a newly established GUA-W technical sequence.

## Triple Constraint Law

### 1. Biblical compliance

The examination preserves Genesis 2:3 in the KJV and reads it in sequence with Genesis 2:1–2. It distinguishes the blessing and sanctification of the seventh day from the finishing of Creation and God's rest.

Exodus 20:8–11 is used as canonical context for the later Sabbath command. Isaiah 40:28 prevents the attribution of fatigue to the Creator. John 5:17 cautions against the claim that God ceased all work of every kind.

These cross-references clarify the scope of interpretation without replacing the wording of Genesis 2:3.

### 2. Scientific coherence

No scientific mechanism is required to establish the direct textual claims that God blessed and sanctified the seventh day.

The passage is making a Scriptural statement about God's action and the day He set apart. It is not presenting a scientific measurement of holiness, a physical process, or an empirical explanation of divine rest.

Scientific coherence is not treated as scientific confirmation of Scripture. No scientific claim is invented to fill a gap in the text.

### 3. GUA-W consistency

The record preserves God as Creator, Maker, Blesser, and Sanctifier. The created work and the seventh day remain recipients of God's actions, not their originating source.

The established Author/creation distinction is maintained. No new GUA-W layer, operator, or technical sequence is invented. Any further architectural placement must be supported by established GUA-W material.

### Joint admissibility result

The direct findings are admitted under all three constraints: God blessed and sanctified the seventh day because He had rested in it from all His work which He created and made. God is the One who acts; the seventh day is the object of His blessing and sanctification.

Exodus 20:8–11 provides further canonical context by connecting the Creation rest with the Sabbath command. Genesis 2:3 alone does not state the entire later Sabbath law. Divine fatigue, cessation of all divine activity, and an invented GUA-W operator are not admitted.
