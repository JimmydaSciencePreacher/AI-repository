# Study Record 0039 — Genesis 2:8 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 2:8 (KJV)**

> And the LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden; and there he put the man whom he had formed.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 2:8 distinguish between the LORD God planting a garden and placing the man there? What does “eastward in Eden” establish? What does the verse say about the man already formed in verse 7, and what remains to be learned about the garden and its placement?

The record must distinguish the wording of the verse from conclusions about geography, chronology, cultivation, and purpose. It must preserve the LORD God as the subject of both actions and must not assign the garden's planting or man's placement to man.

Genesis 2:8 expressly identifies the garden as being “in Eden” and describes it as “eastward”. It does not give coordinates or a modern geographical identification.

## Examination of the Hebrew wording

### 1. “Planted” — נָטַע (*nāṭaʿ*)

The verb *nāṭaʿ* means to plant, especially to set or establish plants in the ground. In Genesis 2:8, the LORD God is the subject and a garden is the object.

The verb identifies an action attributed to God. It does not say that man cultivated or established the garden.

**Direct finding:** The LORD God planted the garden.

**Not admitted:** A claim that this verb specifies a modern horticultural procedure, tools, or a particular planting technique.

### 2. “A garden” — גַּן (*gan*)

The noun *gan* means garden. Its usage can refer to a garden or enclosed cultivated place. The word identifies the place God planted.

Genesis 2:8 does not itself provide the garden's dimensions, complete layout, or boundaries. Those details must not be invented from the noun alone.

The garden is described as being “in Eden”. Thus, the wording distinguishes the garden from the larger named setting in which it is located.

**Direct finding:** God planted a garden.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the word alone establishes the garden's precise size, boundaries, or complete design.

### 3. “Eastward” — מִקֶּדֶם (*miqqedem*)

The expression is formed from *min* (“from”) and *qedem*, a term associated with the east, the front, or what is ancient/former depending on context. In this construction and the KJV rendering, it is understood directionally as “eastward”.

The verse therefore gives a directional description of the garden's location in relation to Eden or the narrative's geographical orientation. It does not supply a compass bearing, coordinates, distance, or a modern place-name.

**Direct finding:** The KJV describes the garden as “eastward”.

**Unresolved:** The precise geographical reference point and modern location, if any, intended by the expression.

**Not admitted:** A claim that this verse alone identifies Eden or the garden with a specific modern country, city, archaeological site, or coordinate.

### 4. “Eden” — עֵדֶן (*ʿēḏen*)

Eden is the proper name used in the verse. The construction places the garden in Eden: the garden is the planted place, and Eden is the named setting.

The verse does not say that “garden” and “Eden” are interchangeable names for exactly the same geographical extent. Later verses describe a river going out of Eden to water the garden and then dividing into four heads (Genesis 2:10–14). That wording further distinguishes the garden and Eden in the narrative, but the geographical details require their own examination.

**Direct finding:** The garden is located in Eden according to the verse.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the verse itself defines all of Eden's boundaries.

### 5. “Put” — וַיָּשֶׂם (*wayyāśem*)

The verb form in the KJV clause “there he put the man” is from *śîm* (שים), meaning to put, set, place, or appoint according to context. It is not the verb *nûaḥ* in this clause.

The form is a wayyiqtol narrative form, commonly used to advance a sequence of past actions. The LORD God is the subject; the man is the object; “there” refers to the garden just described.

The grammatical form supports reading this as the next action in the narrative. It does not quantify the time between the planting and the placement.

**Direct finding:** The LORD God put the man in the garden.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the verb itself specifies a technical process of relocation or a measurable interval.

### 6. “The man whom he had formed”

The clause identifies the man by reference to the prior formation described in Genesis 2:7. The Hebrew uses a form of *yāṣar* (“form, fashion”), the same root used in the previous verse.

The text thus maintains continuity: the man placed in the garden is the man the LORD God had formed.

The relative clause supports the narrative order of reference: formation is already presented before the placement statement. It does not state exactly where the formation occurred, how long before placement it occurred, or what the man experienced between those events.

**Direct finding:** The man placed in the garden is identified as the man God had formed.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the man was formed inside the garden; the verse does not say so.

## The distinction between Eden and the garden

Genesis 2:8 says “a garden eastward in Eden”. Genesis 2:10 then says:

> And a river went out of Eden to water the garden; and from thence it was parted, and became into four heads.

The wording in verse 10 is relevant because it describes a river going out of Eden to water the garden. This is consistent with distinguishing the garden from Eden as a broader setting. It does not by itself settle the geography or the exact boundaries.

For this record, the direct observation is limited: the garden is in Eden, and the narrative subsequently distinguishes the river's relation to Eden and the garden.

**Admitted:** The text distinguishes the named garden from the named setting Eden.

**Unresolved:** The precise spatial relationship and geographical extent.

## Formation, planting, and placement

The narrative sequence in Genesis 2:7–8 gives three distinct actions:

1. The LORD God formed man from the dust of the ground.
2. The LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden.
3. The LORD God put the man whom He had formed in the garden.

The verse order and the phrase “whom he had formed” establish that the man is already identified as formed when his placement is described. The garden is also described as planted by God.

This supports the limited observation that the man does not establish his own place in the garden. God forms him, plants the garden, and places him there.

The text does not explicitly say that every aspect of the garden was complete before man's formation, nor does it specify the elapsed time between the actions. It also does not yet state man's assignment; Genesis 2:15 will describe the man being put into the garden to dress it and keep it.

**Admitted:** God is the subject of formation, planting, and placement in this sequence.

**Unresolved:** The exact chronology and duration among the actions, and the full purpose of the garden before the later instructions are examined.

## Relationship to Genesis 2:9

Genesis 2:9 (KJV):

> And out of the ground made the LORD God to grow every tree that is pleasant to the sight, and good for food; the tree of life also in the midst of the garden, and the tree of knowledge of good and evil.

Verse 9 continues the garden account by describing trees God made to grow out of the ground. It identifies qualities of the trees and names the tree of life and the tree of knowledge of good and evil.

This gives further content to the garden setting. It does not authorize us to import the later narrative of Genesis 3 into the meaning of every detail before that account is examined.

**Direct finding:** The LORD God made trees grow out of the ground, including the two trees named in verse 9.

**Not admitted:** A claim that Genesis 2:8 alone describes the garden's complete contents or purpose.
