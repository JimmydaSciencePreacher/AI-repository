# Study Record 0043 — Genesis 2:13 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 2:13 (KJV)**

> And the name of the second river is Gihon: the same is it that compasseth the whole land of Ethiopia.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 2:13 identify about the second river, Gihon? What does the repeated word “compasseth” establish in relation to the whole land of Ethiopia, represented in Hebrew by *Kûš*? How should the KJV rendering be examined in its biblical and historical context? What is stated, and what remains unresolved about the river's modern identity and the land's location?

This record must distinguish the KJV translation “Ethiopia” from the borders and political identity of the modern country of Ethiopia. It must also distinguish the biblical name *Kûš* from any one proposed modern geographical reconstruction.

Verse 10 introduces four heads. Verses 11–12 describe the first, Pison. Verse 13 names the second, Gihon, and associates it with the whole land translated “Ethiopia”. Verse 14 names the third and fourth rivers and supplies a directional description for Hiddekel.

7. The verse gives no coordinates, distances, elevations, measured course, or modern national borders.
8. The verse does not identify Gihon with a currently known river.
9. The verse does not define the precise boundaries or full extent of Cush in this geographical description.
10. The verse does not state that Cush is identical to the modern nation-state of Ethiopia.

14. The verse does not state that the second river is the Nile or any other modern river.
15. The direct finding is that the second river is named Gihon and is described as encompassing the whole land of Cush, rendered “Ethiopia” in the KJV.

## Examination of the Hebrew wording

### 1. “The name of the second river” — וְשֵׁם הַנָּהָר הַשֵּׁנִי (*wĕšēm hannāhār haššēnî*)

The phrase identifies this as the name of the second river. The ordinal “second” connects the statement to the sequence of four heads introduced in Genesis 2:10.

**Direct finding:** Gihon is identified as the second river in the account.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the order in which the heads are named alone establishes their modern geographical order or course.

### 2. “Gihon” — גִּיחוֹן (*Gîḥôn*)

Gihon is the proper name given to the second river. The verse provides the name but does not explain its derivation or give a modern geographical identification.

The name Gihon occurs elsewhere in Scripture, including references to a water source associated with Jerusalem (for example, 1 Kings 1:33, 38, 45 and 2 Chronicles 32:30). Those passages must be examined in their own contexts. A shared name is not sufficient to prove that the Gihon of Genesis 2:13 and the Jerusalem Gihon are the same watercourse.

**Direct finding:** The second river is named Gihon.

**Unresolved:** The precise derivation of the name and the river's geographical identity.

**Not admitted:** Equating the river in Genesis 2:13 with the Jerusalem spring solely because the names match.

### 3. “The same is it” — הוּא (*hûʾ*)

The pronoun refers back to Gihon and identifies it as the river described by the following relative expression. The sentence continues to describe the same named river.

**Direct finding:** The river being described as encompassing the land is Gihon.

**Not admitted:** Treating the phrase as introducing a different river or a separate geographical feature.

### 4. “Compasseth” — הַסֹּבֵב (*hassōvēḇ*)

The expression is related to the Hebrew root *sāḇaḇ* (סבב), whose range includes going around, surrounding, turning about, or encompassing. The KJV uses “compasseth” here, as it does for Pison in Genesis 2:11.

The repeated wording establishes a textual parallel: both Pison and Gihon are described in relation to a whole land using the same Hebrew expression. It does not, by itself, establish that the rivers have identical courses, that the lands have identical shapes, or that either river forms a technically defined closed loop.

**Direct finding:** Gihon is described as going around or encompassing the whole land of Cush.

**Supported textual comparison:** Genesis 2:11 and 2:13 use the same expression to describe the relationship of the first and second rivers to their respective lands.

**Unresolved:** The exact route and spatial sense intended by the expression in each description.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the repeated verb proves an identical physical arrangement or a completely closed circuit around each land.

### 5. “The whole land” — כָּל־אֶרֶץ (*kol-ʾereṣ*)

The expression means “the whole land” or “all the land” in the stated description. It identifies the scope of the land associated with Gihon.

The phrase does not provide borders, dimensions, or coordinates. “Whole” should be preserved as the wording of the text without inventing a modern political boundary.

**Direct finding:** The description concerns the whole land of Cush.

**Not admitted:** Assigning the phrase to the present-day borders of a modern country without additional evidence.

### 6. “Ethiopia” — כּוּשׁ (*Kûš*, Cush)

The Hebrew name is *Kûš*, conventionally transliterated Cush. The KJV renders it “Ethiopia” in this verse. The ancient geographical and ethnographic name Cush is used elsewhere in the Bible, and its referent must be considered from the particular passage and its historical setting.

In biblical and ancient geographical usage, Cush is commonly associated with regions south of Egypt, including areas of Nubia and the Upper Nile region. The precise extent and usage can vary by period and context. The KJV rendering “Ethiopia” is an older English geographical designation; it must not automatically be equated with the exact borders of the modern Republic of Ethiopia.

This broad contextual observation does not identify Gihon. Nor does it settle the precise extent of Cush as intended in Genesis 2:13.

**Direct finding:** The Hebrew place-name is Cush (*Kûš*), rendered “Ethiopia” by the KJV.

**Contextual observation:** Biblical Cush is an ancient geographical and ethnographic designation associated in many contexts with regions south of Egypt.

**Unresolved:** The precise geographical extent of Cush in Genesis 2:13 and the relationship of this description to any modern map.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the KJV word “Ethiopia” proves that Gihon encircled the exact territory of the modern nation of Ethiopia.

## Comparison with Genesis 2:11

Genesis 2:11 (KJV) describes Pison:

> The name of the first is Pison: that is it which compasseth the whole land of Havilah, where there is gold;

Genesis 2:13 (KJV) describes Gihon:

> And the name of the second river is Gihon: the same is it that compasseth the whole land of Ethiopia.

The repeated expression “compasseth the whole land” is a deliberate textual parallel. Both statements identify a river and relate it to a named land.

The parallel establishes similarity in the wording and descriptive form. It does not establish that Havilah and Cush are adjacent, that the rivers follow the same kind of course, or that their modern identities are known.

**Admitted:** The same expression is used for the first and second rivers in relation to their respective lands.

**Not admitted:** A reconstruction that places the two lands or rivers in a particular modern arrangement solely from this repeated wording.
