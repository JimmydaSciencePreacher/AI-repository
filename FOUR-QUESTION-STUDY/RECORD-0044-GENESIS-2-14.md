# Study Record 0044 — Genesis 2:14 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 2:14 (KJV)**

> And the name of the third river is Hiddekel: that is it which goeth toward the east of Assyria. And the fourth river is Euphrates.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 2:14 identify about the third river, Hiddekel? What does “goeth toward the east of Assyria” establish about its direction and geographical relationship? What does the verse identify about the fourth river, Euphrates? What is stated, and what remains unresolved about their modern courses and their connection to the four heads introduced in Genesis 2:10?

This record must distinguish the names and directional description supplied by the KJV from modern geographical identifications and reconstructions. Familiarity with the Tigris and Euphrates does not authorize claims about the precise original course, the location of Eden, or the complete arrangement of all four heads without supporting evidence.

4. Euphrates.

The descriptions are not uniform. Pison and Gihon are described in relation to lands. Hiddekel is described by a direction relative to Assyria. Euphrates is named without an additional descriptive clause in this verse.

## Examination of the Hebrew wording

### 1. “The name of the third river” — וְשֵׁם הַנָּהָר הַשְּׁלִישִׁי (*wĕšēm hannāhār haššĕlîšî*)

The phrase identifies the third river in the sequence. The ordinal connects this description to the four heads introduced in Genesis 2:10.

**Direct finding:** Hiddekel is the third named river in the account.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the order of naming by itself supplies the modern geographical order or exact physical arrangement of all four rivers.

### 2. “Hiddekel” — חִדֶּקֶל (*Ḥiddeqel*)

Hiddekel is the proper name used by the KJV for the third river. The name is conventionally identified with the Tigris in biblical geography. Daniel 10:4 also uses the name Hiddekel for a river in the setting of Daniel's vision.

The conventional identification is an important contextual observation. It should not be confused with a complete reconstruction of the river's ancient course or with proof of Eden's location.

**Direct finding:** The third river is named Hiddekel.

**Contextual identification:** Hiddekel is conventionally identified with the Tigris.

**Unresolved:** The precise course intended in Genesis 2:14 and the degree to which the present river course can be mapped onto the ancient description.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the verse itself supplies modern coordinates or proves the location of Eden.

### 3. “Goeth” — הֹלֵךְ (*hōlēḵ*)

The form is from the Hebrew root *hālak* (הלך), meaning to go, walk, or proceed. Here it describes the river's course or direction in relation to Assyria.

The wording gives a directional description, but does not specify a measured path, source, endpoint, or complete route.

**Direct finding:** Hiddekel is described as going in the stated direction relative to Assyria.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the verb alone supplies the river's full course or exact geographical coordinates.

### 4. “Toward the east of” — קִדְמַת (*qidmat*)

The expression is related to *qedem* (קדם), a term that can denote the east, the front, or what is in front, depending on context. In this construction the KJV renders it “toward the east of”.

The expression establishes a directional relationship involving Assyria. It does not itself define Assyria's borders, specify a modern compass bearing from a surveyed point, or describe the entire route of Hiddekel.

**Direct finding:** The KJV describes Hiddekel as going toward the east of Assyria.

**Unresolved:** The exact spatial scope intended by the phrase and the geographical reference point from which the direction is understood.

**Not admitted:** A claim that this phrase alone determines a precise modern course or a complete map of ancient Assyria.

### 5. “Assyria” — אַשּׁוּר (*ʾAššûr*)

The Hebrew name is Asshur/Assyria. It identifies the geographical reference used in the directional description. Assyria is a known ancient regional and political designation in biblical and historical contexts, but its extent changed across periods.

The verse does not specify which boundary or historical extent should be used for a modern map, nor does it provide coordinates for the region.

**Direct finding:** Assyria is the named geographical reference for Hiddekel's direction.

**Unresolved:** The precise extent of Assyria intended in this verse and how its boundaries relate to any particular historical period or modern map.

**Not admitted:** Treating Assyria as if it had one unchanging boundary across all periods.

### 6. “The fourth river” — וְהַנָּהָר הָרְבִיעִי (*wĕhannāhār hārĕḇîʿî*)

The phrase identifies the fourth river in the sequence. The text then gives its name.

**Direct finding:** The fourth river is separately identified as Euphrates.

### 7. “Euphrates” — פְּרָת (*Pĕrāṯ*)

The Hebrew name is *Pĕrāṯ*, rendered Euphrates. The Euphrates is a well-known river in the biblical and historical geographical record. The name's continuing identification is strong contextual evidence for recognizing the river-name.

However, Genesis 2:14 does not give its source, route, direction, length, or relation to the other three heads beyond naming it as the fourth. Nor does the verse state that its modern course is identical in every detail to the course in the account.

**Direct finding:** The fourth river is named Euphrates.

**Contextual identification:** The name is conventionally identified with the historical and modern Euphrates River.

**Unresolved:** The precise course and geographical configuration intended in the Genesis account, and how it related spatially to the other heads at the point described.

**Not admitted:** A claim that naming the Euphrates alone establishes the location of Eden or reconstructs the complete four-river system.

## Comparison of the four descriptions

| Order | Name | Description in Genesis 2:11–14 |
|---|---|---|
| First | Pison | Encompasses the whole land of Havilah; resources are named. |
| Second | Gihon | Encompasses the whole land of Cush (KJV: Ethiopia). |
| Third | Hiddekel | Goes toward the east of Assyria. |
| Fourth | Euphrates | Named; no further course description is added in this verse. |

The text gives four named heads but does not give the same kind of geographical description for each. That difference is part of the evidence.

The first two descriptions use a river-to-land relationship. The third gives a directional relation to Assyria. The fourth supplies a name only.

The descriptions should not be made artificially uniform. Nor should the modern identification of two familiar rivers be used to fill in details the passage does not state about the other two.
