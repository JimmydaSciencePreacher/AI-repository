# Study Record 0045 — Genesis 2:15 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 2:15 (KJV)**

> And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.

## Question under examination

What does Genesis 2:15 say the LORD God does with the man? What is the relationship between taking, placing, and the garden of Eden? What do “dress” and “keep” mean in their Hebrew context? What responsibilities are expressly assigned, and what must not be imported from later passages? How does this verse connect the man, the garden, and the work appointed to him?

The examination must distinguish God's action from man's assigned work, the garden from Eden, and the direct wording of this verse from broader theological conclusions. It must also compare Genesis 2:15 with Genesis 2:8, which has already said that God placed the man in the garden. The repeated placement language should not automatically be treated as two separate placements or as a chronology that the text does not state.

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 2:9 (KJV)**

**Genesis 2:10 (KJV)**

**Genesis 2:15 (KJV)**

Verse 8 states that the LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden and put there the man whom He had formed. Verses 9–14 describe trees, a river, and the four heads. Verse 15 states that the LORD God took the man and put him into the garden of Eden, with two stated purposes: to dress it and to keep it.

2. The object of God's action is “the man”, referring to the man formed in Genesis 2:7 and already mentioned in Genesis 2:8.
3. The verse uses two action expressions: God “took” the man and “put” him into the garden of Eden.
4. The destination is “the garden of Eden”.
5. The verse gives two purpose expressions for the man's placement: “to dress it” and “to keep it”.
6. The pronoun “it” in both purpose expressions refers to the garden.
7. The verse assigns the man work or service in relation to the garden.
8. The verse assigns the man a keeping or guarding responsibility in relation to the garden.
9. The verse does not say that the man created the garden, originated its purpose, or independently appointed himself to this work.

12. The passage does not explicitly explain whether Genesis 2:15 is a second physical placement, a recapitulation, or a closer description of the placement reported in verse 8.

## Examination of the Hebrew wording

### 1. “The LORD God” — יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים (*YHWH ʾĔlōhîm*)

The verse identifies the LORD God as the acting subject. The man is not the initiator of the placement described here.

**Direct finding:** The LORD God acts; the man is the recipient of the action.

**Not admitted:** Recasting the man's assignment as self-originated authority or independent appointment.

### 2. “Took” — וַיִּקַּח (*wayyiqqaḥ*), from לָקַח (*lāqaḥ*)

The verb *lāqaḥ* has a broad range that includes taking, receiving, or taking along, with its precise sense determined by context. Here the KJV renders it “took”.

The verse reports God's taking of the man, followed by God's placing him in the garden. It does not explain the physical manner of movement or provide a separate technical mechanism.

**Direct finding:** God took the man.

**Unresolved:** The precise physical manner or sequence implied by the two verbs.

**Not admitted:** A detailed movement mechanism or an additional event not stated by the text.

### 3. “Put” — וַיַּנִּחֵהוּ (*wayyanniḥēhû*), from נוּחַ (*nûaḥ*)

The form is associated with the Hebrew root *nûaḥ*, whose range includes resting, settling, or causing to rest/be set down. In this context the KJV renders the form “put”.

The wording identifies God as the one who places the man in the garden. It is related to the destination and appointment stated in the sentence; it does not by itself establish a complete theory of human purpose or the mechanics of the placement.

**Direct finding:** God put the man into the garden of Eden.

**Not admitted:** Treating the root's wider range as proof that the verse explicitly says the man was placed there to achieve a particular psychological or spiritual state beyond the stated purposes.

### 4. “Into the garden of Eden” — בְּגַן־עֵדֶן (*bĕgan-ʿēḏen*)

The phrase identifies the destination as the garden of Eden. Genesis 2:8 has distinguished the garden planted eastward in Eden; verse 15 uses the expression “the garden of Eden”.

The verse places the man in the garden. It does not say that the man is placed throughout all of Eden or that garden and Eden are interchangeable in every respect.

**Direct finding:** The garden of Eden is the destination of the placement.

**Not admitted:** Collapsing the garden and the wider designation Eden into an undifferentiated geographical term.

### 5. “To dress it” — לְעָבְדָהּ (*ləʿāḇḏāh*), from עָבַד (*ʿāḇaḏ*)

The root *ʿāḇaḏ* has a broad semantic range including work, serve, labour, and perform service. In agricultural contexts it can refer to working or cultivating land. The KJV renders the form here “to dress it”.

The immediate object is the garden. The context therefore supports work or cultivation in relation to the garden. The broader root meaning “serve” should be acknowledged, but the verse should not be reduced to an unrelated religious or institutional meaning.

**Direct finding:** The man is assigned work/service in relation to the garden.

**Contextual understanding:** The KJV “dress” is commonly understood here as working or cultivating the garden.

**Unresolved:** The exact tasks and methods included in this assignment.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the verse specifies a complete agricultural system, particular tools, or a detailed work schedule.

### 6. “And to keep it” — וּלְשָׁמְרָהּ (*ûləšomrāh*), from שָׁמַר (*šāmar*)

The root *šāmar* includes keeping, watching, guarding, observing, or preserving, depending on context. The KJV renders it “to keep it”. The object is again the garden.

The paired purpose expressions assign both work/service and keeping/guarding in relation to the same garden. The verse does not identify a specific threat or say that the garden is already under attack.

**Direct finding:** The man is assigned responsibility to keep or guard the garden.

**Contextual understanding:** The word can carry the sense of watching over or guarding, as well as keeping.

**Unresolved:** The exact scope of the keeping responsibility and whether any specific threat is in view at this point.

**Not admitted:** A claim that Genesis 2:15 itself identifies a particular adversary, danger, or failure that the man must prevent.

## The relationship between Genesis 2:8 and 2:15

Genesis 2:8 says:

> And the LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden; and there he put the man whom he had formed.

Genesis 2:15 says:

> And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.

Both verses attribute the placement to the LORD God. Verse 8 gives the planting of the garden and the placement of the formed man. Verse 15 states the placement again and supplies the two purposes associated with the man's presence there.

The repeated placement language is important. It may function as a return to, or fuller statement of, the earlier placement in the narrative. The text does not explicitly tell us whether it describes a second placement event. Therefore, the record preserves the repetition without manufacturing a chronology.

**Admitted:** Both verses attribute the man's placement in the garden to God; verse 15 states the purposes of dressing and keeping.

**Unresolved:** The exact literary and chronological relationship between the two placement statements.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the man was necessarily removed and physically relocated a second time.

## The relationship between the two assigned purposes

The sentence joins two infinitive purpose expressions:

- To dress/work/serve the garden.
- To keep/watch/guard the garden.

Both relate to the same object: the garden.

The wording establishes two responsibilities, but it does not define a technical division of labour or say that one responsibility must be completed before the other. It is reasonable to observe that cultivation and keeping are distinct aspects of the stated assignment. It is not warranted to invent a full operational sequence.

**Direct finding:** The man's assignment includes both working the garden and keeping it.

**Not admitted:** A detailed sequence, hierarchy, or technical system not supplied by the verse.

## Related Scriptural references

### Genesis 2:7–9

These verses provide the immediate context: the LORD God forms the man, plants the garden, and places the man there. They establish the relationship between divine formation, divine planting, and divine placement before the work assignment is stated in verse 15.

### Genesis 1:28

God blesses humanity and gives instructions concerning fruitfulness, filling the earth, subduing it, and dominion. This is relevant to the broader biblical account of received human responsibility. It must not be used to erase the particular wording or scope of Genesis 2:15.

### Genesis 3:17–19

After the transgression, the ground is cursed in relation to Adam, and toil and thorns are described. These later statements distinguish the pre-Fall assignment in Genesis 2:15 from the hardship described after the Fall. Genesis 2:15 itself does not call the garden work a punishment.

### Genesis 3:23–24

The man is sent out of the garden, and cherubims are placed to keep the way of the tree of life. This later passage uses keeping/guarding language in a different scene and with different agents. It can be compared linguistically, but it must not be imported backward as proof that Genesis 2:15 already identifies the same threat or task.

### Genesis 2:16–17

The next verses record God's command concerning the trees and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. They are relevant to the man's received responsibilities and boundaries, but their specific content belongs to those verses and should not be silently added to the wording of verse 15.
