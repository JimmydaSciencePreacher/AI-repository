# Study Record 0046 — Genesis 2:16 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 2:16 (KJV)**

> And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat:

## Question under examination

What does the LORD God command the man? What is the scope of “every tree of the garden”? What does “thou mayest freely eat” express in Hebrew? What does this verse establish about provision, permission, and the man's received responsibility? How does it relate to Genesis 2:15, and what must remain for Genesis 2:17?

The examination must distinguish the One who commands from the one who receives the command; the provision named from the permission given; and the positive statement in verse 16 from the boundary stated in verse 17.

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 2:15–17 (KJV)**

> 15 And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.  
> 16 And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat:  
> 17 But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.

Verse 15 records the man's placement and assigned work. Verse 16 records the LORD God's command and the broad permission concerning the trees of the garden. Verse 17 begins with “But” and states a specific prohibition and its stated consequence. Verse 16 must be read as part of this two-verse command, but its own positive wording must not be erased by importing the prohibition before examining the distinction.

## Examination of the Hebrew wording

### 1. “The LORD God” — יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים (*YHWH ʾĔlōhîm*)

The LORD God is identified as the acting subject, as in Genesis 2:15. The command originates with Him; the man receives it.

**Direct finding:** The LORD God is the source of the command in this verse.

**Not admitted:** Recasting the permission as an authority the man originates independently.

### 2. “Commanded” — וַיְצַו (*wayṣaw*), from צָוָה (*ṣāwāh*)

The verb *ṣāwāh* means to command, charge, or give an instruction. The form in this sentence reports that the LORD God commanded the man.

The wording is stronger and more specific than merely reporting that God spoke. The content that follows is presented as a command.

**Direct finding:** God issues a command to the man.

**Not admitted:** Treating the instruction as a suggestion originating with the man or as a rule imposed by an independent created authority.

### 3. “The man” — הָאָדָם (*hāʾāḏām*)

The command is addressed to “the man”, the man already formed and placed in the garden in the preceding account. The verse does not introduce a new human subject.

**Direct finding:** The recipient is the man in the Genesis 2 narrative.

**Not admitted:** Extending the immediate addressee beyond what the passage establishes without examining the wider Scriptural context.

### 4. “Of every tree of the garden” — מִכֹּל עֵץ־הַגָּן (*mikkol ʿēṣ-haggān*)

The phrase is rendered in the KJV as “Of every tree of the garden”. It identifies the provision by reference to the trees belonging to the garden.

The expression is broad, but verse 17 immediately identifies a particular tree as excluded from the permission. Therefore, “every tree” in verse 16 cannot responsibly be isolated from the qualification that follows. The two verses together establish broad provision and a specific boundary.

**Direct finding:** The command grants permission concerning the trees of the garden broadly.

**Contextual qualification:** Genesis 2:17 expressly excludes the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

**Not admitted:** Reading verse 16 as an absolute permission that cancels or contradicts verse 17.

### 5. “Thou mayest freely eat” — אָכֹל תֹּאכֵל (*ʾāḵōl tōʾḵēl*)

The Hebrew places the infinitive absolute of the verb אָכַל (*ʾāḵal*, “eat”) before a finite form of the same verb. This construction commonly intensifies or emphatically affirms the verbal action. The KJV expresses it as “thou mayest freely eat”.

The construction emphasizes the permission to eat; it is not a separate technical term meaning that the man possesses independent authority to define the command. The permission remains within the command given by the LORD God and must be read alongside the qualification in verse 17.

**Direct finding:** The man is expressly permitted to eat; the Hebrew construction gives the permission an emphatic form.

**Not admitted:** Treating the intensifying construction as proof of unlimited permission beyond the command's stated scope.

### 6. The colon at the end of the KJV verse

The KJV prints a colon at the end of verse 16, and verse 17 begins “But”. The punctuation helps readers see that the statement continues into a contrast. The Hebrew text itself is not governed by modern English punctuation, so the argument should rest primarily on the wording and relationship of the two verses.

**Direct finding:** Verse 17 supplies the specific qualification to the broad permission in verse 16.

**Not admitted:** Building a doctrine on the English punctuation alone.

## Provision and permission

The verse joins two distinguishable matters:

- **Provision:** “every tree of the garden” identifies the broad range of trees addressed.
- **Permission:** “thou mayest freely eat” states what the man is authorized to do with that provision.

Provision and permission are related, but they are not identical. The presence of a provision does not by itself state every permitted use; the command expresses the permitted action. Verse 17 then identifies a boundary.

This distinction is a textual observation, not a claim that Genesis 2:16 supplies a complete theory of ownership, law, appetite, or human agency.

**Admitted:** God is the source of both the command and the permission stated in this verse.

**Not admitted:** A claim that the man has authority to revise the permission or define its limits independently.

## Relationship to Genesis 2:15 and 2:17

### Genesis 2:15 — received responsibility

The LORD God places the man in the garden “to dress it and to keep it”. The man receives a responsibility in relation to the garden.

### Genesis 2:16 — received command and permission

The LORD God commands the man and grants permission to eat from the garden's trees. The text now records not only the man's assigned work but also an express instruction governing his action.

### Genesis 2:17 — specific boundary

The next verse begins “But” and prohibits eating from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, giving the stated consequence: “for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.”

The sequence in the text is therefore: assigned responsibility (verse 15), broad permission (verse 16), and specific prohibition with stated consequence (verse 17). This is a description of the passage's presentation, not a claim that the verses establish a complete abstract legal system.

**Admitted:** The permission in verse 16 is qualified by the specific prohibition in verse 17.

**Not admitted:** Treating the broad permission as permission to disregard the immediately following boundary.
