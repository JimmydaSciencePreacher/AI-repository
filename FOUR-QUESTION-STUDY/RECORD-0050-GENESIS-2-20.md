# Study Record 0050 — Genesis 2:20 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 2:20 (KJV)**

> And Adam gave names to all cattle, and to the fowl of the air, and to every beast of the field; but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him.

## Question under examination

What groups does Adam name? How does verse 20 summarize the naming in verse 19? What does the contrastive “but” introduce? What does “there was not found an help meet for him” state? Who is represented as the finder—or does the KJV leave the agent unstated? Does the verse say that Adam searched, that God searched, or simply report the result? What does this establish in relation to God's purpose in verse 18, and what must remain for the account in verses 21–23?

The examination must distinguish the naming report from the concluding result, the result from an assumed search procedure, and the absence of a corresponding help among the creatures named from any claim about the worth or purpose of animals.

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 2:18–22 (KJV)**

> 18 And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him an help meet for him.  
> 19 And out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast of the field, and every fowl of the air; and brought them unto Adam to see what he would call them: and whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof.  
> 20 And Adam gave names to all cattle, and to the fowl of the air, and to every beast of the field; but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him.  
> 21 And the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, and he slept: and he took one of his ribs, and closed up the flesh instead thereof;  
> 22 And the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman, and brought her unto the man.

Verse 18 announces God's purpose to make a help corresponding to the man. Verse 19 describes God bringing creatures to Adam and Adam naming them. Verse 20 repeats the naming in summary form and adds a contrasting conclusion: no help corresponding to Adam was found for him. Verses 21–22 then narrate God's action in causing the deep sleep, taking from the man, making a woman, and bringing her to him.

This record examines verse 20. It does not import the details of the woman's formation from verses 21–22 into the wording of verse 20.

## Direct observations

1. Adam is the explicit subject of the first clause: “Adam gave names”.
2. The groups named are cattle, fowl of the air, and every beast of the field.
3. The naming report corresponds to the bringing and naming described in verse 19.
4. The conjunction “but” introduces a contrast with the naming statement.
5. The contrasting statement concerns Adam: “for Adam”.
6. The result concerns an *help meet for him*—the same expression used in verse 18.
7. The verse does not say that an animal was the help corresponding to Adam.
8. The verse does not explicitly narrate Adam conducting a search or describe a search procedure.
9. The KJV renders the result impersonally/passively: “there was not found”.
10. The Hebrew wording uses a form of *māṣāʾ* (“find”), with the agent not expressed as a separate noun in this clause.
11. The verse does not say that God was unable to provide the help He had promised.
12. The verse does not say that the animals were defective, worthless, or created without purpose.
13. The verse does not say that Adam rejected the animals or that they were presented as potential mates.
14. The verse does not yet describe the making of the woman.
15. The direct finding is that the naming account concludes by stating that no help corresponding to Adam was found for him.

## Examination of the Hebrew wording

### 1. “And Adam gave names” — וַיִּקְרָא הָאָדָם (*wayyiqrāʾ hāʾādām*)

The verb is from קָרָא (*qārāʾ*), “to call” or “to name”. The subject is explicitly “the man” (Adam). This restates the naming action from verse 19 in a summary form.

**Direct finding:** Adam is expressly identified as the one who names the creatures.

**Not admitted:** Saying Adam formed the creatures or originated their existence through naming.

### 2. “To all cattle, and to the fowl of the air, and to every beast of the field”

The verse gives the creature groups in a slightly different order from verse 19: cattle, birds of the air, and beasts of the field. The wording summarizes the scope of Adam's naming in the narrative.

The terms are ordinary creature-group descriptions in the KJV. They should not be converted into a modern taxonomic inventory without further evidence.

**Direct finding:** The verse reports Adam naming the cattle, fowl, and beasts of the field.

**Not admitted:** Inferring a complete modern classification of all animal species from the list.

### 3. “But” — וּלְאָדָם (*ûlĕʾādām*) and the contrastive clause

The KJV begins the second part with “but”. The Hebrew clause begins with a conjunction and “for/to Adam”. The contrast is between the naming of the creatures and the result concerning a help corresponding to Adam.

The conjunction marks a turn in the account. The naming is complete as reported, but the corresponding help announced in verse 18 has not been found for Adam in this episode.

**Direct finding:** The verse contrasts the completed naming report with the absence of a corresponding help for Adam.

**Not admitted:** Treating the contrast as a declaration that the animals have no value, or that the naming itself failed.

### 4. “For Adam” — לְאָדָם (*lĕʾādām*)

The phrase identifies Adam as the one for whom the help would be suitable or corresponding. It echoes the “for him” relation in verse 18.

**Direct finding:** The stated lack is specifically in relation to Adam and the corresponding help described in verse 18.

**Not admitted:** Turning the phrase into a statement of ownership or reducing the counterpart to a possession.

### 5. “There was not found” — לֹא־מָצָא (*lōʾ māṣāʾ*)

The verb is from מָצָא (*māṣāʾ*), “to find”. In the Hebrew clause the verb is a Qal perfect, third-person masculine singular form, preceded by “not”. The KJV renders the clause “there was not found”, an idiomatic English construction that does not name an agent.

The Hebrew verb form is not itself a passive Niphal form. Its grammatical subject is not separately expressed in the clause. Depending on how the clause is construed in context, translations may render the sense actively (“he did not find”) or idiomatically/passively (“there was not found”). The immediate narrative makes Adam the central human participant in the naming episode, but the wording should not be made to say more than it does.

Accordingly, the verse clearly reports the result—no corresponding help was found for Adam—but it does not explicitly narrate who conducted a deliberate search, how a search was conducted, or whether the finding language summarizes the outcome of the presentation.

**Direct finding:** The text reports that no help corresponding to Adam was found for him.

**Unresolved:** The precise implied agent or perspective of the finding clause and the extent to which “finding” describes a deliberate search rather than the result of the episode.

**Not admitted:** Claiming as an explicit statement of this verse that Adam systematically searched, that God searched and failed, or that God lacked the ability to provide the promised help.

### 6. “An help meet for him” — עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ (*ʿēzer kĕnegdô*)

This is the same expression examined in Record 0048. *ʿĒzer* means help or aid; *kĕnegdô* conveys correspondence, suitability, or being a fitting counterpart to him.

The repetition connects verse 20 directly to God's announced purpose in verse 18. The result is not merely that Adam did not find any help of any kind; the specific expression is a help corresponding to him.

**Direct finding:** The help not found is the particular corresponding help announced in verse 18.

**Not admitted:** Replacing the expression with “servant”, “assistant of lower rank”, or “identical duplicate”.

## The relation between verses 18, 19, and 20

The sequence can be stated without adding an unrecorded mechanism:

1. **Verse 18 — purpose:** God declares that He will make a help corresponding to the man.
2. **Verse 19 — presentation and naming:** God forms and brings creatures to Adam; Adam names them.
3. **Verse 20 — reported result:** Adam's naming is summarized, and the text states that no help corresponding to him was found for him.
4. **Verses 21–22 — subsequent divine action:** God causes the deep sleep, takes from the man, makes a woman, and brings her to him.

The contrast in verse 20 is meaningful because the naming of the creatures does not produce the corresponding help announced in verse 18. But the verse does not say that the animals were created solely for this comparison, that Adam was selecting among them as mates, or that the naming was a failed experiment. Those explanations go beyond the direct wording.

**Admitted:** The episode of bringing and naming creatures does not conclude with a corresponding help for Adam; the narrative then proceeds to God's action in verses 21–22.

**Unresolved:** The full literary and theological function of the naming episode within the larger sequence.

## Distinctions that must be preserved

### Naming is not finding

Adam names the creatures. The final clause concerns whether a corresponding help is found for him. The verse joins these statements but does not equate the two actions.

### Absence of a counterpart is not absence of creaturely value

The animals are not identified as the corresponding human help. That does not establish that they are useless, defective, or without their own created purpose.

### A reported result is not a narrated search procedure

The KJV says “there was not found”. The verse does not describe a systematic search, list its steps, or explicitly name a searcher in the English clause. Any claim about the implied agent must be identified as grammatical or contextual interpretation, not quoted as an explicit event.

### God's stated purpose is not God's failed ability

Verse 18 records God's intention to make a corresponding help. Verse 20 reports that none was found in the creature-naming episode. The next verses record God's own action. The text does not portray God as unable to fulfill His stated purpose.

## Relationship to Genesis 2:21–24

### Genesis 2:21–22 — God's action

The LORD God causes a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, takes from him, closes the flesh, makes a woman, and brings her to the man. These actions are narrated after the result in verse 20. Their details must be examined in their own record.

### Genesis 2:23 — Adam's recognition and speech

The man responds with speech concerning the woman. This response is not yet part of verse 20 and must not be imported into it.

### Genesis 2:24 — joining

The passage states that a man leaves father and mother, cleaves to his wife, and they become one flesh. This is a further statement in the sequence, not a definition supplied by verse 20 alone.

**Direct finding:** Verse 20 prepares the narrative transition to God's action in verses 21–22 by reporting that no corresponding help was found for Adam in the preceding episode.

**Not admitted:** Treating verse 20 as if it already contained the formation, recognition, or joining described later.
