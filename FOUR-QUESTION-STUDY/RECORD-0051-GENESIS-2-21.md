# Study Record 0051 — Genesis 2:21 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 2:21 (KJV)**

> And the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, and he slept: and he took one of his ribs, and closed up the flesh instead thereof;

## Question under examination

Who causes the deep sleep? What does the Hebrew expression translated “deep sleep” mean? What sequence of actions does the verse explicitly record? Who is the subject of “he took” and “closed up”? What does the KJV “rib” translate, and what range of meaning does the Hebrew *ṣēlāʿ* carry? What does the verse say about the man's flesh, and what details of the woman's making are deferred to verse 22?

The examination must distinguish God's action from Adam's experience, the taking from the closing, the KJV's “rib” from the broader lexical range of the Hebrew word, and what verse 21 records from what verse 22 will state.

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 2:18–23 (KJV)**

> 18 And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him an help meet for him.  
> 19 And out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast of the field, and every fowl of the air; and brought them unto Adam to see what he would call them: and whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof.  
> 20 And Adam gave names to all cattle, and to the fowl of the air, and to every beast of the field; but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him.  
> 21 And the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, and he slept: and he took one of his ribs, and closed up the flesh instead thereof;  
> 22 And the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman, and brought her unto the man.  
> 23 And Adam said, This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh: she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.

Verse 18 records God's announced purpose. Verses 19–20 record the bringing and naming of creatures and the statement that no corresponding help was found for Adam. Verse 21 then records the LORD God's causing Adam to enter a deep sleep, taking one of his *ṣēlāʿ*, and closing the flesh in its place. Verse 22 identifies the LORD God as the one who makes a woman from what He took and brings her to the man. Verse 23 records Adam's response.

This record examines verse 21 directly. The making of the woman is explicitly stated in verse 22; Adam's recognition and speech are stated in verse 23. Neither should be silently substituted for the wording of verse 21.

## Direct observations

1. The explicit acting subject at the beginning of the verse is “the LORD God”.
2. God causes a deep sleep to fall upon Adam.
3. Adam is then stated to have slept.
4. The narrative continues with “and he took”; in context, the subject remains the LORD God.
5. God takes one of Adam's *ṣēlāʿ*, rendered “ribs” in the KJV.
6. The verse next says that God closed up the flesh in its place.
7. The verse does not name a human agent for the taking or closing.
8. The verse does not describe Adam performing or directing these actions.
9. The verse does not specify the duration or physiological mechanism of the deep sleep.
10. The verse does not describe the exact anatomy, dimensions, or surgical procedure involved in the taking.
11. The verse does not itself state that the woman is made; that statement appears in verse 22.
12. The verse does not say that Adam dies; it says that he sleeps.
13. The verse does not explain the biological or medical process by which the flesh is closed.
14. The verse records a sequence of divine action and Adam's sleep, but it does not provide a complete technical account of the event.

## Examination of the Hebrew wording

### 1. “The LORD God caused” — וַיַּפֵּל יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים (*wayyappēl YHWH ʾĔlōhîm*)

The verb is from נָפַל (*nāphal*, “fall”), here in a causative Hiphil form: “caused to fall”. The grammatical subject is the LORD God, and the object affected is the deep sleep that falls upon Adam.

The wording explicitly attributes the initiation of this sleep to God. It is not presented as Adam independently deciding to sleep.

**Direct finding:** The LORD God causes the deep sleep to fall upon Adam.

**Not admitted:** Recasting the event as ordinary self-initiated sleep or assigning its initiation to Adam.

### 2. “A deep sleep” — תַּרְדֵּמָה (*tardēmâ*)

The noun *tardēmâ* denotes a deep sleep or profound sleep. It occurs elsewhere in the Hebrew Scriptures in contexts where a person is overcome by a deep sleep, including Genesis 15:12, 1 Samuel 26:12, Job 4:13, and Job 33:15. Its use in those passages does not mean that every occurrence has an identical narrative function.

In Genesis 2:21 the text directly says that the LORD God causes this state to fall upon Adam. The verse does not supply a medical diagnosis or describe the bodily mechanism.

**Direct finding:** Adam enters a deep sleep caused by the LORD God.

**Not admitted:** Identifying the event as a modern anesthetic procedure, coma, death, or a particular neurological state. Those labels are not supplied by the verse.

### 3. “Upon Adam, and he slept” — עַל־הָאָדָם וַיִּישָׁן (*ʿal-hāʾādām wayyîšān*)

The phrase identifies Adam as the one upon whom the deep sleep falls. The following verb, from יָשֵׁן (*yāšēn*, “sleep”), states that he slept.

The two clauses should be kept distinct: God causes the deep sleep to fall upon Adam; Adam sleeps. The text does not describe Adam's awareness or experience during the event.

**Direct finding:** Adam is the recipient of the divinely caused sleep and is stated to have slept.

**Unresolved:** The precise nature of Adam's subjective experience, which the verse does not describe.

### 4. “And he took” — וַיִּקַּח (*wayyiqqaḥ*)

The verb is from לָקַח (*lāqaḥ*), “take”. The subject is not repeated in the Hebrew clause, but the continuous narrative context identifies the LORD God as the actor: He caused the sleep, and He took the *ṣēlāʿ*.

Verse 22 confirms this identification by referring to “the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man”.

**Direct finding:** The LORD God takes the item from Adam.

**Not admitted:** Assigning the taking to Adam or to an unnamed human agent.

### 5. “One of his ribs” — אַחַת מִצַּלְעֹתָיו (*ʾaḥat miṣṣalʿōṯāyw*)

The expression consists of “one” and a form of צֵלָע (*ṣēlāʿ*), with the possessive suffix “his”. The KJV translates *ṣēlāʿ* here as “rib”.

The Hebrew noun *ṣēlāʿ* has a broader lexical range. Depending on context, it can refer to a side, side-part, or rib; it is also used for the sides of structures or objects, such as parts of the tabernacle. In this human-body context, “rib” is the traditional KJV rendering. The broader lexical range should be acknowledged, but it does not by itself prove that the intended bodily part was an entire half or side of Adam.

The verse says “one of” his *ṣēlāʿ*. Verse 22 refers back to the item taken. The text does not provide anatomical measurements or a detailed account of the structure removed.

**Direct finding:** God takes one of Adam's *ṣēlāʿ*, rendered “ribs” in the KJV.

**Unresolved:** Whether the English word “rib” fully communicates the anatomical nuance intended by the Hebrew in this context.

**Not admitted:** Claiming that the word necessarily means an entire side of Adam, or claiming that its broader uses in architecture prove that meaning here.

### 6. “And closed up the flesh instead thereof” — וַיִּסְגֹּר בָּשָׂר תַּחְתֶּנָּה (*wayyisgōr bāśār taḥtennāh*)

The verb סָגַר (*sāgar*) means “shut” or “close”. The subject remains the LORD God. The object is “flesh” (*bāśār*), and the final expression indicates the place or position in relation to what was taken: the flesh is closed in its place.

The KJV renders the phrase “closed up the flesh instead thereof”. The wording records God's closing of the flesh where the *ṣēlāʿ* was taken. It does not specify the medical process, the extent of the closure, or whether any visible mark remained.

**Direct finding:** After taking the *ṣēlāʿ*, God closes the flesh in its place.

**Not admitted:** Treating this as a detailed surgical description or supplying a healing mechanism absent from the text.

## The sequence recorded in verse 21

The verse presents an ordered sequence:

1. **God causes** the deep sleep to fall upon Adam.
2. **Adam sleeps.**
3. **God takes** one of Adam's *ṣēlāʿ*.
4. **God closes** the flesh in its place.

The subjects and actions matter. Adam is the recipient of the sleep and the one from whom the *ṣēlāʿ* is taken. The LORD God is the actor who causes the sleep, takes, and closes.

The text does not say that Adam cooperates consciously, performs the taking, or closes the flesh. Nor does it describe the process in technical medical terms.

**Admitted:** The verse attributes the initiating and operative actions to God and states Adam's sleep.

**Not admitted:** Adding an unrecorded human action, medical mechanism, or detailed anatomical reconstruction.

## Relationship to Genesis 2:18–23

### Genesis 2:18 — purpose announced

God declares that He will make a help corresponding to the man. The purpose is stated before the sequence involving the creatures and the deep sleep.

### Genesis 2:19–20 — creatures named; no corresponding help found

God brings creatures to Adam, Adam names them, and the text states that no corresponding help was found for him. This is the immediate narrative context for the action in verse 21.

### Genesis 2:21 — preparation and taking

God causes the deep sleep, takes one of Adam's *ṣēlāʿ*, and closes the flesh. Verse 21 does not yet state the making of the woman.

### Genesis 2:22 — making and bringing

The next verse explicitly says that the LORD God made a woman from the *ṣēlāʿ* taken from the man and brought her to him. That is the next recorded action and must be examined in its own right.

### Genesis 2:23 — Adam's recognition

Adam speaks of “bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh” and names the woman. His response belongs to verse 23, not verse 21.

**Direct finding:** Verse 21 records the divinely caused sleep, taking, and closing that precede the making and bringing narrated in verse 22.

**Not admitted:** Treating verse 21 alone as a complete account of the woman's making, Adam's recognition, or the joining described later.

## Distinctions that must be preserved

### Sleep is not death

The verse says Adam slept. It does not say he died. “Deep sleep” must not be converted into death or a spiritual state without further textual evidence.

### Taking is not making

Verse 21 says God took one of Adam's *ṣēlāʿ*. Verse 22 says God made a woman from what He had taken. The two actions are sequentially related but are not the same statement.

### “Rib” and the full lexical range of *ṣēlāʿ*

The KJV's “rib” is a translation choice supported by the bodily context. The Hebrew term has a wider range elsewhere. Neither the translation nor the broader range should be used to claim more anatomical detail than the passage provides.

### Closing is not a medical explanation

The text attributes the closing of the flesh to God. It does not explain the physiology or mechanics. A scientific account must not be invented and then presented as if Genesis supplied it.

### Adam's passivity in this event does not erase his later response

Adam sleeps during the actions in verse 21. In verse 23 he speaks. The narrative gives different roles at different points. The Witness must preserve both without making Adam the actor in God's actions or denying the response the text later attributes to him.
