# Study Record 0052 — Genesis 2:22 (KJV)

## Subject / Scripture

**Genesis 2:22 (KJV)**

> And the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman, and brought her unto the man.

## Question under examination

Who makes the woman? What is the relationship between the item taken in verse 21 and the woman made in verse 22? What does the Hebrew verb translated “made” mean? What does “from man” state? What is the significance of “brought her unto the man”? Does the verse identify the woman as the corresponding help announced in verse 18, and what does the immediate context establish? What must be reserved for Adam's speech in verse 23?

The examination must distinguish the KJV's “made” from the Hebrew verb's lexical sense, the material identified as taken in verse 21 from the woman made in verse 22, and God's making and bringing from Adam's response in verse 23.

## KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 2:18–24 (KJV)**

> 18 And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him an help meet for him.  
> 19 And out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast of the field, and every fowl of the air; and brought them unto Adam to see what he would call them: and whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof.  
> 20 And Adam gave names to all cattle, and to the fowl of the air, and to every beast of the field; but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him.  
> 21 And the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, and he slept: and he took one of his ribs, and closed up the flesh instead thereof;  
> 22 And the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman, and brought her unto the man.  
> 23 And Adam said, This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh: she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.  
> 24 Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.

Verse 18 announces God's purpose to make a help corresponding to the man. Verses 19–20 describe the bringing and naming of creatures and report that no such help was found for Adam. Verse 21 records God taking one of Adam's *ṣēlāʿ* and closing the flesh. Verse 22 now states that the LORD God makes a woman from what He had taken and brings her to the man. Verse 23 records Adam's response and wordplay between “man” and “Woman”. Verse 24 gives the statement concerning leaving, cleaving, and becoming one flesh.

This record examines verse 22 directly. It uses the surrounding verses to establish context but does not import Adam's speech from verse 23 or the joining statement from verse 24 as though either were part of verse 22.

## Direct observations

1. The LORD God is the explicit subject of the making action.
2. The object previously taken from the man is identified as the material from which the woman is made.
3. The verse repeats the relation to the prior event: “which the LORD God had taken from man”.
4. The KJV translates the main Hebrew verb as “made”.
5. The Hebrew verb is from בָּנָה (*bānâ*), commonly “build” or “construct”.
6. The result is identified as “a woman” (*ʾiššâ*).
7. The LORD God brings her to the man.
8. The verse therefore records both making and bringing, with God as the actor in both.
9. The verse does not say that Adam makes the woman.
10. The verse does not say that the woman makes or names herself.
11. The verse does not give anatomical details about how the taken *ṣēlāʿ* becomes the woman.
12. The verse does not specify the woman's age, appearance, or the duration of the making.
13. The verse does not itself quote Adam's recognition; that is in verse 23.
14. The immediate context strongly connects the woman with the corresponding help announced in verse 18, although verse 22 does not repeat the exact phrase *ʿēzer kĕnegdô*.
15. The verse does not, by itself, provide a complete definition of marriage or the “one flesh” statement in verse 24.

## Examination of the Hebrew wording

### 1. “And the LORD God” — וַיִּבֶן יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים (*wayyiben YHWH ʾĔlōhîm*)

The Hebrew begins with a wayyiqtol form of בָּנָה (*bānâ*) followed by “the LORD God”. The subject is explicit: the LORD God performs the action.

The verb *bānâ* commonly means “build” or “construct”. The KJV renders it “made” in this verse. The lexical choice is worth noting because Genesis 2:7 uses יָצַר (*yāṣar*, “formed”) for the man, and Genesis 2:19 uses the same verb for the beasts and fowl. Genesis 2:22 uses *bānâ* for the making of the woman.

This difference in verbs is a textual observation. It invites careful comparison but does not by itself establish a hidden engineering process, a hierarchy of value, or a complete architectural model.

**Direct finding:** The LORD God is said to *bānâ* the woman; the KJV renders the verb “made”.

**Not admitted:** Claiming that the verb proves a modern construction process, that the woman is less or more valuable because a different verb is used, or that the lexical difference alone establishes a GUA-W structure.

### 2. “The rib, which the LORD God had taken from man” — אֶת־הַצֵּלָע אֲשֶׁר־לָקַח מִן־הָאָדָם (*ʾet-haṣṣēlāʿ ʾăšer-lāqaḥ min-hāʾādām*)

The phrase identifies the *ṣēlāʿ* as the item God had taken from the man in verse 21. The relative clause “which the LORD God had taken” explicitly links the making in verse 22 to the prior taking.

The preposition מִן (*min*) means “from”. The phrase *min-hāʾādām* is “from the man”. It identifies the source from whom the *ṣēlāʿ* was taken; it does not say that the woman is merely a duplicate of the man or that the woman is the whole man.

The Hebrew *ṣēlāʿ* has a broader range that can include side, side-part, or rib. The KJV's “rib” is retained as the translation in this passage. The word's broader usage does not settle the exact anatomy beyond what the narrative states.

**Direct finding:** The woman is made from the *ṣēlāʿ* that the LORD God had taken from the man.

**Not admitted:** Claiming that the verse proves an entire side was taken, that the woman is a mere copy, or that the text provides a detailed anatomical account.

### 3. “Made he a woman” — בָּנָה ... לְאִשָּׁה (*bānâ ... lĕʾiššâ*)

The verb is *bānâ*, “build”. The phrase with *lĕʾiššâ* identifies the result as a woman. The KJV's idiomatic English order is “made he a woman”; the Hebrew places the verb before the subject and the result phrase.

The text does not describe intermediate steps between the taken *ṣēlāʿ* and the woman. It simply attributes the making to God and identifies the result.

The different verb from Genesis 2:7 and 2:19 should be recorded without overreading. Lexical distinction is real; an explanation for why this verb is selected requires more than the fact of the difference.

**Direct finding:** God makes/builds the woman from the item taken from the man.

**Unresolved:** The full literary significance of the choice of *bānâ* here in relation to *yāṣar* and *ʿāśâ* elsewhere in Genesis.

**Not admitted:** Treating “build” as proof that the woman is an architectural object, or claiming that the verb by itself explains her nature or status.

### 4. “And brought her unto the man” — וַיְבִאֶהָ אֶל־הָאָדָם (*wayĕbiʾehā ʾel-hāʾādām*)

The verb is from בּוֹא (*bôʾ*, “come”), in a causative Hiphil form: “bring”. The suffix “her” refers to the woman. The phrase *ʾel-hāʾādām* means “to/unto the man”.

The LORD God remains the subject. The verse records that God brings the woman to the man. This echoes the earlier scene in which God brought the creatures to Adam (verse 19), but the outcomes differ: the creatures are named, while this verse presents the woman whom God has made from the man.

The textual parallel should be noticed without claiming the two presentations are identical in purpose or status.

**Direct finding:** God brings the woman to the man.

**Not admitted:** Saying that Adam finds or creates her, or that the verse itself describes a marriage ceremony.

## The relation between verses 18, 20, 21, and 22

The sequence can be stated from the text:

1. **Verse 18 — announced purpose:** God says He will make a help corresponding to the man.
2. **Verse 20 — reported absence:** No such corresponding help is found for Adam in the preceding creature-naming episode.
3. **Verse 21 — taking:** God causes the deep sleep, takes one of Adam's *ṣēlāʿ*, and closes the flesh.
4. **Verse 22 — making and bringing:** God makes a woman from the taken *ṣēlāʿ* and brings her to the man.

The immediate context supports identifying the woman as the realization of the corresponding help announced in verse 18. The exact phrase *ʿēzer kĕnegdô* is not repeated in verse 22, so the connection is contextual and sequential rather than a direct quotation in this verse.

**Admitted:** Verse 22 is the narrative realization that follows the announced purpose and the report that no corresponding help was found among the creatures brought to Adam.

**Not admitted:** Claiming that verse 22 alone supplies every aspect of the woman's role, or that it completes the full account of recognition and joining found in verses 23–24.

## Distinctions that must be preserved

### Taking is not making

Verse 21 records the taking of the *ṣēlāʿ*. Verse 22 records the making of the woman from that which was taken. These are connected actions but remain distinct steps in the narrative.

### Making is not bringing

God makes the woman and then brings her to the man. The verse records both. Do not collapse the making and presentation into one undifferentiated action.

### “Build” is not a license for architectural invention

The verb *bānâ* means build or construct and is rendered “made” by the KJV. That lexical observation is legitimate. It does not authorize a claim that the woman is a building, that the verse encodes a modern engineering model, or that a new GUA-W layer has been demonstrated.

### Source relation is not identity collapse

The woman is made from the *ṣēlāʿ* taken from the man. The verse establishes this source relation. It does not say that the woman and man are one person, nor does it say that she is merely a duplicate. Adam's words in verse 23 and the joining statement in verse 24 must be examined in their own sequence.

### Divine bringing is not Adam's recognition

God brings the woman to the man in verse 22. Adam's speech and recognition are recorded in verse 23. The Witness must preserve the distinction between God's action and Adam's response.
