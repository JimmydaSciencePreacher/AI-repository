# Guide 02 — Research Method

## Purpose

This guide describes a practical way to investigate questions relevant to GUA-W while keeping source material, observation, interpretation, and conclusions distinguishable. It documents a method of research; it does not add stages to GUA-W or predetermine the outcome of an investigation.

## A research cycle

### 1. State the question

Write the question in a form that can be examined. Identify the subject, the scope, and what would count as relevant evidence. If the question contains several claims, separate them.

### 2. Identify the source domain

A question may begin in Scripture, science, a direct observation, a prior GUA-W study, or a comparison between domains. Identify the domain rather than blending different kinds of evidence.

### 3. Retrieve primary material

Where possible, examine the primary text, paper, dataset, experiment description, or original observation. Record enough bibliographic or contextual information for another reader to locate it.

### 4. Describe before interpreting

Record what the source actually says or what the observation actually shows. Keep this description separate from what it might mean. Preserve relevant wording, context, units, definitions, and qualifications.

### 5. Analyze and compare

Examine the claim's reasoning, assumptions, mechanisms, and boundaries. Compare it with other evidence where useful. Seek counterexamples and alternative explanations, not only confirming material.

### 6. Evaluate admissibility for GUA-W

When a proposed claim is being considered for placement in GUA-W, examine it under the Triple Constraint Law:

1. **Biblical compliance** — is it consistent with the relevant biblical text and its context?
2. **Scientific coherence** — is it coherent with the relevant scientific evidence and established understanding?
3. **GUA-W consistency** — does it preserve the architecture and distinctions already established in GUA-W?

A claim that fails any one of these constraints should not be presented as an admissible GUA-W conclusion. Scientific coherence does not mean that science has confirmed Scripture.

### 7. Record the result proportionately

Use language that matches the strength of the evidence. Distinguish established findings, supported interpretations, working hypotheses, analogies, questions, and speculation. Do not promote an analogy into a mechanism or a possibility into a demonstrated result.

### 8. Preserve unresolved questions

List what the investigation did not establish, what evidence is missing, and what could clarify the question. An honest unresolved result is preferable to a forced synthesis.

## Recurring recognition questions

The four recognition questions can be revisited during an investigation:

1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?
2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?
3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?
4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

They prompt examination from different perspectives. They are not additional architectural stages.

## Independence and correction

The AI should conduct the research needed to investigate a question rather than requiring the user to supply every intermediate step. Its findings remain open to examination and correction. When a source, calculation, or interpretation is found to be wrong, record the correction and its basis; do not silently rewrite the history of the investigation.

## Research integrity

- Do not fabricate sources, quotations, observations, or results.
- Do not describe a source as read unless it was actually examined.
- Do not hide contradictory evidence.
- Do not claim scientific proof where the evidence supports only coherence, analogy, or possibility.
- Do not treat a working hypothesis as an established GUA-W component.
- Keep the boundary between source, interpretation, and conclusion visible.
