# Guide 04 — Study Format

## Purpose

This guide provides a repeatable structure for recording investigations in `STUDIES/`. Adapt the sections to the question. A short investigation does not need empty headings, and a complex investigation may need additional sections or appendices.

## Suggested study record

# Study [number] — [descriptive title]

**Status:** Draft / In progress / Reviewed / Superseded  
**Created:** YYYY-MM-DD  
**Last updated:** YYYY-MM-DD  
**Research area:** Scripture / science / GUA-W architecture / cross-disciplinary / other

## 1. Question

State the question being investigated. Separate multiple questions when they require different evidence.

## 2. Scope

Describe what the study covers and what it does not attempt to establish.

## 3. Context

Summarize relevant prior work or GUA-W concepts. Link to the exact reference repository files, studies, or passages used.

## 4. Sources and materials

List primary and secondary sources, datasets, observations, tools, and relevant versions. Include locators that allow another reader to find the material.

## 5. Source observations

Record what the sources say or what was observed. Keep this section as descriptive as practical; do not blend conclusions into source descriptions.

## 6. Analysis

Explain the reasoning, comparisons, calculations, definitions, assumptions, and alternative explanations. Make the steps between evidence and conclusion visible.

## 7. Triple Constraint examination

Include this section when considering whether a claim belongs within GUA-W.

- **Biblical compliance:** relevant passage, context, and assessment.
- **Scientific coherence:** relevant evidence, current understanding, limits, and assessment.
- **GUA-W consistency:** relevant architectural concepts and assessment.

Record a failure or unresolved issue plainly. Do not treat a partial pass as a complete admissibility result.

## 8. Findings

Separate findings by status. For example:

- directly supported by the examined source;
- supported interpretation;
- working hypothesis;
- analogy only;
- not supported by the available evidence.

## 9. Limitations and counter-evidence

Record missing evidence, uncertainty, competing explanations, contradictory material, and limits on generalization.

## 10. Unresolved questions

List the questions that remain open and what evidence or investigation might help address them.

## 11. References

Provide complete source details and stable links where available.

## 12. Change record

Briefly record substantial revisions, corrections, or changes in the study's status.

---

## Writing standards

A study should be understandable without requiring the reader to reconstruct the conversation in which it was produced. Define specialized terms when first used or link to their definitions. Use descriptive headings. Distinguish quotations from paraphrases. Avoid overstating certainty.

## Numbering and filenames

Use sequential study numbers where practical, with a descriptive filename, for example:

`STUDIES/0001-descriptive-study-title.md`

Do not reuse a number for an unrelated study. If a study is substantially revised, retain its identity and record the revision in its change record rather than creating a duplicate without explanation.

## Handoffs

Handoffs belong in `HANDOFFS/` and use a descriptive date or sequence in the filename. A handoff records the session's actual work: sources examined, files created or changed, findings reached, unresolved matters, and the state of the work at that time. It is a historical record, not a set of rules or instructions that claims authority over future sessions.
