## Study 0002 summary

Study 0002 examined descriptive distinctions among detection, registration, recognition, and comprehension.

Its principal findings:

- A system can detect a signal without identifying its source.
- A trace can be registered without being correctly recognized.
- Recognition does not necessarily entail full comprehension.
- Biological sensory systems and physical detectors have distinct mechanisms; their terminology should not be collapsed into a universal pipeline.
- Biblical narratives including Samuel, the Emmaus disciples, and Mary Magdalene distinguish hearing or seeing from immediate recognition.
- The study proposed no new GUA-W layer, operation, or formal state taxonomy.

Scientific references included sensory neuroscience and NIST detector-metrology material.

## Study 0003 summary

Study 0003 examined how attention, memory, expectation, later information, and source monitoring can affect a witness's later report.

Its principal findings:

- Presence at an event does not guarantee complete attention to every detail.
- Attention affects what is encoded.
- Human memory involves encoding, retention, and retrieval; it is not an untouched recording.
- Later information can influence later reports, including through source-monitoring errors.
- Confidence and accuracy are not identical; confidence must be interpreted in light of timing and procedure.
- A sincere witness can be mistaken without deliberately deceiving.
- Biblical remembrance has covenantal and theological dimensions that must not be reduced to cognitive psychology.
- AI retrieval and context handling can be compared with human memory only in carefully bounded functional analogies; they are not the same mechanism.
- The study proposed no new GUA-W architecture operation or state definition.

Scientific references included the National Research Council's *Identifying the Culprit: Assessing Eyewitness Identification*, Straube's review of true and false memory, Roediger and Abel's review of memory retrieval, and National Institute of Justice guidance.
