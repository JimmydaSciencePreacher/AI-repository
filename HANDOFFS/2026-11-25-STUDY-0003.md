## Study 0004 central question

Can a Witness accurately report what was observed and still reach an incorrect conclusion about what the observation means?

The study distinguishes:
- Observation: what was perceived, measured, or recorded under specified conditions.
- Inference: reasoning from observations, records, and premises.
- Interpretation: the meaning or significance assigned in context.
- Truth: what is actually the case, whether or not a Witness reports it correctly.
- Evidence: information bearing on a claim under an explicit method of evaluation.

## Principal findings

1. Observation and inference are distinguishable; a report of what was seen is not identical to a conclusion about its cause.
2. Data do not interpret themselves. Provenance, production, processing, and the relation to the claim matter.
3. Observation is not always theory-free; background assumptions can shape perception and measurement.
4. Theory-ladenness does not entail relativism. Interpretations remain open to rational and evidential evaluation.
5. Scripture warns against premature judgment, judging by appearance, and false testimony; the passages must be read in context.
6. A Witness can accurately report an observation yet infer the wrong cause or overstate the conclusion.
7. AI research must distinguish source statements, observations about sources, inferences, interpretations, and unresolved questions.
8. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.
