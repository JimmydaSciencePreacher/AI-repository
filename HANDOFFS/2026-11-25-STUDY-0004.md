## Study 0005 central questions

Can two events or variables be consistently associated without one causing the other? How can a Witness distinguish an observed relationship from a justified causal claim?

How should scientific claims about proximate causes be distinguished from the GUA-W theological claim that God alone is Original Cause?

## Principal findings

1. Correlation alone does not establish causal direction or structure.
2. A common cause can produce an association between two variables.
3. Temporal precedence is relevant but does not, by itself, prove causation.
4. Mechanistic evidence can add explanatory detail, but proposed mechanisms also require evidence.
5. Interventions can strengthen causal inference when design and assumptions are adequate; not every question can be randomized.
6. Observational causal inference is possible but requires careful methods and explicit assumptions.
7. Not every explanation is causal; mathematical and structural explanations may answer legitimate explanatory questions.
8. A proximate physical cause and the ultimate theological source are distinct explanatory questions.
9. The theological claim of Original Cause does not replace empirical investigation of physical processes; a scientific causal model does not, by itself, identify or replace the Author.
10. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.
