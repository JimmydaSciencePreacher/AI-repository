## Central questions

What do the KJV passages state about God's relation to Creation after the initial creation account? How should creating, sustaining, governing, providence, created activity, and human responsibility be distinguished? How can scientific accounts of physical processes be kept distinct from the theological claim of Original Cause?

## Principal findings

1. Genesis 1:1 identifies God as Creator; Genesis 1 narrates divine speech and action with the realized results.
2. Genesis 2:1 declares the heavens, earth, and all their host finished. The later account of conditions, Garden placement, and human work must be read without silently making the completed whole unfinished.
3. John 1:1–3 and Colossians 1:15–17 identify the Word/Son in relation to Creation.
4. Hebrews 1:3 explicitly describes the Son as “upholding all things by the word of his power.”
5. Acts 17:24–28 describes human life and activity as dependent upon God, who gives life, breath, and all things.
6. Scripture also describes creaturely action and human responsibility, including the man's assigned work and command in Genesis 2:15–17.
7. The KJV passages examined do not provide a complete technical taxonomy of primary and secondary causes. Those terms are philosophical-theological categories and must not be misrepresented as direct biblical wording.
8. Creaturely participation does not make creatures Original Cause.
9. Scientific accounts of physical mechanisms do not, by themselves, settle the theological question of ultimate origin; theological claims do not replace empirical investigation of physical processes.
10. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.
