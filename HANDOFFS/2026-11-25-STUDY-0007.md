## Central question

If Genesis 2:1 declares the heavens, earth, and all their host finished, how should Genesis 2:4–17 be read as it describes the ground, plant growth, the Garden, man's formation, placement, and assigned work?

## Principal findings

1. Genesis 2:1–3 declares completion and God's rest. Genesis 2:4 begins the next literary section; the relationship between the accounts must be read with attention to the text and its structure.
2. Genesis 2:5 states that field plant and herb conditions are related to the absence of rain and the absence of a man to till the ground. The verse does not explicitly state that no plant life existed anywhere.
3. Genesis 2:6 describes a mist that went up from the earth and watered the whole face of the ground. The text does not call this rainfall.
4. Genesis 2:7 describes the LORD God forming man from the dust of the ground and breathing into his nostrils the breath of life.
5. Genesis 2:8–9 describes the LORD God planting the Garden in Eden, placing the man there, and causing trees to grow.
6. Genesis 2:15 explicitly assigns the man to dress and keep the Garden. The text establishes an assigned human task; it does not, by itself, define a new GUA-W runtime operation or layer.
7. Genesis 2:16–17 gives the man a command and a boundary. Work and responsibility occur within the Creator's order.
8. Plant science distinguishes seed viability, germination, seedling growth, and later plant development. Water availability, oxygen, temperature, and species-specific conditions matter; light requirements differ among species.
9. Scientific descriptions of germination and cultivation may illuminate physical processes, but they do not establish the theological meaning of Genesis or identify a mechanism for divine sustaining.
10. “Purpose” is explicit in the sense that the man is placed in the Garden with stated work. Whether Purpose is a formal bridge or placement within GUA-W remains unresolved and is not added to the architecture.
