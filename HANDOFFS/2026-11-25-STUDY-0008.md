## Central discovery

Genesis 2:5 and Genesis 2:15 use the same Hebrew root, עבד (*‘abad*), although the KJV translates it “till” in Genesis 2:5 and “dress” in Genesis 2:15.

Genesis 3:23 uses the same root again:

- Genesis 2:5 — no man yet to work/till the ground (*’adamah*).
- Genesis 2:15 — man placed in the Garden (*gan*) to work/serve it (*‘abad*) and keep/guard it (*shamar*).
- Genesis 3:23 — man sent out to work/till the ground (*’adamah*).
- Genesis 3:24 — the way to the tree of life is guarded (*shamar*).

The repeated Hebrew roots make a narrative connection visible that the English wording can obscure.

## Principal findings

1. עבד (*‘abad*, H5647) has a range including work, serve, labour, and till/cultivate. Context determines the appropriate sense.
2. שמר (*shamar*, H8104) has a range including keep, guard, watch, preserve, and observe. In Genesis 2:15 it expresses the man's responsibility to keep/guard the Garden.
3. גן (*gan*, H1588) means garden or cultivated/enclosed garden space. The word alone does not establish that Eden is the whole earth or a temple in every technical sense.
4. אדמה (*’adamah*, H127) means ground, soil, or land. Genesis relates *’adam* (man) and *’adamah* (ground) through a notable word relationship.
5. The pairing *‘abad* and *shamar* appears again in passages describing Levitical duties associated with the tabernacle and sanctuary charge (Numbers 3:7–8; 8:26; 18:5–6).
6. This canonical parallel makes a sanctuary or priestly interpretation of Eden worth investigating, but does not prove that Adam held the later Levitical office or settle the Eden-temple question.
7. Human work is present before the Fall (Genesis 2:15). Genesis 3 describes painful toil and changed conditions, not the first appearance of work.
8. The lexical and narrative pattern is a meaningful textual observation. It does not by itself establish a new GUA-W mechanism, layer, or state.
