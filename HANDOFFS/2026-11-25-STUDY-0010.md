## Central question

If Genesis 2:1–3 declares God's work finished and records His cessation on the seventh day, what is the relationship between that completed work and the man's placement and assignment in Genesis 2:8–15?

The phrase “eighth day” does not occur in Genesis 2:1–17. It is a research question and possible canonical comparison, not a day number stated by Genesis.

## Principal findings

1. Genesis 2:1 declares the heavens, earth, and all their host finished.
2. Genesis 2:2–3 describes God's cessation from His work and the blessing and sanctification of the seventh day.
3. The Hebrew roots *kalah* and *shavat* express distinct but related ideas: completion and cessation.
4. Genesis 2:4 introduces a *toledot* narrative section; it does not call the next events an eighth day.
5. Genesis 2:8 uses וַיָּשֶׂם (*vayyāśem*), from שׂוּם / שִׂים (*sum/sim*, H7760), for placing the man.
6. Genesis 2:15 uses וַיַּנִּחֵהוּ (*vayyanniḥēhû*), a Hiphil placement form. Lexical indexing is nuanced: many interlinear resources tag it under יָנַח (*yānaḥ*, H3240), while Strong's notes that Hiphil forms with the dagesh may be referred to נוּחַ (*nûaḥ*, H5117). The form supports a setting-down/placing/settling range; the precise contextual nuance should not be overstated.
7. The different Hebrew placement forms are obscured by the KJV's use of “put” in both verses.
8. This lexical distinction does not establish that Adam entered the Garden on the seventh or eighth day.
9. Genesis distinguishes God's creative work from the man's assigned Garden work.
10. Later explicit eighth-day passages occur in priestly inauguration (Leviticus 9:1 after seven days in Leviticus 8), feast observance (Leviticus 23:36, 39), temple dedication (2 Chronicles 7:8–10), and covenantal practice (Genesis 17:12; Leviticus 12:3).
11. These later passages merit canonical comparison, but the number eight alone does not establish one universal symbolic meaning or a chronology for Genesis 2.
12. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Important lexical correction/qualification

Do not simply state that Genesis 2:15 is unambiguously “from H5117” without noting the indexing nuance. The Hebrew form is וַיַּנִּחֵהוּ. Sources tag it as H3240 (יָנַח); Strong's entry itself includes a note that the Hiphil forms with the dagesh may be referred to H5117 (נוּחַ). Report the morphology and the lexical note transparently. The KJV “put” is contextually sound; the range includes set down/place/allow to remain, with a possible settle/rest nuance.
