## Central question

Who originates the Temple's pattern, what role do David and Solomon receive, and what does the account distinguish between completion of the building and the manifestation of God's glory?

## Principal findings

1. David desires to build a house for the LORD, but the LORD redirects the plan (2 Samuel 7; 1 Chronicles 17).
2. The LORD promises David a house in the dynastic sense; the passage's use of *bayit* must be read contextually and not collapsed into the physical Temple.
3. David prepares materials and resources, but Solomon receives the building assignment (1 Chronicles 22; 28–29).
4. 1 Chronicles 28:11–19 explicitly attributes the Temple pattern to divine disclosure received by David and transmitted to Solomon.
5. Solomon receives the assignment and builds; the narrative does not present him as the originating source of the pattern.
6. Construction includes skilled labor, material planning, and international cooperation.
7. Completion of the building and furnishings is reported before the ark is brought in and the dedication occurs.
8. The ark links the Temple to the covenant made with Israel at Horeb.
9. 1 Kings 8:10–11 records the cloud and glory of the LORD filling the house.
10. Solomon explicitly says that heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain God, much less the house he has built (1 Kings 8:27).
11. 2 Chronicles 7:1–3 records fire consuming the offerings and the glory of the LORD filling the house.
12. The eighth-day assembly in 2 Chronicles 7:8–10 belongs to the Temple dedication and feast chronology; it does not establish a universal symbolic meaning for eight.
13. The Temple has meaningful correspondences with the Tabernacle and Creation, while remaining a distinct historical and covenantal structure.
14. Later biblical history prevents Solomon's Temple from being treated as the final, permanent form of divine dwelling.
15. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Important distinctions preserved

- **David's desire versus divine authorization:** A human intention does not override the LORD's word.
- **David's preparation versus Solomon's construction:** The tasks and assignments are distinct.
- **Pattern versus builder:** 1 Chronicles 28 attributes the pattern to divine disclosure; Solomon is its appointed executor.
- **Completion versus dedication:** The building and furnishings are reported complete before the ark's placement and dedication.
- **Structure versus God:** Solomon explicitly denies that God can be contained by the Temple (1 Kings 8:27).
- **Human work versus divine glory:** The people build; the glory is the LORD's.
- **Different glory scenes:** 1 Kings 8 and 2 Chronicles 7 give complementary but distinguishable descriptions.
- **Eighth-day context:** The eighth-day assembly belongs to the dedication feast; no universal numerical doctrine is inferred.
- **Sanctuary identities:** Eden, Tabernacle, Temple, Christ, and New Jerusalem remain distinct, even when canonical relationships are examined.
