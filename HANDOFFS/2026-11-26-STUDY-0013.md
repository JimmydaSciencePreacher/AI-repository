## Central question

What does Scripture say was given to David as the Temple's pattern, how does that pattern relate to the construction descriptions in Kings and Chronicles, and what can be concluded without claiming more than the text establishes?

## Principal findings

1. 1 Chronicles 28:11–18 describes a pattern that includes architectural spaces, service arrangements, vessels, and measured materials.
2. In 1 Chronicles 28:19, David attributes his understanding of the written pattern to the LORD.
3. The exact grammatical force of “by his hand upon me” should not be expanded beyond what the KJV wording and Hebrew evidence establish.
4. The Hebrew noun תַּבְנִית (*tabnît*, H8403) has a range that includes pattern, plan, form, construction, figure, likeness, and similitude.
5. The same Hebrew noun is used in the Tabernacle instructions and the Temple pattern account, but shared vocabulary does not make the structures identical.
6. 2 Chronicles 3:3 describes Solomon as instructed for the building of the house of God.
7. 1 Kings 6–7 and 2 Chronicles 3–4 provide detailed records of the Temple's construction, furnishings, and equipment.
8. Kings and Chronicles agree on important principal dimensions and many structural features.
9. The accounts also contain numerical differences, including the porch, pillars, and molten sea capacity, which this study does not resolve.
10. The distinction between pattern, reception, transmission, instruction, execution, and record is supported by the passages examined.
11. The evidence does not establish a complete modern architectural blueprint or a universal pattern-to-structure mechanism.
12. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Important distinctions preserved

- **Origin versus reception:** David attributes the pattern to the LORD; David is its recipient.
- **Reception versus transmission:** David receives and transmits the pattern to Solomon.
- **Pattern versus construction record:** The pattern passage and the later construction descriptions have related but distinct functions.
- **Architecture versus service:** The pattern includes worship arrangements and vessels, not only building spaces.
- **Shared term versus identical structure:** *Tabnît* appears in both Tabernacle and Temple contexts; this does not make the structures identical.
- **Parallel agreement versus numerical difference:** Kings and Chronicles must be compared without erasing differences or inventing harmonizations.
- **Construction versus divine presence:** Physical completion does not mechanically cause or contain God's glory.
