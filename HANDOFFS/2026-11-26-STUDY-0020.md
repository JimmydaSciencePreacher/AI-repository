## Central question

What is the relationship between the new heart, the new spirit, and God's Spirit in Ezekiel 36:24–28?

## Principal findings

1. Ezekiel 36:16–23 provides the context: Israel's uncleanness, scattering, and the profaning of God's holy name.
2. The LORD explicitly says He acts for His holy name's sake, not because of Israel's merit.
3. Ezekiel 36:24–28 describes gathering, cleansing, a new heart, a new spirit, God's Spirit within, enabled obedience, and restored covenant life.
4. “A new heart” and “a new spirit” are gifts attributed to God in verse 26.
5. “A new spirit” (*ruach chadashah*) and “my spirit” (*ruchi*) share the Hebrew noun *ruach*, but their modifiers and contextual roles differ.
6. Verse 27 explicitly connects the LORD's Spirit within the people with His causing them to walk in His statutes and keep His judgments.
7. “Heart of flesh” is a metaphorical contrast with the stony heart in the context of inward responsiveness, not a claim about literal organ replacement.
8. Deuteronomy 30 and Jeremiah 31 provide important canonical parallels concerning God's inward work, covenant, love, obedience, and the law written in the heart.
9. Ezekiel 37 continues the restoration theme and explicitly identifies the bones as the whole house of Israel.
10. John 3 and Romans 8 provide New Testament teaching concerning birth and life by the Spirit; their contexts should not be collapsed into Ezekiel's immediate historical-prophetic setting.
11. Genesis 2:7 concerns the creation of man as a living soul; Ezekiel 36 concerns promised covenant cleansing and inward renewal. They are comparable but not identical events.
12. The complete doctrinal relation between the renewed human spirit and the indwelling Spirit of God requires further canonical study.
13. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Important distinctions preserved

- God's action is for His holy name's sake (Ezekiel 36:22–23), not Israel's merit.
- Ezekiel 36:26 says “a new heart” and “a new spirit”; verse 27 says “my spirit.”
- Both spirit expressions use the Hebrew noun *ruach*, but their modifiers and contextual roles differ.
- Verse 27 explicitly connects the LORD's Spirit within with the LORD causing covenant obedience.
- “Heart of flesh” is a contextual metaphor, not literal organ replacement.
- Ezekiel 36 concerns Israel's covenant restoration; Genesis 2:7 concerns man's creation as a living soul.
- John 3 and Romans 8 provide canonical comparisons, but the contexts are not identical.
- The complete doctrine of the human spirit and the indwelling Spirit is not settled by Ezekiel 36 alone.
