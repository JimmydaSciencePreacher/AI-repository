## Principal findings

1. Hebrew `mot tamut` emphatically announces death as certain; it does not grammatically mean two separate deaths.
2. The contextual force of `beyom` in Genesis 2:17 is debated and cannot be settled by assertion.
3. Genesis records immediate consequences after eating: changed awareness, fear, hiding, judgment, and expulsion.
4. Genesis 3:22–24 connects exclusion from the tree of life with preventing the man from eating and living for ever.
5. Genesis 3:19 announces return to the ground; Genesis 5:5 records Adam's eventual bodily death.
6. Genesis does not explicitly say Adam had eaten from the tree of life before the Fall or that he was biologically immortal before the Fall.
7. Romans 5 and 1 Corinthians 15 connect Adam's transgression with death's entrance/reign and the resurrection hope through Christ.
8. “Spiritual death” / broken communion is a supported theological interpretation of the immediate consequences, but it is not a technical phrase explicitly defined in Genesis 2:17.
9. The bounded synthesis relates immediate alienation and exclusion from the tree of life to eventual bodily death without reducing the warning to only one dimension.
10. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Boundaries preserved

- Do not claim *mot tamut* itself means two deaths.
- Do not treat *beyom* as unambiguously a strict 24-hour reference or as unambiguously “a thousand years.”
- Do not assert Adam's biological immortality as an explicit Genesis statement.
- Do not assert Adam ate from the tree of life before the Fall.
- Distinguish explicit Genesis statements from canonical theological synthesis.
