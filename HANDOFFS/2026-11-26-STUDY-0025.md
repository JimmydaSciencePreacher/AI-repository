## Principal findings

1. Genesis 2:9 explicitly places the tree of life in the Garden before the Fall.
2. Genesis 2:16 grants permission to eat from every tree; Genesis 2:17 identifies the tree of knowledge as the stated exception. The tree of life therefore appears to be included in the permission.
3. Permission is not proof of actual consumption. Genesis does not narrate Adam or Eve eating from the tree of life before the transgression.
4. Genesis 3:22 presents the possibility of the man taking and eating from the tree of life and living for ever.
5. The Hebrew *gam* (“also”) is best read as an additive comparison with the prior taking/eating from the tree of knowledge, not as proof of a prior eating from the tree of life.
6. The prospective concern introduced by *pen* (“lest”), together with the narrative sequence, supports the reading that God prevents a new act of eating from the tree of life.
7. Some scholars propose prior, continuing consumption; that alternative is recorded and evaluated rather than suppressed.
8. Mettinger's proposal that the tree's significance may have been unknown to the human pair and may have functioned as a reward is an inference, not an explicit statement.
9. Genesis 3:23–24 records expulsion and the guarding of the way to the tree of life. It does not say the tree itself was destroyed.
10. Genesis connects eating from the tree with living for ever but does not specify whether one or repeated eating is required or explain a mechanism.
11. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Key distinctions preserved

- Presence is not permission.
- Permission is not actual consumption.
- Physical availability is not proof that the humans understood the tree's significance.
- “Take also from the tree of life” is not equivalent to “take again from the tree of life.”
- A guarded way is not a destroyed tree.
- Explicit text, grammatical inference, and theological interpretation remain distinct.
