## Principal findings

1. Romans 5:12 explicitly says sin entered the world through one man, death through sin, and death spread to all people.
2. The KJV “for that” translates `eph’ hō`; the major readings are causal (“because”), consecutive/resultative (“with the result that”), and relative (“in whom” or a related construction).
3. The causal reading is dominant in many modern translations. Fitzmyer and Dunson defend a consecutive/resultative reading. The relative reading has a major history in the Western tradition through the Latin `in quo`, but that Latin rendering does not settle the Greek syntax.
4. The aorist `hēmarton` alone does not prove that all people performed Adam's act simultaneously.
5. Romans 5:13–14 says sin was in the world before the Mosaic law and death reigned from Adam to Moses, including over those who did not sin in the same manner as Adam.
6. Romans 5:15–19 repeatedly attributes death, condemnation, and the many being made sinners to the one man's transgression and disobedience.
7. Paul holds Adam's representative consequence and the universal reality of human sin together. They should not be treated as mutually exclusive explanations.
8. The passage does not, by itself, specify every mechanism by which Adam's disobedience affects each descendant.
9. The Adam–Christ contrast is central; Christ's gift is greater than Adam's offence.
10. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Boundaries preserved

- Do not treat the disputed phrase as grammatically settled.
- Do not make the aorist verb alone prove simultaneous participation in Adam's act.
- Do not reduce Paul's argument to individual sins alone; verses 15–19 retain Adam's representative consequence.
- Do not erase personal sin and responsibility.
- Do not use the Latin Vulgate as if it independently proves the Greek syntax.
- Do not turn a grammatical interpretation into a complete doctrine of original sin without further evidence.
- Keep the Adam–Christ comparison central.
