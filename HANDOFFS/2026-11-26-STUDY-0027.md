## Principal findings

1. Romans 5:19 uses two passive forms of `καθίστημι` (*kathistēmi*): `κατεστάθησαν` (aorist passive, “were made/constituted”) and `κατασταθήσονται` (future passive, “will be made/constituted”).
2. The verb has a range including set, place, appoint, establish, and constitute. Its meaning in Romans 5:19 must be determined from context, not a single dictionary gloss.
3. The clauses are deliberately parallel: through the disobedience of one, the many were constituted sinners; through the obedience of the One, the many will be constituted righteous.
4. The passive forms do not, by themselves, identify the ultimate agent or explain the complete mechanism by which the result applies to the many.
5. Romans 5:16–18 supplies explicit judicial language: judgment, condemnation, justification, and gift. Therefore, verse 19 cannot be reduced to inward moral transformation alone.
6. The paragraph also speaks of death, life, reign, grace, and righteousness. Therefore, a purely forensic reading that excludes changed life and conduct is also too narrow.
7. An imitation-only reading does not account for Paul's claims about condemnation and justification arising from the acts of the one.
8. “Constituted” is a defensible explanatory rendering, but it does not settle imputation, participation, inherited condition, or the exact mechanism of Adamic consequence.
9. The future tense in the Christ clause must be retained and examined alongside Paul's present language about justification and the gift of righteousness.
10. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Boundaries preserved

- Do not let a lexical gloss replace contextual exegesis.
- Do not treat the passive voice as a complete explanation of agency or mechanism.
- Do not reduce the Adam–Christ parallel to imitation alone.
- Do not reduce “made sinners / made righteous” to inward moral transformation alone.
- Do not reduce the whole paragraph to legal status while excluding the reign of grace, life, and resulting conduct.
- Do not make the future tense mean that righteousness has no present reality.
- Do not claim that the verse alone settles imputation, inherited guilt, inherited corruption, or the scope of “many.”
- Preserve Paul's asymmetry: grace abounds “much more.”
