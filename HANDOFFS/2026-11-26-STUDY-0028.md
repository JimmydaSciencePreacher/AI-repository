## Principal findings

1. Romans 5:19 uses **κατασταθήσονται** (*katastathēsontai*), future passive indicative, third person plural.
2. The future wording must be preserved; the form alone does not identify a detailed chronology or a single eschatological event.
3. Romans 5:1 and 5:9 speak of present justification, while Romans 5:9–10 also speak of future salvation. Present justification and future salvation are not contradictory in Paul's argument.
4. Romans 6 joins present participation in Christ and newness of life with future likeness to His resurrection.
5. Romans 8 and 1 Corinthians 15 establish a clear future horizon of glory, bodily redemption, and resurrection.
6. Commentators differ: the future may emphasize final consummation, the continuing application of Christ's work as people believe, or the certainty of the promised result.
7. An exclusively final-judgment reading is possible but not proven by the verb alone. An exclusively distributive or gnomic reading is likewise not established by morphology alone.
8. “Made righteous” in Romans 5:19 must not be collapsed into “made alive” in 1 Corinthians 15:22; the passages use different verbs and address related but distinguishable claims.
9. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Boundaries preserved

- Do not erase the future tense.
- Do not use the future tense to deny present justification.
- Do not use present justification to deny future completion.
- Do not equate justification, sanctification, glorification, and resurrection without examining their contexts.
- Do not treat a canonical parallel as identical wording or mechanism.
- Do not claim that the future tense alone proves a detailed end-times chronology.
- Preserve competing interpretations and unresolved scope questions.
