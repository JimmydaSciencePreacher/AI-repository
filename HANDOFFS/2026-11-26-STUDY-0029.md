## Principal findings

1. Romans 5:9–10 repeats the future passive verb **σωθησόμεθα** (*sōthēsometha*), “we shall be saved.”
2. Romans 5:9 places “now justified” alongside “we shall be saved.”
3. Romans 5:10 places reconciliation through the Son's death alongside future salvation “in/by his life.”
4. Romans 5:11 says reconciliation has now been received.
5. The phrase “in/by his life” refers to the living Christ; sources differ on whether its emphasis is His exalted intercession, resurrection life shared with believers, sustaining power, final completion, or a combination.
6. Romans 5:9–10 and 5:19 are related within Paul's larger argument, but they use different Greek verbs and different grammatical subjects.
7. “Saved” and “made/constituted righteous” should not be treated as exact synonyms without further argument.
8. The scope relation among “we,” “many,” and “all men” remains unresolved.
9. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Boundaries preserved

- Preserve the difference between justification, reconciliation, and salvation.
- Preserve the contrast between the Son's death and His life without inventing a mechanism.
- Do not equate *sōzō* and *kathistēmi* merely because both occur in future passive forms.
- Do not settle the scope of “we,” “many,” and “all” prematurely.
- Do not convert a commentator's theological proposal into a grammatical fact.
