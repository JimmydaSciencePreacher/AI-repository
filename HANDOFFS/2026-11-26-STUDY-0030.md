## Principal findings

1. Romans 6:1–2 responds directly to Romans 5:20–21; grace does not authorize continuing in sin.
2. Romans 6:3–4 describes baptism into Christ's death and burial with Him, with the purpose that believers walk in newness of life.
3. Romans 6:5 connects union in the likeness of Christ's death with the likeness of His resurrection. Its future verb has competing interpretations: logical/gnomic, eschatological, or a relation including both.
4. Romans 6:8 more explicitly expresses future hope: “we believe that we shall also live with him.”
5. Romans 6:9–10 describes Christ's resurrection as decisive and permanent: He dies no more and lives unto God.
6. Romans 6:11–14 applies this reality to present conduct: reckon yourselves alive to God, do not let sin reign, and present yourselves to God.
7. Romans 6 clarifies that salvation through the risen Christ includes present new life and future life with Him. These dimensions are related but not identical.
8. The believer's present moral life is not the same event as final bodily resurrection.
9. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Boundaries preserved

- Do not force one temporal meaning onto every future verb in Romans 6.
- Do not claim the believer's body has already undergone final resurrection.
- Do not claim believers are already incapable of sin.
- Do not treat baptism as an independently originating source of life.
- Do not reduce future resurrection to present ethical conduct.
- Do not infer a physical/scientific mechanism from theological language.
