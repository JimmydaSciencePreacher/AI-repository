## Principal findings

1. The former priests were many because death prevented them from continuing; Christ continues ever and has a priesthood that does not pass to another.
2. Hebrews 7:25 explicitly says Christ is able to save those who come to God through Him because He ever lives to make intercession for them.
3. The phrase εἰς τὸ παντελές (*eis to panteles*) is strongly argued by Meyer and Alford to mean completely or entirely rather than simply “forever”; the enduring priesthood context supplies the permanent horizon.
4. Hebrews 7:27 says Christ offered Himself once for all. Continuing intercession is not a repeated sacrifice.
5. Romans 8:34 similarly joins Christ's death, resurrection, exaltation, and intercession.
6. Hebrews 7:25 clarifies an important dimension of Romans 5:10's “saved by his life,” but does not exhaust every possible aspect of the phrase.
7. Romans 6:9–10 emphasizes Christ's permanent resurrection life; Hebrews 7 emphasizes the priestly and intercessory significance of that life.
8. The exact mode of heavenly intercession is not revealed.
9. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Boundaries preserved

- Do not treat continuing intercession as a repeated sacrifice.
- Do not suggest Christ's once-for-all offering is incomplete.
- Do not reduce “to the uttermost” to duration alone when lexical evidence favors completeness.
- Do not claim the exact mechanism of intercession is disclosed.
- Do not equate every expression in Romans and Hebrews.
