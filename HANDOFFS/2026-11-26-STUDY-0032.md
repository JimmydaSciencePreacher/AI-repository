## Principal findings

1. Romans 8:31–39 draws assurance from the preceding argument of Romans 8:28–30 concerning God's purpose, calling, justification, and glorification.
2. “Who can be against us?” does not mean believers have no opponents; it asks whether opposition can overturn God's saving purpose.
3. God's giving of His Son is Paul's ground for confidence that He will complete His saving purpose. “All things” must be read in that context, not as a guarantee of every desired temporal possession.
4. Paul answers accusation by declaring that God justifies; he answers condemnation by pointing to Christ's death, resurrection, exaltation, and intercession.
5. Romans 8:34 is directly related to Hebrews 7:25 and the continuing intercession of the risen Christ, as studied in 0031.
6. Romans 8:35–36 names real suffering and quotes Psalm 44:22. Suffering and lament are not denied or treated as proof of divine abandonment.
7. “In all these things” believers are more than conquerors through the One who loved them; victory is not immunity from affliction.
8. Romans 8:38–39 declares that no created power or reality can separate those addressed from God's love in Christ Jesus.
9. The precise identity of every cosmic power and the full extent of debated doctrinal questions are not settled here.
10. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Boundaries preserved

- Do not equate divine assurance with absence of suffering.
- Do not turn “all things” into a guarantee of prosperity or every temporal desire.
- Do not treat lament as unbelief.
- Do not claim every suffering event has an explanation available to the sufferer.
- Do not claim “more than conquerors” means worldly success or immunity from death.
- Do not use this passage alone to settle every debate about election or the extent of the atonement.
