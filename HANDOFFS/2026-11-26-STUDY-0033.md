## Principal findings

1. Romans 8:9–10 describes the Spirit's present indwelling and the believer's continuing bodily mortality.
2. Romans 8:11 connects the Spirit of the God who raised Jesus with God's future giving of life to believers' mortal bodies.
3. The future bodily-resurrection reading best accounts for “shall quicken your mortal bodies,” especially when read alongside Romans 8:23's “redemption of our body.”
4. A present spiritual-renewal reading is represented in the Cambridge Greek Testament Commentary and is retained as a genuine counter-reading. The study concludes that present Spirit-enabled life and future bodily resurrection are related but distinct.
5. The final phrase in Romans 8:11 has a textual/grammatical translation issue: “by his Spirit” versus “because of his Spirit.” The KJV is retained as the primary text and the variation recorded.
6. First Corinthians 15 identifies Christ as firstfruits and describes the resurrection body's transformation from corruption, dishonour, weakness, and natural life to incorruption, glory, power, and spiritual life.
7. “Spiritual body” should not be casually equated with a non-body. The study finds the Spirit-characterized/Spirit-empowered bodily reading better supported by the examined scholarship, while recording debate.
8. Paul's seed analogy illustrates continuity and transformation; it does not provide a physical mechanism for resurrection.
9. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Boundaries preserved

- Do not collapse present indwelling into the future resurrection event.
- Do not read “spiritual body” as automatically meaning immaterial or non-bodily.
- Do not equate resurrection with ordinary biological germination.
- Do not promote the seed analogy into a mechanism.
