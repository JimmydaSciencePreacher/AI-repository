## Principal findings

1. The treasure in 2 Corinthians 4:7 is closely connected to the gospel and the knowledge of God's glory in Christ in 4:4–6; the exact referent is not reduced to a single technical definition.
2. The earthen vessel describes human fragility and weakness; it does not establish that material existence or the body is intrinsically evil.
3. The surpassing power belongs to God, not the human minister.
4. The hardship contrasts in 4:8–9 acknowledge real suffering; they do not promise immunity from injury, persecution, death, or emotional distress.
5. “The dying of the Lord Jesus” describes Paul's ongoing participation in the pattern of Christ's suffering in ministry, not a second or supplementary atonement.
6. The life of Jesus is manifested in mortal flesh in the present (4:10–11).
7. “Death worketh in us, but life in you” (4:12) describes the cost borne by apostolic ministers and the life received by the Corinthian community through their ministry; the messenger is not the originating source.
8. Paul's faith leads him to speak (4:13), echoing Psalm 116:10 in KJV numbering.
9. God who raised Jesus will also raise Paul and present him with the believers (4:14).
10. Present manifestation of Jesus' life and future bodily resurrection are connected but distinct.
11. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Boundaries preserved

- Do not interpret hardship contrasts as a guarantee of earthly invulnerability.
- Do not treat Paul's suffering as a second atoning sacrifice.
- Do not treat the body as intrinsically evil because it is described as an earthen vessel.
- Do not confuse the present manifestation of Jesus' life with future bodily resurrection.
- Do not treat faith as the originating cause of resurrection.
- Do not claim that the passage describes a scientific mechanism for resurrection.
