## Principal findings

1. The four Gospels report healings, exorcisms, raisings of the dead, provision miracles, and works over nature.
2. The Gospel writers connect Jesus' works with His identity and mission, the Father's action, compassion, and the Kingdom of God.
3. Matthew 10:1–8 records a direct commission to the Twelve, including the command: “Freely ye have received, freely give.”
4. Acts describes healings and signs performed in Jesus' name; Peter explicitly refuses to attribute the lame man's healing to his own power or holiness (Acts 3:12).
5. Acts 19:11 attributes special miracles to God working through Paul's hands; Acts 19:13–17 warns against treating Jesus' name as a manipulable formula.
6. 1 Corinthians 12 describes differentiated gifts distributed by the Spirit; it does not say every believer has every gift.
7. Love, edification, order, and discernment govern the use of gifts (1 Corinthians 13–14).
8. Deuteronomy 13 and Matthew 7 warn that signs alone do not establish that a messenger is approved by God.
9. Irenaeus provides second-century testimony that Christians reported healing and exorcism in Jesus' name and understood such gifts as freely received.
10. Historical scholarship can examine early reports, source relationships, context, and Jesus' reputation as healer and exorcist. It cannot experimentally verify every supernatural claim.
11. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Questions carried forward

1. How should “greater works than these” in John 14:12 be read in the context of Jesus' departure and the coming of the Spirit?
2. How do the signs in John's Gospel relate to the healing and exorcism narratives in the Synoptic Gospels?
3. How should the signs and wonders in Acts be compared with the gifts and operations described in 1 Corinthians 12–14?
4. What is the relationship between healing, forgiveness, repentance, and the Kingdom in individual Gospel accounts?
5. How should James 5:13–18 be read alongside medical care and the differentiated gifts of 1 Corinthians 12?
6. How did healing and exorcism practices develop in the first five centuries, and how did early Christian writers distinguish prayer from magic?
7. What criteria should be used to assess modern miracle claims while preserving openness to God's action and protection against exploitation?
8. How do warnings about false signs relate to the testing of spirits in 1 John 4:1–6?
9. How does Christ's resurrection differ from the raisings of the dead during His ministry and in Acts, particularly regarding finality and resurrection life?
