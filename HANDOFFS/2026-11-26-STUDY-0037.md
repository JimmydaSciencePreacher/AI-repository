## Principal findings in the existing Study 0037

1. The prophetic texts anticipate God's promise to pour out and place His Spirit among His people (Ezekiel 36–37; Joel 2).
2. Jesus promises the Comforter; John identifies the Comforter as the Holy Ghost (John 14:16–17, 26).
3. The Father gives the promised Spirit at the Son's request; the risen and exalted Christ pours forth the gift (John 14:16; Acts 2:33).
4. Acts 1:8 connects the Spirit's power with witness to Jesus and the mission's geographical expansion.
5. The Holy Ghost is presented as personally active and divine, not merely as an impersonal force or a possession controlled by a believer.
6. The Gift of the Holy Ghost and the differentiated gifts distributed by the Spirit are related but distinct categories.
7. 1 Corinthians 12 describes diverse gifts distributed as the Spirit wills for the common good; not every believer has every gift.
8. Acts records different sequences of Spirit reception in Jerusalem, Samaria, Saul's experience, Cornelius's household, and Ephesus. These accounts should not be forced into a single mechanical chronology.
9. The relationship between John 20:22 and Pentecost in Acts 2 remains debated.
10. The Spirit's work includes new life, indwelling, adoption, sanctification, prayer, understanding, gifts, witness, and resurrection hope.
11. Gifts are not proof of superior holiness or moral maturity; love, fruit, discernment, and order remain essential.
12. The Spirit cannot be bought, commanded, manipulated, or treated as a technique.
13. No GUA-W architectural change is proposed.

## Important interpretive boundaries

- Do not flatten Father, Son, and Holy Ghost into one another or diminish the Spirit's divine and personal identity.
- Do not reduce the Holy Ghost to a created instrument or impersonal force.
- Do not claim every believer must manifest the same gift or speak in tongues as a universal proof without addressing the full biblical and interpretive record.
- Do not turn Acts into a mechanical formula for every believer and community.
- Do not confuse the Gift of the Spirit with the several gifts distributed by the Spirit.
- Do not equate giftedness with moral superiority.
- Do not claim scientific instruments can verify the Spirit's divine identity or agency.
- Do not advise abandoning appropriate medical care.
- No new GUA-W layer, operation, state, or definition.

## Governing foundation

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

God alone is Original Cause. Everything else is derivative witness. The derivative cannot become the Source or alter the Source.

The Holy Ghost must not be described as merely a created instrument; the biblical text attributes divine identity and personal agency to Him. The repository's Source/Witness distinction must not erase the distinctions among Father, Son, and Holy Ghost.
