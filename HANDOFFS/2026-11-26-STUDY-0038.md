## Research question

What is the biblical relationship between Jesus' command, “freely ye have received, freely give” (Matthew 10:8), and Peter's declaration, “such as I have give I thee: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk” (Acts 3:6)?

## Principal findings

1. Matthew 10:1–8 records Jesus giving the Twelve authority and commanding them to give freely what they had freely received.
2. Matthew 10:10 immediately recognizes provision for the worker. Therefore “freely give” does not mean all material support for ministry is forbidden.
3. Acts 3:6 records Peter saying “such as I have give I thee” and commanding the lame man to rise in the name of Jesus Christ.
4. Acts 3:12 is an essential interpretive control: Peter rejects the idea that the healing was accomplished by his own power or holiness.
5. Acts 3:16 attributes the man's strength to the name of Jesus and faith in His name.
6. Acts 4:7–12 and 4:33 keep the witness focused on the risen Jesus Christ.
7. Acts 8:18–24 directly rejects Simon's attempt to purchase the gift of God.
8. 2 Kings 5:15–27 condemns Gehazi's deception and personal enrichment connected with Naaman's healing.
9. Acts 19:11–17 distinguishes God's works through Paul from an attempt to use Jesus' name as a formula.
10. 1 Corinthians 12:7, 11 says the Spirit distributes gifts as He wills for the common good.
11. 1 Peter 4:10–11 describes received gifts as stewardship to be ministered to others, with glory to God through Jesus Christ.
12. The biblical distinction is between selling or exploiting a divine benefit and receiving legitimate ordinary support for ministry.
13. The user's wording is a synthesis of Matthew 10:8 and Acts 3:6, not a verbatim quotation of one verse.
14. No new GUA-W layer, operation, state, or definition is proposed.
