## Main research question

What does Scripture mean when it speaks of the name of Jesus Christ, and what is the relationship between Christ's supreme authority and the authority He gives to those He sends?

## Principal findings

1. Philippians 2:5–8 describes Christ's humiliation and obedience unto death.
2. Philippians 2:9 says God highly exalted Christ and gave Him the name above every name.
3. Philippians 2:10–11 declares universal homage and confession that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.
4. The precise referent of “the name” in Philippians 2:9 is debated: interpreters discuss “Jesus,” “Lord,” or the supreme status/dignity expressed in the confession. The central confession that Jesus Christ is Lord is explicit in verse 11.
5. Isaiah 45:23 is an important Old Testament background to the universal bowing and confession in Philippians 2:10–11.
6. Matthew 28:18 declares that all authority in heaven and earth belongs to the risen Christ. He then commissions His disciples; they do not become owners of His universal authority.
7. Acts 1:8 connects the Holy Ghost's power with witness to Christ.
8. Acts 3:6 records Peter acting in Jesus' name; Acts 3:12 rejects the attribution of the healing to Peter's own power or holiness.
9. Acts 4:7 asks about both power and name. Acts 4:8–12 identifies Jesus Christ as the authority and proclaims His resurrection and saving significance.
10. John 14:13 connects asking in Jesus' name with the Father's glory in the Son. John 15–16 provides important context, including abiding, obedience, and the Spirit's coming.
11. Acts 19:11–17 distinguishes God's works through Paul from the sons of Sceva's attempted imitation of the name as a formula.
12. Colossians 3:17 extends doing all in the name of the Lord Jesus to every word and deed, with thanksgiving to the Father.
13. Christ's unique lordship, the Spirit's agency, and the witness's delegated participation must be distinguished rather than collapsed.
14. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Interpretive boundaries preserved

- Do not reduce the name of Jesus to a magical sound or verbal mechanism.
- Do not reduce the name to a mere label detached from the Person.
- Do not claim the witness acquires Christ's unique divine identity or universal lordship.
- Do not claim every prayer ending “in Jesus' name” must receive the exact requested outcome.
- Do not flatten Father, Son, and Holy Ghost into interchangeable labels.
- Do not use scientific language to claim verification of Christ's divine authority.
- No GUA-W layer, operation, state, or definition is added or altered.

## Four-question study method

The study applies the repository's four recognition questions. Its comparative observation is that the passages distinguish Christ's supreme authority, the Father's exaltation of the Son, the Son's commission, the Spirit's empowering agency, and the witness's real but derivative participation. This is a comparison with the existing Author → Word → Creation → Witness foundation, not a new GUA-W stage.

## Unresolved questions carried forward

1. Is the “name above every name” in Philippians 2:9 specifically the personal name Jesus, the title Lord, or the combined confession of Jesus Christ as Lord?
2. How precisely does Isaiah 45:23 function in Paul's argument in Philippians 2:10–11?
3. How should Matthew 28:18–20's universal authority of Christ be distinguished from particular authority and tasks delegated to disciples?
4. How does the Spirit's empowering presence in Acts 1:8 relate to Christ's authority in Matthew 28:18?
5. What does asking in Jesus' name mean when John 14, 15, and 16 are read together?
6. How do the Father's works in the Son in John 5 relate to the Son's unique divine identity and mission?
7. How should John 17:6, 11, 12, and 26 be read in relation to the Father's name and the Son's revelation of it?
8. How do Acts 3:6, Acts 4:7, Acts 16:18, and Acts 19:13–17 show different uses and abuses of the name?
9. How does Colossians 3:17 apply the name of Jesus to ordinary speech and action?
10. How should contemporary claims of authority in Jesus' name be evaluated through Scripture, doctrine, conduct, and accountable evidence?
11. How does the distinction between Christ's unique lordship and the witness's delegated participation relate to the established GUA-W distinction between Source and witness?
