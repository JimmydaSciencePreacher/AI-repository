## Main research question

When Jesus Christ performs a miraculous work, what does the Gospel account reveal about Jesus, the Father who sent Him, the people who receive the work, and the purpose of the event?

## Primary Scripture — KJV

- Matthew 8:1–4 — cleansing of the leper.
- Mark 2:1–12 — healing of the paralytic and authority to forgive sins.
- Luke 7:11–17 — raising of the widow's son at Nain.
- John 5:1–18 — healing at Bethesda.
- John 9:1–41 — healing of the man born blind.
- John 11:1–44 — raising of Lazarus.
- John 20:30–31 — stated purpose of John's selected signs.
- Contextual passages include Exodus 4:1–9; 1 Kings 17:8–24; 2 Kings 4:18–37; Isaiah 35:3–6 and 61:1–3; Matthew 11:2–6; Luke 4:16–21; John 5:19–30; John 10:25–38; John 12:23–43; Romans 6:9; and 1 Corinthians 15:12–28.

## Principal findings

1. Matthew 8:1–4 presents Jesus' willingness and authority to cleanse the leper, followed by instruction to seek priestly recognition.
2. Mark 2:1–12 connects the visible healing of the paralytic with Jesus' declared authority to forgive sins. Mark says Jesus saw their faith, referring to those who brought the man.
3. Luke 7:11–17 explicitly says Jesus had compassion on the widow and raised her son; no request from the widow is recorded.
4. John 5:1–18 presents Jesus healing a man who describes his inability to reach the pool. The ensuing Sabbath dispute leads to Jesus' statement, “My Father worketh hitherto, and I work” (John 5:17).
5. John 9:1–41 rejects the disciples' proposed attribution of the man's congenital blindness to his or his parents' sin. The healed man becomes a witness whose understanding develops through the narrative.
6. John 11:1–44 connects Jesus' love for the family, His delay, His tears, the Father's glory, His identity as resurrection and life, and Lazarus' restoration.
7. Lazarus' return to mortal life is distinguished from Jesus' resurrection, which the New Testament presents as death's defeat and the firstfruits of resurrection.
8. John 20:30–31 states that selected signs are written so readers may believe Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life through His name.
9. The selected narratives do not support a single mechanical formula requiring every recipient to request a miracle or demonstrate faith in the same way.
10. The signs reveal different dimensions of Jesus' identity, authority, compassion, relation to the Father, and mission. They do not automatically compel every observer to believe.
11. John 9 must not be used to assign blame for disability; John 11 must not be used to claim that every illness or bereavement has the same divinely disclosed purpose.
12. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.

## Interpretive boundaries preserved

- Do not claim every miracle has one identical purpose.
- Do not make the recipient's faith a universal mechanical prerequisite or claim lack of healing proves lack of faith.
- Do not assign personal or parental guilt for illness or disability where Scripture does not.
- Do not treat Lazarus' restoration as identical to Jesus' resurrection.
- Do not claim Gospel accounts are modern clinical records.
- Do not claim historical or scientific methods alone settle the metaphysical question of divine action.
- Preserve the unique identity of the Son and His relation to the Father; do not reduce Jesus to a created witness or ordinary commissioned instrument.
- No GUA-W layer, operation, state, or definition is added or altered.

## Four-question study method

The study applies the repository's four recognition questions. Its comparative observation is that the selected accounts distinguish Jesus as the acting subject, the works He performs, the people who receive or witness them, and the testimony and responses that follow. The comparison preserves the unique identity of the Son and His relation to the Father; it is not a new GUA-W stage.

## Unresolved questions carried forward

1. How does Matthew's cleansing account relate to Leviticus 13–14?
2. How does Mark's ordering of forgiveness before healing shape the account's meaning?
3. How does Luke's use of Elijah and Elisha traditions contribute to the identity question in Luke 7?
4. What is the exact relationship between Bethesda and the Father's work in John 5:17–30?
5. How should John 9:3 be translated and punctuated, and what does this mean for the relation between suffering and God's works?
6. How does the healed man's developing testimony model witness under opposition?
7. How should Jesus' delay in John 11:5–6 be read alongside His love for the family?
8. What is the relation between Jesus' tears, compassion, and authority over death?
9. How does Lazarus' raising lead to the plot against Jesus?
10. How does John 20:30–31 define the relation between signs, belief, Jesus' identity, and life?
11. How do the miracles relate to Isaiah 35 and 61?
12. How should testimony about a miracle be distinguished from proof of a minister's character or doctrine?
13. How can historical research evaluate testimony without pretending to settle every theological or metaphysical question?
