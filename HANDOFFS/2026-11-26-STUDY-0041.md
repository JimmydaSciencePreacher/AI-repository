## Principal findings

1. “In the name of Jesus” cannot be reduced to pronouncing a sound or using a verbal formula. The name identifies Jesus Christ Himself; context may emphasize identity, authority, representation, commission, or allegiance.
2. The Old Testament's messenger/prophet pattern provides a useful background for representation under another's authority, but a messenger does not become the sender.
3. Jesus' relation to the Father is unique. John 5:19–30 and 6:38 show the Son's works and mission in perfect unity with the Father's will; this is not simply identical to an apostle's delegated commission.
4. Acts 3:6, 3:12, and 3:16 are central: Peter acts in Jesus' name, explicitly rejects attribution to his own power or holiness, and directs attention to Jesus.
5. Acts 4:7–12 connects the name with the risen Jesus and salvation, not merely with a healing formula.
6. Acts 19:11–20 contrasts God's work through Paul with the sons of Sceva's attempted use of Jesus' name as a technique.
7. Matthew 7:21–23 warns that claims of works in Jesus' name do not by themselves establish faithful obedience or relationship to Christ.
8. John 14–15 connects asking in Jesus' name with abiding in Christ, His words, obedience, love, and the Father's glory.
9. Colossians 3:17 broadens acting in Jesus' name to every word and deed, not only miraculous acts.
10. 1 John 5:14 explicitly qualifies prayer by asking according to God's will.
11. Philippians 2:12–13 holds together the believer's real command to act and God's work in believers both to will and to do.
12. Ezekiel 36:26–27 and Romans 8:12–14 connect inward renewal and Spirit-enabled obedience with real human action.
13. Romans 9:16 denies that God's mercy originates in human willing or exertion, within Paul's wider argument about promise and calling.
14. The user's statement may be faithfully clarified as: “I do not need to govern myself as though I were my own source. I have the Will of God as my Guide.” This is a devotional paraphrase of the user's meaning, not a biblical quotation.
15. Scripture does not establish that the human capacity to will, desire, respond, and act disappears. It does support examining the difference between having a will and treating oneself as the independent governing authority.
16. No GUA-W architectural change is proposed.

### Findings and boundaries
1. The Potter/clay, earthen-vessel/treasure, and temple/indwelling-Spirit images are related but distinct metaphors; do not collapse them into one technical definition.
2. 2 Corinthians 4:7 explicitly states the purpose: the excellency of the power is of God, not the human vessel.
3. The New Testament explicitly teaches the indwelling Holy Ghost (e.g. 1 Corinthians 6:19; Romans 8:9–11).
4. Ephesians 2:1–10 gives the sequence: former deadness in trespasses, God's mercy, made alive with Christ, salvation by grace rather than works, no boasting, and creation in Christ unto good works.
5. The former sinful condition is not made good and God is not identified as its author. God's redemptive purpose overcomes and redeems the condition; do not infer that God authored sin or directly caused every specific act of brokenness.
6. 2 Corinthians 12:9–10 teaches Christ's strength manifested in Paul's weakness; do not universalize it into a claim that every illness or affliction was specifically sent to make someone useful.
7. 2 Timothy 2:20–21 adds holiness and readiness for the Master's use; the vessel image is not an excuse to treat conduct as irrelevant.
8. Synthesis: the vessel is not the Potter, the treasure, or the power; glory belongs to God.
9. The vessel imagery connects with acting in Jesus' name: receive rather than claim the source, serve rather than self-exalt, obey rather than invent authority, give rather than boast, and direct glory to God.

2. John 17 connects believers' unity in God with love, mission, and witness to the world.
3. John 15 says branches bear fruit by abiding in the vine; the branch does not originate the life.
4. Colossians 1:27–29 joins “Christ in you” with Paul's real labour, performed according to Christ's working in him.
5. The compact synthesis is: “The life is received; the fruit is borne; the glory is attributed to God.”
