## Scope and principal content

The study:
- Examines John 6:35–45 and 6:60–71, especially verses 37, 39, 40, 44, 45, 63, and 65.
- Reads “No man can come to me, except the Father ... draw him” in its discourse context.
- Traces the Father's giving, drawing, and teaching; the person's hearing, learning, coming, and believing; and the Son's receiving, keeping, and raising.
- Examines Greek terms for ability (dynamai), drawing (helko / helkyō), and being taught by God, without treating a lexical gloss as sufficient to settle all theological debates.
- Compares John 6:44–45 with 1 Corinthians 2:10–16 while keeping their distinct contexts visible.
- Records the natural-law keyhole boundary: the Father's drawing is a spiritual/theological claim in this passage, not a described physical mechanism or identified natural law.
- Treats GUA-W mappings as provisional correspondences only. No architecture change is proposed.

## Principal findings

1. John 6:44 explicitly says no one can come to Christ unless the Father draws him.
2. John 6:65 repeats that coming depends upon what is given by the Father.
3. John 6:45 connects the Father's drawing with being taught by God, hearing, and learning, after which the person comes to Christ.
4. John 6:37 connects the Father's giving with coming to the Son and the Son's promise not to cast out the one who comes.
5. John 6:39–40 and 44 connect the Son's work with raising those given to Him / those who come at the last day.
6. The text includes real human response—hearing, learning, coming, and believing—without presenting that response as independently originating the divine initiative.
7. The passage does not disclose a physical mechanism for divine drawing and does not, by itself, settle every debate about resistance, the precise operation of grace, or the chronology of salvation.
8. No GUA-W architecture change was made.

## Triple Constraint disposition

The Father's initiative and the dependence of coming to Christ upon His drawing and teaching are direct textual findings. Human response is also directly present in the passage. A physical or natural-law mechanism for the Father's drawing is not stated and must not be invented. The study therefore preserves a distinction between physical processes that may be investigated scientifically and divine spiritual action described by Scripture.
