## Scope and principal content

The study:
- Examines John 1:9–14, especially verses 12–13, in the full Prologue context.
- Distinguishes receiving Christ and believing in His name (verse 12) from the source of birth as God's children (verse 13).
- Examines the Greek terms *lambanō*, *pisteuō eis*, *exousia*, *tekna theou*, and *gennaō* with context controlling the lexical observations.
- Examines the plural reading of John 1:13, followed by a textual-critical note on the singular variant and why the plural reading is retained in the study.
- Compares John 1:12–13 with John 3, John 6, 1 Corinthians 2, James 1, 1 Peter 1, and 1 John 5.
- Preserves the distinction between divine origin and real human response.
- States that John 1:12–13 alone does not settle the precise chronology of regeneration and faith.
- Preserves the natural-law keyhole boundary: biological birth can be investigated physically, but spiritual birth is attributed to God and is not described as a measurable biological mechanism.
- Proposes no GUA-W architecture change.

## Principal findings

1. John 1:12 names a real human response: receiving Christ and believing in His name.
2. Christ gives the recipients the right/authority to become children of God.
3. John 1:13 attributes the birth of these children to God, not ancestry, fleshly will, or human will.
4. The plural reading of verse 13 refers to the believers described in verse 12; it is not a statement that Christ was born in the same way as believers.
5. The grammar supports divine origin but does not, by tense alone, establish a complete chronological sequence between regeneration and faith.
6. The person receives; Christ gives; God is the source of the birth; the witness testifies.
7. No GUA-W architecture change was made.

## Triple Constraint disposition

The direct textual findings support both receiving/believing and the divine origin of birth as God's children. Human will is not named as the source of this birth. The study rejects the claim that human response is unreal, and also rejects turning biological reproduction or natural laws into an explanation of spiritual birth. It does not claim that science confirms Scripture or that GUA-W can model or reproduce regeneration.
