# Scripture Placement 0001 — Genesis 1:1

**Study:** Separate Scripture Placement  
**Record:** 0001  
**Starting Scripture:** Genesis 1:1 (KJV)

## Scripture

> In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.

## Immediate observation

The verse names God as the acting subject and states that He created the heaven and the earth. The verse itself does not use the word “Word,” name the Son, describe a physical mechanism, or state a technical sequence. Those distinctions must be preserved when other passages are brought alongside it.

## What the relevant GUA-W sources connect to Genesis 1:1

The current GUA-W Genesis 1 material places the opening declaration within the relationship **Author → Word → Creation → Witness**. In that discussion, it identifies three canonical passages that speak explicitly about the Word/Son in relation to Creation:

1. **John 1:1–3**
2. **Colossians 1:16–17**
3. **Hebrews 1:1–3**

The three passages are companion witnesses. The GUA-W sources do not prescribe an order among them, so this record does not create one.

## Four distinctions preserved

- **Genesis 1:1:** God created the heaven and the earth.
- **John 1:1–3:** the Word was in the beginning; all things were made by Him.
- **Colossians 1:16–17:** all things were created by Christ and for Him; He is before all things, and by Him all things consist.
- **Hebrews 1:1–3:** God made the worlds by the Son; the Son upholds all things by the word of His power.

The GUA-W synthesis relates these statements. This record does not attribute the full wording of the later passages to Genesis 1:1 itself.

## Triple Constraint

- **Biblical compliance:** The verse and companion passages are kept distinct and identified by their actual references.
- **Scientific coherence:** No scientific mechanism or empirical conclusion is asserted.
- **GUA-W consistency:** The recorded relationship follows the Author–Word–Creation–Witness framework without adding an architectural stage or independent cause.

## Current study position

Genesis 1:1 is the starting piece. Its GUA-W companion group is John 1:1–3, Colossians 1:16–17, and Hebrews 1:1–3. These are examined together in Placement 0002. The sources do not state which of the three must be read first.

## Limits and open questions

1. Genesis 1:1 does not itself name the Word; the explicit identification is supplied by the companion passages.
2. The three companion passages have distinct contexts and emphases.
3. No scientific mechanism is inferred from the verse.
4. No order among the three companion passages is asserted.
