## 3. Romans 11:36 — GUA-W directs the reader to keep Romans 9–11 together

Romans 11:36 is named in *The Answer Before the Question* as a witness that all things are of God, through God, and to God.

GUA-W's *Human Will and Salvation Reference Work* explicitly warns that **Romans 9–11 must be read as one connected argument**:

- **Romans 9** addresses God's purpose, mercy, election, Pharaoh, hardening, the potter and clay, and the calling of Jews and Gentiles.
- **Romans 10** addresses Christ, the word of faith, believing, confessing, calling, hearing, and preaching.
- **Romans 11** continues with Israel, the Gentiles, mercy, the remnant, and the warning against boasting.

The GUA-W cross-reference table in the *Human Will and Salvation Internal Unification Synthesis* explicitly connects **Romans 9–11** with:

- **Genesis 25**
- **Malachi 1**
- **Exodus 9**
- **Exodus 33**
- **Romans 10**
- **Ephesians 1–2**

These are therefore added as further passages for examination along the Romans 11:36 branch. Romans 10 is not detached from Romans 9 or Romans 11. The Genesis 25, Malachi 1, Exodus 9, and Exodus 33 references are retained because GUA-W's own cross-reference register names them.

**Important boundary:** These references do not all state the same proposition. Their contexts and wording must be examined separately. In particular, the GUA-W record preserves grammatical qualifications in Romans 9:22–23 and does not use Romans 9–11 to erase the real proclamation and response described in Romans 10.

## 4. Ephesians 1:9–11 — GUA-W expands the passage to its full context and connected witnesses

The source records do not leave Ephesians 1:9–11 isolated. The *Internal Unification Map and Research Agenda* and Chapter 15 identify the broader unit **Ephesians 1:3–14**.

### 4.1 Directly connected passages concerning prior purpose

Chapter 15 explicitly compares Ephesians 1:3–14 with:

- **1 Peter 1:19–20** — Christ foreordained before the foundation of the world and manifested in the last times.
- **2 Timothy 1:9** — God saves and calls according to His purpose and grace given in Christ Jesus before the world began.
- **Titus 1:2** — the promise of eternal life before the world began.
- **John 17:5, 24** — the Son's glory with the Father before the world was and the Father's love for Him before the foundation of the world.
- **John 1:1–3** — the Word in the beginning and all things made by Him.
- **Hebrews 1:1–3** — God made the worlds by the Son, who upholds all things.
- **Colossians 1:15–17** — all things created by and for the Son and held together in Him.

The *Internal Unification Map* records this as a canonical comparison of God's Will/Purpose and the Word. It expressly warns against turning the relationship into a temporal sequence of separate causes. These passages are related witnesses, not interchangeable statements.

### 4.2 The Father gives; the Son receives and keeps

Chapter 15 then identifies a connected set of passages:

- **John 6:37–40**, with **John 6:44, 65** — the Father gives and draws; the Son receives, keeps, and raises those given to Him.
- **John 10:27–30** — the sheep hear and follow; the Son gives eternal life and keeps them.
- **John 17:1–26**, especially **John 17:2, 5–6, 9, 20, 24** — the Son's given work, the people given Him, His keeping, and those who will believe through the disciples' word.

The GUA-W record directs that these passages be read in their own contexts. “Given,” “drawn,” “come,” “hear,” “believe,” and “follow” are not to be treated as technical synonyms.

### 4.3 The Book of Life branch

Chapter 15 explicitly brings the following passages into the comparison:

- **Revelation 17:8**
- **Revelation 13:8**
- **Revelation 21:27**
- **Luke 10:20**
- **Philippians 4:3**
- **Hebrews 12:23**

The source preserves a specific grammatical caution: Revelation 17:8 directly associates the foundation-of-the-world phrase with the names not written in the Book of Life; Revelation 13:8 has a disputed syntactic attachment and must not be cited as though that question were settled. The passages are not collapsed.

### 4.4 The Son's saving work

Chapter 15 further names:

- **John 3:16–18**
- **Romans 5:6–11**
- **Romans 8:31–34**
- **Ephesians 1:7**
- **Colossians 1:13–22**
- **Hebrews 1:1–3**

These passages are connected in the source's examination of the Father's purpose and the Son's saving work. They retain their distinct testimony concerning the giving of the Son, redemption, reconciliation, the Cross, Resurrection, and the Son's sustaining relation to Creation.

These are recorded as a network of connected passages. They are not a single chronological formula. The source preserves both divine initiative and the real acts of hearing, attending, repenting, coming, believing, obeying, and doing.

## 5. The completion and consummation branch is also expanded

The *Creation–Redemption–Consummation Internal Unification Synthesis* further connects the three completion witnesses to additional passages:

- **Genesis 2:1–3** — Creation completion.
- **John 19:28–30** — Christ's declaration in the Passion context.
- **Revelation 21:1–6** and **Revelation 22:1–5** — the new heaven and earth, God's dwelling with His people, the end of the curse, and the tree of life.
- **John 17:4–5** — the Son speaks of the work given Him by the Father and of the glory He had with the Father.
- **1 Corinthians 15:21–23, 45–49** — Adam and Christ, death and resurrection, and Christ as the last Adam.
- **Romans 4:25** and **1 Corinthians 15:3–20** — the Resurrection in the apostolic Gospel and its significance.
- **Romans 8:18–23** — the future liberation of Creation.
- **Luke 24:50–53** and **Acts 1:1–11** — the Ascension accounts, with their distinct narrative details.
- **Acts 2:32–36** and **Ephesians 1:19–23** — Resurrection, exaltation, and Christ's heavenly position.
- **Hebrews 1:3**, **Hebrews 7:25**, and **Romans 8:34** — the Son's exaltation and continuing intercession.
- **Hebrews 1:10–12** — the Son's enduring identity in contrast with the created heavens and earth.
- **Hebrews 11:3** and **Hebrews 12:26** — creation by God's word and the promised future shaking, with the source preserving a distinction between what each passage actually says.
- **Revelation 16:17** — another occurrence of the wording “It is done,” which must not be confused with the distinct declaration in Revelation 21:6.

The synthesis explicitly states that Hebrews 1:3 is not a fourth completion. It concerns the Son's upholding of all things. John 17:4 is not a fourth completion either; it is a witness to the Father's commission and the Son's accomplishment before the Passion declaration in John 19:30.

## 6. Isaiah 46:9–10 and Revelation 4:11 — current source boundary

*The Answer Before the Question* names Isaiah 46:9–10 and Revelation 4:11 alongside Romans 11:36 and Ephesians 1:9–11 as complementary witnesses concerning God's authorship, will, purpose, and relation to all that exists.

In the GUA-W records examined during this pass, these two passages are named as anchors, but no further passage-by-passage chain is explicitly assigned to either one in those particular sections. They remain active branches for further source examination. No next verse is invented on their behalf.
