# Scripture Placement 0004 — John 14:6: The Way, the Truth, and the Life

**Study:** Separate Scripture Placement  
**Record:** 0004  
**Scripture examined:** John 14:6 (KJV)

## Scripture

> Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.

## Why this passage is examined here

Placement 0002 records John 14:6 as a distinct Scriptural identification of Christ named in GUA-W. The current GUA-W completed architecture, §7, identifies Christ as “the Word made flesh, the Way, the Truth, and the Life” and cites John 1:1–14 and John 14:6. Chapter 1, “One Author, One Word, One Creation,” also names John 14:6 alongside John 1:14 and 1 Corinthians 15:45–49.

John 14:6 is therefore a source-supported passage for examination after John 1:14. This relationship does not mean the GUA-W sources prescribe a universal verse-by-verse sequence.

## Immediate biblical context: John 14:1–11

The setting is Jesus speaking to His disciples:

- **Verse 1:** Jesus tells the disciples not to let their hearts be troubled and calls them to believe in God and in Him.
- **Verses 2–3:** He speaks of His Father's house, the many mansions, His going to prepare a place, and His promise to come again and receive them unto Himself.
- **Verse 4:** He tells them that they know where He is going and the way.
- **Verse 5:** Thomas says they do not know where Jesus is going and asks how they can know the way.
- **Verse 6:** Jesus answers, identifying Himself as the Way, the Truth, and the Life, and states that no man comes unto the Father but by Him.
- **Verse 7:** Jesus speaks of knowing Him and knowing His Father.
- **Verses 8–9:** Philip asks Jesus to show them the Father. Jesus answers by pointing to His presence with them and asking whether Philip has known Him.
- **Verses 10–11:** Jesus speaks of His relation to the Father and calls the disciples to believe that the Father is in Him and He in the Father, with His words and works bearing witness.

The immediate question is Thomas's question about the way to where Jesus is going. Jesus' answer is not merely a set of directions: He identifies Himself as the Way.

## What the verse states

The verse directly records three self-identifications by Jesus:

1. **The Way**
2. **The Truth**
3. **The Life**

It then states an exclusive relation: no man comes unto the Father but by Jesus.

The three terms belong together in Jesus' answer. This placement does not separate them into three independent routes or reduce them to abstract technical labels. The verse identifies Jesus Himself as the Way, the Truth, and the Life.

## Relationship to John 1:14 and the GUA-W record

John 1:14 identifies the Word made flesh, who dwelt among us and was full of grace and truth. John 14:6 records Jesus' own declaration that He is the Way, the Truth, and the Life. The two passages are related by their identification of Christ, while retaining their different settings and statements.

The current GUA-W material explicitly places these identifications within its account of Christ:
- GUA-W-COMPLETED-ARCHITECTURE.md, §7, “The Genesis 1–2 / Genesis 3 comparison.”
- book/01-ONE-AUTHOR-ONE-WORD-ONE-CREATION.md, section “The Word is not an invention of the witness.”

The same GUA-W sources distinguish Christ from GUA-W as the map/name of the architecture. John 14:6 is a statement about who Jesus is and how man comes to the Father; it is not a claim that Christ is merely a component or label in an architecture.

## The Father, the Son, and the witness of words and works

John 14:7–11 continues the subject of verse 6. Philip asks to see the Father; Jesus directs attention to Himself and speaks of the Father dwelling in Him and doing the works. The passage therefore connects the way to the Father with Jesus' identity and His relation to the Father.

The text also provides a useful distinction for the study's witness theme: Jesus' words and works are presented as testimony concerning His relation to the Father. This observation is limited to the immediate passage; it does not by itself establish a complete doctrine of witness or authorize a technical mapping of every phrase to a GUA-W stage.

## Triple Constraint

- **Biblical compliance:** John 14:6 is read as Jesus' answer to Thomas within John 14:1–11. The exclusive statement concerning access to the Father is retained, and the three self-identifications are kept together.
- **Scientific coherence:** This is a theological and canonical examination. It proposes no physical mechanism and makes no empirical scientific claim.
- **GUA-W consistency:** The passage agrees with the GUA-W source record identifying Christ as the Way, the Truth, and the Life. Christ is not conflated with GUA-W as a descriptive map of Creation.

## Limits and unresolved questions

1. This placement records the immediate context and the GUA-W relationship; it does not attempt to exhaust the meaning of “the Way,” “the Truth,” or “the Life.”
2. The relation between John 14:6 and John 1:14 is a canonical connection, not a claim that the two verses have identical purposes or wording.
3. John 14:7–11 raises further questions about the relation of the Son and the Father that require careful examination in the wider Gospel context.
4. GUA-W also names 1 Corinthians 15:45–49 as a distinct identification of Christ as the last Adam. This record does not decide whether that passage must be the next placement.

## Current study position

The study has examined Genesis 1:1 and its three companion passages, then John 1:14, and now John 14:6. The current GUA-W sources also explicitly identify 1 Corinthians 15:45–49 as a distinct Scriptural description of Christ. Its context and the related Adam–Christ passages recorded in Placement 0002 remain to be examined; no further sequence is asserted here.
