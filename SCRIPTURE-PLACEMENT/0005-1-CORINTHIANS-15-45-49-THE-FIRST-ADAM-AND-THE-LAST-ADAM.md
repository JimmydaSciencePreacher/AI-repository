# Scripture Placement 0005 — 1 Corinthians 15:45–49: The First Adam and the Last Adam

**Study:** Separate Scripture Placement  
**Record:** 0005  
**Scripture examined:** 1 Corinthians 15:45–49 (KJV)

## Scripture

> And so it is written, The first man Adam was made a living soul; the last Adam was made a quickening spirit.  
> Howbeit that was not first which is spiritual, but that which is natural; and afterward that which is spiritual.  
> The first man is of the earth, earthy: the second man is the Lord from heaven.  
> As is the earthy, such are they also that are earthy: and as is the heavenly, such are they also that are heavenly.  
> And as we have borne the image of the earthy, we shall also bear the image of the heavenly.

## Immediate biblical context: 1 Corinthians 15:12–58

Paul's subject throughout this section is the resurrection of the dead and the resurrection of Christ.

- **Verses 12–19:** Paul addresses the claim that there is no resurrection of the dead. He sets out the consequences if Christ has not risen: apostolic preaching and faith would be vain, the witnesses would be false, believers would remain in their sins, and those fallen asleep in Christ would have perished.
- **Verses 20–23:** Paul declares that Christ is risen from the dead and become the firstfruits of them that slept. He places death in relation to the first man and resurrection of the dead in relation to a man, Christ; each is associated with his proper order.
- **Verses 24–28:** Paul speaks of the end, the putting down of rule and authority, the subjection of all things, and the Son's relation to the Father. The passage must retain its own statements and order.
- **Verses 29–34:** Paul continues arguing from the reality and consequence of resurrection, including the danger and cost of the apostolic witness, and calls the Corinthians to sobriety and righteousness.
- **Verses 35–44:** Paul addresses how the dead are raised and with what body they come. He uses the sowing of a seed and the differences among fleshly and heavenly bodies to distinguish the body sown from the body raised. He speaks of corruption and incorruption, dishonour and glory, weakness and power, and a natural body and a spiritual body.
- **Verses 45–49:** Paul cites the first man Adam and contrasts him with the last Adam; he also contrasts the first man, earthy, with the second man, the Lord from heaven. He relates the image borne from the earthy to the image of the heavenly.
- **Verses 50–58:** Paul continues with the inheritance of the kingdom of God, the mystery that not all shall sleep but all shall be changed, the incorruptible putting on incorruption, the mortal putting on immortality, the defeat of death, and steadfastness in the Lord's work.

The immediate context therefore governs the passage: verses 45–49 are part of Paul's sustained explanation of resurrection, not an isolated naming exercise detached from the chapter's argument.

## What the passage directly states

The passage directly states:

1. **The first man Adam** was made a living soul.
2. **The last Adam** was made a quickening spirit.
3. Paul distinguishes what is natural and what is spiritual, stating that the natural was first and afterward that which is spiritual.
4. **The first man** is of the earth, earthy.
5. **The second man** is the Lord from heaven.
6. Paul relates the image of the earthy to those who are earthy and the image of the heavenly to those who are heavenly.
7. Paul says that, as believers have borne the image of the earthy, they shall also bear the image of the heavenly.

The passage uses both “last Adam” and “second man.” These expressions occur within Paul's argument and should be preserved as written. The passage identifies the second man as “the Lord from heaven”; it does not present Adam and Christ as equivalent or competing original causes.

The GUA-W completed architecture places Christ's identification as the last Adam within its Genesis 1–2 / Genesis 3 comparison. This is a recorded canonical relationship, not a new GUA-W layer or a claim that the architecture's terminology replaces Paul's wording.

The GUA-W synthesis in **CREATION-REDEMPTION-CONSUMMATION-INTERNAL-UNIFICATION-SYNTHESIS.md, §6** specifically relates:

- Christ as the last Adam (1 Corinthians 15:45–49);

This relationship is consistent with the chapter's own progression: Paul first declares Christ risen and firstfruits (verses 20–23), then addresses the nature of the resurrection body (verses 35–44), and then uses Adam and Christ in verses 45–49 as part of that explanation.

## What is directly stated, and what is not added

**Directly stated by Paul:** Adam is the first man; Christ is identified as the last Adam and the second man, the Lord from heaven; the passage contrasts the earthy and the heavenly and speaks of bearing the image of each.

**Recorded by GUA-W:** This passage is a Scriptural identification of Christ as the last Adam and belongs to the canonical relationship between Adam, Christ, death, and resurrection.

**Not asserted by this placement:** It does not invent a physical mechanism for resurrection; it does not make “natural” and “spiritual” synonyms for unrelated technical categories; it does not collapse the first Adam and the last Adam into one person; and it does not claim that every phrase in Paul's argument is a one-to-one label for a GUA-W architectural stage.

## Triple Constraint

- **Biblical compliance:** The passage is read within 1 Corinthians 15:12–58. Its Adam/Christ contrast remains within Paul's argument about resurrection. The wording “last Adam” and “second man” is retained, and the distinction between the earthy and the heavenly is not erased.
- **Scientific coherence:** This is a theological and canonical examination. The passage does not provide a detailed physical mechanism for resurrection, and this placement does not invent one or claim scientific confirmation.
- **GUA-W consistency:** The placement follows the documented relationship identifying Christ as the last Adam while preserving God as Original Cause and the distinction between the originating Word, Creation, and derivative witness. No new architectural layer, runtime stage, or independent cause is proposed.

## Limits and unresolved questions

1. The full meaning and scope of “quickening spirit” must remain tied to Paul's argument and the wider canonical context; this placement does not reduce the phrase to a technical mechanism.
2. The distinction between the natural body and the spiritual body in verses 42–49 deserves continued examination in its own context.
3. The relationship between the first Adam's disobedience and Christ's obedience is also treated in Romans 5:12–21, which remains a separately recorded GUA-W connection and has not been placed by this record.
4. The relationship between Christ as firstfruits (verses 20–23), the last Adam (verses 45–49), and the transformation described in verses 50–58 is recorded here from the chapter's progression; further detail must be examined from the text rather than assumed.
5. This placement does not establish a mandatory next passage among the remaining GUA-W cross-references.
