## 3. Psalm 19:1–4 — the governing witness group identified by GUA-W

Chapter 3, under **“Theology and the ultimate WHY,”** explicitly lists these governing Scriptural witnesses:

- **Genesis 1–2**
- **John 1:1–3**
- **Colossians 1:16–17**
- **Hebrews 1:1–3**
- **Romans 11:36**
- **Psalm 19:1–4**
- **Revelation 4:11**

The chapter says these passages must be read in their own contexts. Together they direct the inquiry to God as Creator, the Word in relation to Creation, and the purpose and end of what God has made.

### 3.1 Further Scriptures recorded from this source

The additional passages identified alongside Psalm 19:1–4 are therefore:

- **Genesis 1–2** — the foundational account of Creation.
- **John 1:1–3** — the Word in the beginning and the creation of all things through Him.
- **Colossians 1:16–17** — all things created by and for Christ, and their continuance in Him.
- **Hebrews 1:1–3** — God made the worlds by the Son; the Son upholds all things.
- **Romans 11:36** — all things are of Him, through Him, and to Him.
- **Revelation 4:11** — all things were created by God's will and for His pleasure.

These are **companion governing witnesses in one GUA-W list**. The Chapter 3 source does not say that each is a direct quotation from Psalm 19, nor does it establish a verse-by-verse chain from Psalm 19 to each of them. Their placement together is explicit; a stronger cross-reference claim is not.

### 3.2 What Psalm 19 itself contributes

Psalm 19:1–4 speaks of the heavens declaring God's glory and the firmament showing His handiwork. Its day-and-night language presents a continuing, universal testimony through the created order.

The source placement supports reading Psalm 19 within the Creator/Creation witness group. It does not authorize turning the psalm's poetic language into a scientific mechanism, or assuming that every observer interprets the testimony correctly.

### 3.3 Source boundary

The Chapter 3 source names the governing group but does not provide a further Psalm 19-specific cross-reference chain beyond that list. The branch remains open for further source examination. No additional verse is supplied merely from a resemblance in wording.

## 4. Isaiah 48:12–17 — the Isaiah 40–48 branch

The authoritative study **Human Will, Creaturely Capacity, and the Creator's Authority — Job 38–41, Isaiah 40–48, Colossians 2:8, and Romans 1** examines Isaiah 40–48 as a connected witness to the Creator/creature distinction.

Its section 4.6 specifically identifies:

- **Isaiah 48:12–13** — the LORD is the first and the last; His hand laid the foundation of the earth and His right hand spread out the heavens.
- **Isaiah 48:17** — the LORD is Israel's Redeemer, the One who teaches them to profit and leads them in the way they should go.

The source does not isolate these verses from the larger Isaiah argument.

### 4.1 Wider Isaiah passages explicitly retained by GUA-W

The same GUA-W investigation names the following contextual units and passages:

- **Isaiah 40:12–14, 18–20, 21–31** — the incomparable Creator, the heavens, the created host, and the everlasting LORD.
- **Isaiah 41:1–7, 8–29** — the challenge to rival claims and the contrast between the LORD and powerless idols.
- **Isaiah 42:5, 8** — the LORD as Creator and His declaration that He will not give His glory to another or His praise to graven images.
- **Isaiah 43:10–13** — the LORD's uniqueness and Israel called as a witness.
- **Isaiah 44:6–20** — the first and the last, the challenge to rival claims, and the maker of an idol who mistakes a creaturely product for a god.
- **Isaiah 45:7–12, 18–23** — the LORD's sovereignty, the potter and clay, the Maker of the earth and Creator of man, and the call to look unto Him and be saved.
- **Isaiah 46:1–11** — the contrast between idols that must be carried and the LORD who carries His people, declares the end from the beginning, and whose counsel stands.
- **Isaiah 48:12–13, 17** — the first and the last, the Creator of heaven and earth, and Israel's Redeemer and Teacher.

These references preserve the source's wider Isaiah 40–48 context. They are not presented as a newly invented linear sequence.

### 4.2 Further canonical passages explicitly named in the same GUA-W investigation

The Isaiah study itself identifies these additional Scriptural connections:

- **Job 38–41** — the Creator's questions concerning the foundations, boundaries, governance, and creatures of Creation.
- **Romans 1:23–25**, read within **Romans 1:18–32** — the explicit comparison to Isaiah 44's critique of the creature being treated as the Creator.
- **Colossians 1:15–20** — Christ as the image of the invisible God and the One by whom and for whom all things were created, in whom all things consist.
- **Colossians 2:3** — in Christ are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.
- **Colossians 2:6–15**, especially **Colossians 2:8** — the warning against being taken captive by teaching not according to Christ.

The Isaiah study also names Genesis 1–3 as its foundational narrative and identifies **Isaiah 40:1–48:22** among its primary Scriptural sources.

### 4.3 The precise relationship of these passages

The source explicitly says that Isaiah 44's critique closely parallels Romans 1:23–25. This is a stated canonical comparison.

The source also examines Colossians 2:6–15 and recalls Colossians 1:15–20 and 2:3. These are connected within its examination of knowledge, the Creator/creature distinction, and Christ. They are not all direct quotations or direct cross-references to Isaiah 48:12–17.

Job 38–41 and Isaiah 40–48 are examined together as a wider Creator/creature witness. Their genres, arguments, and immediate contexts remain distinct.
