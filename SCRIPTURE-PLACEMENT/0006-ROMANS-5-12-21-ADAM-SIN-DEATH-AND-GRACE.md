# Scripture Placement 0006 — Romans 5:12–21: Adam, Sin, Death, and the Grace of Jesus Christ

**Study:** Separate Scripture Placement  
**Record:** 0006  
**Scripture examined:** Romans 5:12–21 (KJV)

## Scripture

> Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:
>
> (For until the law sin was in the world: but sin is not imputed when there is no law.
>
> Nevertheless death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over them that had not sinned after the similitude of Adam's transgression, who is the figure of him that was to come.
>
> But not as the offence, so also is the free gift. For if through the offence of one many be dead, much more the grace of God, and the gift by grace, which is by one man, Jesus Christ, hath abounded unto many.

## Immediate biblical context: Romans 5:1–21

Romans 5 begins with the consequences of justification by faith and moves into the Adam–Christ comparison.

- **Verses 1–5:** Paul speaks of peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ, access by faith into grace, hope of the glory of God, tribulations, patience, experience, hope, and the love of God shed abroad in hearts by the Holy Ghost.
- **Verses 6–11:** Paul describes Christ dying for the ungodly, God's commendation of His love, justification by Christ's blood, reconciliation through the death of God's Son, and joy in God through Jesus Christ.
- **Verses 12–14:** Paul traces the entrance of sin into the world through one man and death through sin. He states that death reigned from Adam to Moses, including over those whose sin was not after the similitude of Adam's transgression, and calls Adam “the figure of him that was to come.”
- **Verses 15–17:** Paul distinguishes the offence from the free gift. He contrasts the consequence of one man's offence with the abundance of grace and the gift by grace through Jesus Christ; he also contrasts death reigning through the offence with the reign in life received through Jesus Christ.
- **Verses 18–19:** Paul states the comparison in terms of one man's offence and condemnation, and one man's righteousness and the free gift; he then contrasts one man's disobedience with the obedience of one.
- **Verses 20–21:** Paul speaks of the law entering so that the offence might abound, grace abounding much more, and grace reigning through righteousness unto eternal life by Jesus Christ our Lord.

The passage is therefore not merely a general comparison between two historical men. Paul's stated subject includes sin, death, condemnation, the free gift, justification, righteousness, grace, and eternal life.

## What the passage directly states

1. Through one man, sin entered into the world, and death by sin; death passed upon all men (verse 12).
2. Sin was in the world before the law, and death reigned from Adam to Moses (verses 13–14).
3. Adam is called “the figure of him that was to come” (verse 14).
4. Paul distinguishes the offence from the free gift and contrasts their consequences (verses 15–17).
5. The grace of God and the gift by grace are through one man, Jesus Christ (verse 15).
6. Paul contrasts condemnation associated with the offence of one with the free gift associated with many offences unto justification (verse 16).
7. Paul contrasts death reigning through one man's offence with the reign in life of those who receive abundance of grace and the gift of righteousness through Jesus Christ (verse 17).
8. Paul sets the offence of one and the righteousness of one in parallel in verse 18.
9. Paul expressly contrasts one man's disobedience with the obedience of one (verse 19).
10. Paul states that the law entered so that the offence might abound, but where sin abounded, grace did much more abound (verse 20).
11. Paul concludes that grace reigns through righteousness unto eternal life by Jesus Christ our Lord (verse 21).

## The Adam–Christ relationship recorded in GUA-W

The GUA-W synthesis names Romans 5:12–21 as a passage contrasting Adam's transgression with the obedience and gift through Jesus Christ. Its §6 also records 1 Corinthians 15:21–22 and 45–49 as related Adam–Christ and resurrection witnesses.

The passages contribute distinct statements:

- **Romans 5:12–21:** the entrance of sin and death through one man; Adam as a figure of the One to come; the contrast between offence/disobedience and the free gift/obedience of Jesus Christ; and grace reigning through righteousness unto eternal life.
- **1 Corinthians 15:20–22:** Christ is risen and become the firstfruits of them that slept; Paul relates death through a man to resurrection of the dead through a man.
- **1 Corinthians 15:45–49:** Paul identifies the first man Adam, the last Adam, and the second man, the Lord from heaven, within his argument about the resurrection body and the earthy/heavenly image.

These passages illuminate the Adam–Christ relationship, but their wording and immediate arguments are not interchangeable. Romans 5 focuses on sin, condemnation, justification, the free gift, obedience, and grace; 1 Corinthians 15 focuses on Christ's Resurrection, the resurrection of the dead, and the nature of the resurrection body.

## What is directly stated, and what is not added

**Directly stated by Paul:** Adam's transgression is connected with the entrance of sin and death; Adam is a figure of the One to come; Jesus Christ is the One through whom the free gift and abundance of grace come; and Paul's contrast includes disobedience and obedience.

**Recorded by GUA-W:** Romans 5:12–21 belongs to the source-documented Adam–Christ relationship and is read alongside the resurrection passages in 1 Corinthians 15.

**Not asserted by this placement:**
- It does not make Adam an original cause equal to God. Paul's passage describes the consequence of one man's transgression; the GUA-W governing distinction remains God alone as Original Cause.
- It does not make Christ's obedience merely a reversal in a human moral example. Paul identifies the gift and grace as coming through Jesus Christ.
- It does not collapse “offence,” “judgment,” “condemnation,” “free gift,” “justification,” “righteousness,” “obedience,” and “grace” into synonyms.
- It does not resolve every theological debate about the scope of “all men” and “many” in verses 18–19 by isolating those phrases from the whole paragraph and wider Pauline context.
- It does not claim that Romans 5 supplies a physical mechanism for sin, death, resurrection, or salvation.

## Triple Constraint

- **Biblical compliance:** Romans 5:12–21 is read within Romans 5:1–21. The distinctions between Adam's offence, Christ's obedience, condemnation, the free gift, justification, righteousness, and grace are retained. The wording of verses 18–19 is not detached from the surrounding comparison.
- **Scientific coherence:** This is a theological and canonical examination. It makes no claim that scientific measurement confirms Paul's statements and proposes no physical mechanism for sin, death, or the free gift.
- **GUA-W consistency:** The passage is placed within the documented Adam–Christ relationship while preserving God as Original Cause and Jesus Christ's distinct role in Paul's account. No new GUA-W layer, runtime stage, or independent cause is proposed.

## Limits and unresolved questions

1. The relationship between “all men” in verse 18 and “many” in verse 19 requires careful reading of Paul's argument and wider context; this placement does not settle the full scope by a phrase detached from its paragraph.
2. Verse 14's description of Adam as “the figure of him that was to come” deserves to be kept distinct from the more developed “last Adam” language in 1 Corinthians 15:45.
3. The relation between the law, sin, and the offence in verses 13–20 must retain Paul's wording; this passage does not say that the law originated sin.
4. The relationship between the gift of righteousness in verse 17, justification in verses 16 and 18, and being made righteous in verse 19 is recorded without treating the terms as identical.
5. The connection to 1 Corinthians 15:20–22 remains a separate passage examination. It is identified by the GUA-W synthesis, but this placement does not substitute for reading its full chapter context.
