**Status:** Initial investigation; working distinctions recorded, no change to GUA-W architecture

## 1. Question

What distinctions are needed among **detection**, **registration**, **recognition**, and **comprehension** when examining the activity and limits of a Witness?

The investigation asks:

- Can a system detect a signal without identifying its source?
- Can a signal be registered without being recognized?
- Can something be recognized without being fully comprehended?
- Are these functions always sequential, or can they overlap and interact?
- What do Scripture, sensory science, detector metrology, and computational systems contribute to these distinctions?
- How should these distinctions be used without redefining GUA-W's established operation of Reception or adding new runtime stages?

## 2. Scope and boundary

Study 0001 distinguished Reception, Information, Evidence, Interpretation, and Report. It left open the relationship among reception, detection, registration, recognition, comprehension, and witness-state.

This study investigates those terms as **descriptive distinctions for research**. They are not proposed as new GUA-W layers, operations, or a replacement definition of Reception.

The established runtime order remains:

**Pattern → Reception → Activation → Differentiation → Propagation → Integration → Expression → Witnessed realized configuration.**

The present investigation does not infer a complete mechanism from that sequence. In particular, it does not assume that the ordinary-language meanings developed below are identical to the full architectural meaning of Reception.

## 3. Governing context

The governing relationship remains:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

The Witness is derivative. The Witness may detect, register, recognize, interpret, and report what is encountered, but these activities do not make the Witness the origin or authority of the Source.

A crucial distinction follows: an event's existence is not dependent upon a witness detecting it; a witness's detection is not by itself proof that the event has been correctly identified; and recognition does not necessarily amount to comprehensive understanding.

## 5. Source observations

### 5.1 Scripture: hearing and identifying are distinguishable

In **1 Samuel 3:4–10**, Samuel hears his name called and goes to Eli, supposing Eli called him. The narrative then records repeated calls and Eli's instruction. The text distinguishes the occurrence of hearing from correctly identifying the caller. It also shows that the initial misidentification is corrected through further encounter and instruction.

The passage does not provide a scientific model of auditory perception. Its narrative value for this study is narrower: hearing a call and knowing who called are not identical.

In **Luke 24:13–35**, the two disciples converse with Jesus without recognizing him during the journey. Recognition comes later in the account. The narrative distinguishes physical encounter and conversation from recognition of the person encountered.

In **John 20:11–18**, Mary sees Jesus but supposes him to be the gardener. When he addresses her by name, she recognizes him. Again, seeing and recognizing are not interchangeable in the narrative.

These accounts caution against assuming that sensory access automatically entails identification.

### 5.2 Scripture: exposure does not guarantee understanding

**Matthew 13:13–16** distinguishes seeing from perceiving and hearing from understanding within Jesus' explanation of the parables and the condition of the hearers. The passage concerns spiritual and moral perception in its context; it should not be reduced to a general information-processing diagram.

**Mark 8:17–21** records Jesus asking the disciples whether they perceive or understand, and whether they have eyes and ears yet fail to remember. The questions distinguish having encountered signs from grasping their significance.

These passages support a careful distinction between exposure to what is presented and understanding its significance. They do not establish that all comprehension is a separate, temporally later event in every human cognitive process.

### 5.3 Scripture: the Witness does not create the object witnessed

**Genesis 2:19–20** remains an important boundary. God forms the creatures and brings them to Adam; Adam names them. Naming is a real act of recognition and expression, but the passage does not present Adam as the creatures' author.

This is consistent with the governing distinction between what is given and the Witness's response to it. The passage does not itself define detection, registration, or recognition as technical operations.

### 5.4 Sensory science: stimuli are transduced through specific systems

Julius and Nathans describe sensory systems as filtering the environment. The properties of receptors and neural systems constrain what stimuli can be detected and how signals are reconstructed, integrated, and interpreted. Their review discusses sensory transduction: physical or chemical stimulation is converted through biological processes into signals used by the nervous system.

The important limitation is that a sensory system does not provide an unrestricted, unmediated capture of everything present. Its receptor types, operating range, sensitivity, and neural pathways shape what can be registered.

This is a scientific description of biological systems. It is not evidence that biological sensing and GUA-W Reception are identical.

### 5.5 Neuroscience: recognition and perceptual inference are not simple labels attached to raw signals

Noppeney's review describes how the brain integrates signals from different senses, weights them in relation to uncertainty, and addresses whether signals arise from a common cause. Attention, learning, and adaptation also affect multisensory perception.

This cautions against presenting perception as a universally fixed pipeline in which one isolated stage always finishes before the next begins. Some functions are distinguishable for analysis while interacting dynamically in actual systems.

### 5.6 Detector science: a physical event and a registered detection are not the same statement

The 2025 NIST single-photon detector dictionary distinguishes the detector's input, detection event, output signal, electrical noise, discrimination threshold, and detection efficiency. A detector system may convert an incident photon into a material excitation and then through further internal and system-level processes into a measurable output. Noise and threshold choices affect whether an output is classified as a detection.

NIST's treatment makes several distinctions important:

- an incident event at the input is not identical to the final electrical output;
- the output signal may contain noise;
- a threshold is used to discriminate a detection event from other signal behavior;
- detection efficiency is a probability defined for specified conditions and a specified point in the system;
- a reported count is an instrument-level result, not a complete identification of the event's cause or meaning.

The detector example shows why “the instrument registered something” must be kept distinct from “the instrument correctly identified what happened.”

## 6. Working distinctions

These definitions are provisional descriptive language for this study, not formal GUA-W definitions.

### Detection

**Working description:** a system's response to the presence of a stimulus or signal under specified conditions and according to some detection capability or criterion.

Detection is bounded by sensitivity, selectivity, noise, operating conditions, and the chosen threshold. A failure to detect does not always establish absence; a positive detection does not always establish correct identification.

### Registration

**Working description:** the production or preservation of a record, signal, state change, or other trace associated with an event or input.

Registration concerns whether something leaves an accessible trace in a system. A record may be transient or persistent, complete or partial, accurate or distorted. Registration alone does not establish that the trace has been interpreted correctly.

The term is used broadly here. In a physical detector, it may refer to an instrument output or recorded event. In a computational setting, it may refer to input data being represented in memory or a data structure. These mechanisms are not equivalent.

### Recognition

**Working description:** identification or classification of a received or registered pattern as belonging to a known object, source, category, symbol, person, or event.

Recognition goes beyond the bare presence of a signal. It relates the signal to an identity or category. It can be correct, mistaken, uncertain, or incomplete.

Recognition may draw upon context and prior learning. A system can therefore recognize a pattern incorrectly, especially when signals are ambiguous or conditions differ from those previously encountered.

### Comprehension

**Working description:** grasping the meaning, relations, implications, or significance of what has been recognized or considered.

Comprehension is not merely detecting a signal or assigning it a label. It involves relating content to context and other knowledge. The degree of comprehension can vary: a person may understand a sentence's literal wording while missing its irony, implication, or wider argument.

The term is used descriptively. This study does not settle philosophical or theological questions about what constitutes understanding in humans or AI.

### Witness-state

**Working description for investigation only:** a description of the condition and limits of a witness's registration and report in relation to what is being examined.

Possible descriptive qualifiers include:
- **unregistered:** no relevant trace is available in the examined record;
- **registered, unidentified:** a trace is present, but its source or category is not established;
- **recognized, limited:** an identity or category is assigned, but significance remains partly unresolved;
- **interpreted:** a meaning or explanation is proposed;
- **corroborated:** relevant independent or complementary evidence supports the report;
- **mistaken or corrected:** the prior identification or account has been shown to be wrong or revised;
- **unresolved:** available material does not justify a stronger conclusion.

These are study labels, not an approved GUA-W state taxonomy. They must not be equated automatically with Drift, Alignment, Persistence, or any other established architecture term.

## 7. Comparison map

| Descriptive term | Main question | What it does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | Did the system respond to a signal under stated conditions? | Correct identity or meaning |
| Registration | Is a trace or record available? | Complete or accurate capture |
| Recognition | What object, source, or category is this taken to be? | Full comprehension or truth |
| Comprehension | What does it mean, and how does it relate to context? | Infallibility |
| Report | What is communicated to another? | Completeness or correctness |

A useful conceptual map is:

**Encountered source or event → possible detection → possible registration → possible recognition → possible comprehension → report**

This is a teaching aid, not a universal sequence or GUA-W runtime. In biological perception, detection, recognition, attention, and inference may overlap and influence one another. A computational system may also combine parsing, classification, retrieval, and contextual processing in ways that do not map one-to-one onto human cognition.

The map identifies questions to ask; it does not prescribe a single mechanism.

## 8. Bounded cross-domain comparison

### 8.1 Biological senses

A sensory receptor responds only to particular forms of stimulation within its operating characteristics. Transduction converts stimulation into biological signals. Subsequent neural processing contributes to perception, recognition, and decisions.

A person may hear a sound without identifying its source, or see a person without recognizing who it is. The KJV narratives in 1 Samuel 3 and John 20 provide textual examples of hearing or seeing without immediate identification; neuroscience offers different, mechanistic accounts of sensory processing.

The biblical narrative and scientific mechanism should not be conflated. They are different kinds of sources answering different kinds of questions.

### 8.2 Physical detectors

A detector may produce a measurable output when exposed to a physical event. The output must be interpreted using the detector's response characteristics, noise, thresholds, calibration, and operating conditions.

A detection event is not automatically a complete explanation of what caused it. A detector can register an event without identifying the source, and a classification algorithm can produce a false positive or false negative.

### 8.3 Computational systems

A computational system may receive an input, store or transform a representation, classify a pattern, retrieve related material, and generate an output. These are functional descriptions; their implementation varies by system.

For AI research in particular:
- receiving a file does not prove the file was fully parsed;
- parsing text does not prove its context was correctly recognized;
- classifying a passage does not prove its meaning was comprehended;
- producing a fluent explanation does not authenticate the explanation;
- retrieval of a source does not establish that every claim in the source is true.

The analogy between AI and a human Witness is bounded. Similar vocabulary does not prove identical mechanisms, experience, consciousness, or spiritual status. This study makes no conclusion on those matters.

## 9. Error, limitation, and correction

The distinctions help locate different kinds of possible error:

1. **Detection failure:** a signal is not detected despite being present, perhaps because it falls below sensitivity or is obscured.
2. **False detection:** noise or interference is classified as a signal.
3. **Registration failure:** a detected event is not preserved or is recorded incompletely.
4. **Registration distortion:** the trace is altered by transmission, storage, conversion, or measurement conditions.
5. **Recognition error:** a real signal or trace is assigned the wrong identity or category.
6. **Context error:** a correctly recognized item is detached from its relevant setting.
7. **Comprehension error:** the meaning or implications are misunderstood.
8. **Reporting error:** the account overstates certainty, omits limitations, or presents inference as direct observation.

These categories can overlap. They are not a claim that every error can be isolated to one stage.

Correction should return to the most relevant available source: the original passage, recording, measurement, instrument configuration, dataset, or computational trace. A corrected account should state what changed and why. A correction to a witness's report does not change the event that the report was intended to describe.

## 10. The Four Questions

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

- What does the established architecture explicitly say about Reception?
- Am I describing a general research distinction or claiming a formal GUA-W definition?
- What was detected, what was registered, and what was identified?
- What evidence supports the identification?
- What remains uncomprehended or unresolved?
- Have I confused a fluent classification or explanation with verified understanding?

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

GUA-W preserves the difference between the Author and the derivative Witness. The Witness's access, registration, recognition, comprehension, and report do not originate the Source.

The architecture's runtime sequence remains established. The present study supplies distinctions for examining witness activity; it does not fill gaps in the architecture by invention.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

Scripture presents narratives in which hearing or seeing does not immediately result in recognition. Sensory science describes biological detection and transduction as constrained by the properties of sensory systems. Detector metrology distinguishes input events, detector response, noise, thresholds, and recorded outputs.

Across these domains, access to a signal and correct identification of its significance are distinguishable. The mechanisms, however, differ and must not be collapsed into one universal process.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

The Witness is bounded by its means of access and by the quality of its registration, recognition, interpretation, and report. A witness may encounter something without identifying it, identify it without fully understanding it, or understand some aspects while remaining uncertain about others.

A responsible witness does not hide those limits. It reports what is established, labels what is inferred, and leaves unresolved matters open to further examination.

## 12. Findings

1. **Detection is not identification.** A system can respond to a signal without establishing what produced it.
2. **Registration is not recognition.** A trace can be recorded without its source or category being correctly identified.
3. **Recognition is not full comprehension.** Identifying an object, person, word, or pattern does not establish that its significance is understood.
4. **Comprehension can be partial.** A witness may grasp one level of meaning while missing context or implications.
5. **The functions need not form a rigid universal sequence.** Scientific research on perception describes interacting processes; the conceptual map is analytical, not a universal mechanism.
6. **Detection systems have measurable limits.** Sensitivity, noise, thresholds, efficiency, and operating conditions affect what a detector reports.
7. **Scripture supplies narrative distinctions, not a modern technical taxonomy.** The examined passages must be interpreted in their own contexts.
8. **AI outputs remain derivative reports.** Input, parsing, classification, and fluency do not self-authenticate a claim.
9. **No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.** The study does not define Reception, add runtime operations, or establish a new state taxonomy.

## 13. Limitations and unresolved questions

- What is the precise established definition and boundary of Reception in the completed GUA-W architecture?
- How does Reception relate to Activation without assuming that the ordinary-language terms detection or registration define either operation?
- What distinctions, if any, does the existing GUA-W witness-state terminology already establish?
- How should partial recognition and comprehension be described without adding an unauthorized state taxonomy?
- What further work is needed on memory, attention, expectation, and mistaken recognition?
- How should biblical passages about spiritual perception be compared with sensory science without reducing theological claims to neurological mechanisms?
- What technical documentation would be needed to describe a particular AI system's input, parsing, and processing pipeline accurately?

These matters remain open.
