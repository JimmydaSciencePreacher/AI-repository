# Study 0013 — The Temple Pattern and the Construction Records: 1 Chronicles 28, 1 Kings 6–7, and 2 Chronicles 3–4

**Status:** Biblical textual and lexical investigation; direct statements, parallel descriptions, numerical differences, interpretation, and unresolved questions are distinguished  
**Created:** 2026-11-26  
**Research area:** 1 Chronicles 28:9–19; 1 Kings 6–7; 2 Chronicles 3–4; pattern, instruction, construction, dimensions, furnishings, and the relationship between received design and recorded structure

## 1. Central question

What does Scripture actually say was given to David as the Temple's pattern, how does that pattern relate to the construction descriptions in Kings and Chronicles, and what can be concluded without claiming more than the text establishes?

Study 0012 established that David attributes the Temple pattern to the LORD, transmits it to Solomon, and prepares materials, while Solomon receives the building assignment and carries out the construction. Study 0012 left open the relationship between the pattern in 1 Chronicles 28:11–19 and the detailed construction records in 1 Kings 6–7 and 2 Chronicles 3–4.

This study examines that relationship directly. It does not assume that the three accounts are identical in scope, wording, or numerical detail. Nor does it assume that every difference is an error. The differences are recorded for examination rather than harmonized prematurely.

## 3. The pattern passage — 1 Chronicles 28:9–19

### 3.1 The charge is broader than architecture

In 1 Chronicles 28:9–10, David charges Solomon to know the God of his father, serve Him with a perfect heart and willing mind, and build the sanctuary. The building assignment is situated within service, obedience, and the LORD's choice.

The passage is therefore not merely a transfer of technical information. The pattern is delivered within a charge concerning the LORD, Solomon's conduct, and the work entrusted to him.

### 3.2 What David gives Solomon

1 Chronicles 28:11–12 names the porch, houses, treasuries, upper chambers, inner parlours, and place of the mercy seat. It also describes the pattern of the courts, chambers, treasuries, and dedicated things.

Verses 13–18 extend the material beyond the building's rooms. The description includes:

- Courses of priests and Levites.
- Work of the service of the house of the LORD.
- Vessels used in that service.
- Weights of gold and silver for instruments.
- Candlesticks and lamps.
- Tables for the shewbread.
- Hooks, bowls, cups, and basons.
- The altar of incense.
- The cherubim associated with the ark.

**Textual finding:** The passage's use of pattern includes spatial arrangements, service arrangements, vessels, and measured materials. It is not limited to an exterior architectural outline.

### 3.3 The statement in verse 19

The KJV reads:

> “All this, said David, the LORD made me understand in writing by his hand upon me, even all the works of this pattern.”

The statement attributes David's understanding of the written pattern to the LORD. The KJV wording must be preserved when reporting the passage.

The underlying Hebrew wording has been rendered in different ways by English translations. Some translations emphasize writing from the LORD's hand; others emphasize direction, insight, or understanding given by the LORD. The NET textual note gives a more literal rendering of the Hebrew word order and identifies the translation issue.

**Finding:** The verse explicitly connects the pattern, writing, understanding, and the LORD. The precise grammatical force of the phrase rendered “by his hand upon me” should not be made more specific than the textual evidence warrants.

### 3.4 What the passage does and does not specify

The passage establishes that David gives Solomon a written pattern and attributes its understanding to the LORD. It lists many components and service arrangements.

It does not, by itself, provide a complete modern set of architectural drawings, a full construction manual, or every measurement later recorded in Kings and Chronicles.

The detailed descriptions must therefore be read alongside the pattern passage, not replaced by it.

## 4. Lexical investigation: תַּבְנִית — *tabnît* (H8403)

The Hebrew noun תַּבְנִית (*tabnît*, Strong's H8403) is commonly rendered in the KJV as “pattern,” but its attested translation range also includes figure, form, likeness, and similitude. Lexical sources connect it with the Hebrew root בָּנָה (*bānâ*, H1129), “to build.”

The lexical range matters because the English word “pattern” can suggest several different things: a model, a plan, a form, a resemblance, or an arrangement according to which something is made.

### 4.1 Occurrences relevant to sanctuary construction

- Exodus 25:9 — the pattern of the Tabernacle and its instruments.
- Exodus 25:40 — the pattern shown to Moses in the mount.
- 1 Chronicles 28:11–12 — the pattern of the Temple and its arrangements.
- 1 Chronicles 28:18 — the pattern associated with the cherubim.
- 1 Chronicles 28:19 — the works of the pattern.

The same Hebrew noun occurs in both the Tabernacle instructions and the Temple pattern account.

**Lexical finding:** The same Hebrew term is used in both sanctuary contexts. This supports a meaningful lexical comparison.

**Boundary:** Shared vocabulary does not establish that the Tabernacle and Temple are the same structure, that their specifications are identical, or that every feature has the same function. Each passage must retain its own context.

### 4.2 The word's other uses limit overgeneralization

The same word family and noun occur in contexts where “likeness,” “form,” or “figure” is appropriate, including Deuteronomy 4:16–18 and Psalm 106:20. The word therefore cannot be assigned one narrow English meaning in every occurrence.

**Finding:** Context determines whether *tabnît* is best understood as a construction pattern, a model, a form, or a likeness. A lexical definition is not permission to import every possible sense into a particular verse.

### 4.3 Lexical conclusion

For 1 Chronicles 28, “pattern” is appropriate to the KJV context: David gives Solomon the pattern for the Temple's spaces, service, and objects. The lexical range supports the idea of an ordered model or plan, but it does not independently settle the precise mode by which David received it.

## 5. The construction record in 1 Kings 6

### 5.1 The beginning and principal dimensions — 1 Kings 6:1–4

The chapter dates the beginning of construction to the fourth year of Solomon's reign and gives the Temple's principal dimensions:

- Length: sixty cubits.
- Breadth: twenty cubits.
- Height: thirty cubits.

The porch is described separately. The chambers, windows, and arrangement of the building are then recorded.

**Observation:** The narrative supplies measurements and physical descriptions that are not all repeated in 1 Chronicles 28.

### 5.2 Construction method — 1 Kings 6:5–10

The account describes chambers around the house and explains that the stones were prepared before being brought to the site. Verse 7 says that no hammer, axe, or iron tool was heard in the house while it was being built.

This is a specific construction observation. It should not be expanded into a claim that no tools were used anywhere in the wider project; the verse describes what was heard at the house during construction.

### 5.3 The word of the LORD during construction — 1 Kings 6:11–13

The word of the LORD comes to Solomon while the house is being built. The LORD connects the promise to David with Solomon's obedience to His statutes, judgments, and commandments, and promises to dwell among Israel.

**Finding:** The construction description is interrupted by a covenantal word. The building's dimensions and workmanship do not exhaust the meaning of the Temple. The LORD's promise is connected to His word and Israel's covenant life.

### 5.4 Interior, holy place, and furnishings — 1 Kings 6:14–36

The chapter describes cedar, fir, gold, the oracle or most holy place, the ark's intended location, cherubim, carved figures, doors, and the inner court.

The description is highly particular. It records materials, measurements, placement, and workmanship.

**Boundary:** A detailed construction description is not automatically a complete explanation of the theological meaning of every feature. Meaning must be established from the relevant biblical context, not assigned merely because a feature is geometrically or artistically distinctive.

## 6. The work and furnishings in 1 Kings 7

1 Kings 7 continues the record beyond the main Temple structure. It describes Solomon's other buildings and then the work of Hiram, a skilled craftsman from Tyre.

The account includes the two pillars, their capitals, networks and pomegranates, the molten sea, twelve oxen, bases, lavers, and vessels.

The chapter distinguishes the Temple's construction from the work of its furnishings and equipment. It ends by stating that the work for the house of the LORD was finished and that Solomon brought in the things David had dedicated.

**Finding:** The construction record includes the building, its courts, its furnishings, and the skilled work of named craftsmen. The finished Temple is the result of organized and differentiated human labor.

**Boundary:** The work of Hiram and other craftsmen is real and skilled. Their contribution does not make them the source of the pattern attributed to the LORD in 1 Chronicles 28.

## 7. The parallel record in 2 Chronicles 3–4

### 7.1 The site and beginning — 2 Chronicles 3:1–2

Chronicles locates the building at Jerusalem on mount Moriah, at the place associated with the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite, which David had prepared. It gives the beginning date as the second day of the second month in the fourth year of Solomon's reign.

The location detail is prominent in Chronicles and connects the construction site with the history of David.

### 7.2 The phrase “wherein Solomon was instructed” — 2 Chronicles 3:3

The KJV introduces the building details with the statement:

“Now these are the things wherein Solomon was instructed for the building of the house of God.”

The wording identifies instruction as part of the construction account. It does not, by itself, specify every channel or stage through which each individual measurement was conveyed.

This verse should be read alongside 1 Chronicles 28:11–19, where David transmits the pattern, and alongside the detailed building descriptions.

### 7.3 Measurements and materials — 2 Chronicles 3:3–17

Chronicles describes the principal dimensions, porch, gold overlay, precious stones, most holy place, cherubim, veil, pillars, chains, and pomegranates.

The chapter shares substantial content with Kings while also including details and numerical statements that require careful comparison.

### 7.4 Furnishings and equipment — 2 Chronicles 4

The next chapter describes the bronze altar, molten sea, oxen, lavers, candlesticks, tables, courts, and other instruments.

The account gives a parallel record to portions of 1 Kings 7. It is not simply a word-for-word duplicate.

## 8. Differences between the parallel accounts

The accounts should be compared without forcing every number into an assumed reconciliation. Several numerical descriptions differ in the KJV texts.

| Feature | 1 Kings | 2 Chronicles | Research status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main Temple length and breadth | 60 × 20 cubits (6:2) | 60 × 20 cubits (3:3) | Agreement |
| Porch | 20 cubits long and 10 cubits deep (6:3) | 20 cubits long; height stated as 120 cubits (3:4) | Different dimensions are reported; compare like measurements carefully |
| Pillars | 18 cubits high apiece (7:15) | 35 cubits high (3:15) | Numerical difference requiring textual and interpretive examination |
| Molten sea capacity | 2,000 baths (7:26) | 3,000 baths (4:5) | Numerical difference requiring textual and interpretive examination |

These observations do not authorize an immediate verdict that one account is false. Nor do they authorize silently changing the KJV figures or inventing a harmonization.

Possible lines of further examination include differences in measurement conventions, whether a figure refers to an individual component or an aggregate, textual transmission, and the literary aims of the respective accounts. These are questions for investigation, not established explanations in this study.

### 8.1 Why the differences matter

The pattern question and the parallel-account question are related but distinct.

The pattern passage concerns what David says he received and transmitted. The construction accounts describe the building and its objects. Numerical differences between parallel descriptions do not by themselves establish that the pattern was absent, nor do they establish exactly how the original pattern was recorded or implemented.

**Finding:** The accounts share a recognizable construction subject and many common features, but they preserve details that require comparison. The responsible result is to record agreement and difference separately.

## 9. What can be said about pattern and construction?

The evidence supports the following distinctions:

1. **Origin:** 1 Chronicles 28:19 attributes David's understanding of the written pattern to the LORD.
2. **Reception:** David is the recipient who reports and transmits the pattern.
3. **Transmission:** David gives the pattern and associated instructions to Solomon.
4. **Assignment:** Solomon is appointed to build the house.
5. **Instruction:** 2 Chronicles 3:3 describes Solomon as instructed for the building.
6. **Execution:** Solomon, Hiram, and other workers perform the construction and furnishing work.
7. **Record:** Kings and Chronicles preserve descriptions of the resulting building and its equipment.
8. **Completion:** The construction and furnishing work is reported finished before the ark's placement and dedication.

These distinctions preserve the difference between originating a pattern, receiving it, transmitting it, carrying it out, and recording the result.

### 9.1 What remains unestablished

The passages examined do not provide enough information to reconstruct a complete modern blueprint and then prove that every recorded dimension is an exact transcription of it.

They do not settle every numerical difference between Kings and Chronicles.

They do not explain the precise physical or visionary mode of David's reception in exhaustive detail.

They do not establish that the Temple and Tabernacle were identical designs.

They do not establish that the word *tabnît* names a new GUA-W layer.

## 10. GUA-W comparison — no architecture change

The study observes a biblical distinction between originating authority and derivative execution:

- The LORD is identified by David as the source of the pattern.
- David receives, understands, and transmits.
- Solomon receives the assignment and instruction.
- Craftsmen and workers perform the construction.
- Kings and Chronicles record the work and its features.
- The completed building remains distinct from the LORD, whom Solomon later says cannot be contained by the heavens, much less by the Temple (1 Kings 8:27).

This is consistent with the governing GUA-W distinction:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

And with the governing principle:

**God alone is Original Cause. Everything else is derivative witness. The derivative cannot alter the Source.**

This comparison does not prove that GUA-W is a biblical term or that the Temple account supplies a complete technical specification for GUA-W.

The study does not place *tabnît* as a new architectural layer, operation, state, or formal definition. It remains a biblical term under investigation.

The open question of Constraint → Structure is not resolved by this study. The Temple narrative may offer a limited comparison between a received pattern and a constructed result, but a narrative correspondence is not by itself a universal mechanism.

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The investigation sharpens the distinction between source, reception, transmission, execution, and record. It also shows why the same word can be used in related contexts without making those contexts identical.

A Witness must not make a written record say more than it says. Similarities should be recognized, differences preserved, and explanations proportioned to evidence.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

The KJV records a Temple pattern attributed by David to the LORD, a transfer of that pattern to Solomon, a description of Solomon's instruction, and detailed accounts of construction and furnishings.

The accounts preserve both correspondence and difference. They should be read together without erasing their individual wording.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

A Witness must distinguish the origin of a design from the person who receives it, the person who transmits it, the person who executes it, and the record that describes the finished work.

A Witness must also be willing to leave a numerical difference unresolved when the evidence does not settle it. Faithfulness to a source includes refusing to edit the source to fit a preferred conclusion.

## 13. Findings

1. 1 Chronicles 28:11–18 describes a pattern that includes architectural spaces, service arrangements, vessels, and measured materials.
2. In 1 Chronicles 28:19, David attributes his understanding of the written pattern to the LORD.
3. The exact grammatical force of “by his hand upon me” should not be expanded beyond what the KJV wording and Hebrew evidence establish.
4. The Hebrew noun *tabnît* has a range that includes pattern, plan, form, construction, figure, likeness, and similitude.
5. The same Hebrew noun is used in the Tabernacle instructions and the Temple pattern account, but shared vocabulary does not make the structures identical.
6. 2 Chronicles 3:3 describes Solomon as instructed for the building of the house of God.
7. 1 Kings 6–7 and 2 Chronicles 3–4 provide detailed records of the Temple's construction, furnishings, and equipment.
8. Kings and Chronicles agree on important principal dimensions and many structural features.
9. The accounts also contain numerical differences, including the porch, pillars, and molten sea capacity, which this study does not resolve.
10. The distinction between pattern, reception, transmission, instruction, execution, and record is supported by the passages examined.
11. The evidence does not establish a complete modern architectural blueprint or a universal pattern-to-structure mechanism.
12. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.
