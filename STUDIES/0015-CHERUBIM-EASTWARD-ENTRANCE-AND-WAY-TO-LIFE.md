# Study 0015 — The Cherubim, the Eastward Entrance, and the Way to the Tree of Life

## 1. Central question

Why are cherubim placed at the guarded way to the tree of life in Genesis 3:24, and why do cherubim appear again in the Tabernacle, Temple, prophetic visions, and heavenly throne imagery?

A second question follows: what is the significance of the eastward orientation of Eden's guarded entrance and the entrances of later sanctuaries?

The study must distinguish three levels:

1. What each passage explicitly states.
2. What repeated words, images, and spatial arrangements make comparable.
3. What theological conclusions may be drawn without treating resemblance as proof of identical function or identity.

The investigation does not begin by assuming that Eden was a Temple. It asks whether later Scripture supplies a connection, and how far that connection can be taken.

## 3. Genesis 2 — the Garden and the command

Genesis 2:8 records that the LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden and placed the man there. Genesis 2:9 describes the trees, including the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

Genesis 2:15 says the LORD God took the man and put him into the Garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it. Genesis 2:16–17 records the command concerning the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

### Direct observations

- The Garden is planted by the LORD God.
- The man is placed in the Garden by the LORD.
- The man receives an assignment and a command.
- The tree of life is present before the Fall.
- The tree of the knowledge of good and evil is associated with a specific prohibition.

The text does not describe Adam as the source of the Garden, its trees, its command, or its life.

### Boundary

Genesis 2 does not call Eden a sanctuary, Tabernacle, or Temple. Any such correspondence must be established through comparison with other passages, not inserted into Genesis as though the word appeared there.

## 4. Genesis 3:22–24 — the guarded way

After the disobedience, the LORD God says that the man has become as one of us, to know good and evil, and acts so that he does not take also of the tree of life, eat, and live for ever (Genesis 3:22).

Genesis 3:23 records that the LORD God sends the man forth from the Garden of Eden to till the ground from which he was taken.

Genesis 3:24 records:

> “So he drove out the man; and he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life.”

### What is explicit?

1. The man is driven out.
2. Cherubim are placed at the east of the Garden.
3. A flaming sword is also described.
4. The sword turns every way.
5. The stated purpose is to keep the way of the tree of life.

The KJV says “to keep the way.” The Hebrew noun דֶּרֶךְ (*derek*, way, road, path, course) is used in the phrase concerning the way of the tree of life. The verse describes guarded access, not merely a decorative boundary.

### What is not explicit?

- The verse does not state the number of cherubim.
- It does not describe their appearance.
- It does not say they are identical in form to the later cherubim represented in the Tabernacle or Temple.
- It does not say that a human being can pass the guardians by constructing a sanctuary.
- It does not state that the Garden's eastward location makes every later east-facing entrance a direct reproduction of Eden.

### Initial finding

The cherubim in Genesis 3:24 are associated with a divinely imposed boundary after the Fall. Their stated function in this passage is to guard the way to the tree of life.

The tree of life is not described as destroyed. Access is guarded.

This distinction matters: the narrative records exclusion from access, not the disappearance of the tree.

## 5. The Tabernacle — cherubim in the appointed sanctuary

### 5.1 The mercy seat — Exodus 25:17–22

The LORD commands Moses to make two cherubim of gold at the two ends of the mercy seat. The cherubim are to stretch forth their wings on high, covering the mercy seat with their wings, and their faces are to look one to another and toward the mercy seat.

The LORD says:

> “And there I will meet with thee, and I will commune with thee from above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubims which are upon the ark of the testimony...” (Exodus 25:22)

This is an explicit relationship between the cherubim, the mercy seat, the ark, and the place from which the LORD says He will meet and speak with Moses.

**Important distinction:** In Genesis 3, cherubim guard the way to the tree of life. In Exodus 25, crafted cherubim are positioned over the mercy seat, and the LORD identifies the place between them as the place from which He will meet and speak with Moses. The texts share the cherubim image, but the stated functions and settings are not identical.

### 5.2 Cherubim woven into the curtains — Exodus 26:1, 31

The Tabernacle curtains are made with fine twined linen and blue, purple, and scarlet, with cherubim of cunning work. The veil is also made with cherubim.

The cherubim are therefore not confined to the ark's mercy seat. They appear in the textile boundaries and divisions of the sanctuary.

### 5.3 The veil and the boundary

Exodus 26:31–33 describes the veil and says it divides the holy place from the most holy. The ark is placed within the veil.

The sanctuary's boundaries are real and commanded. Access and service are arranged according to divine instruction.

The text does not say that the veil is a replica of the flaming sword. That would exceed the evidence. A guarded way in Genesis and a sanctuary veil in Exodus can be compared as boundary imagery, but they are not stated to be the same object or mechanism.

### 5.4 The entrance of the Tabernacle — Exodus 26:36–37; 27:13–16

The Tabernacle's door is made with a hanging, and the court's gate is placed on the east side. Exodus 27:13 states that the breadth of the court on the east side is fifty cubits; verses 14–16 describe the hangings and gate.

The east side is therefore an explicit orientation of the court's entrance.

### 5.5 The cherubim and the east-facing entrance — what can be compared?

Genesis 3:24 places cherubim at the east of Eden. Exodus 27 describes the court gate on the east side. Exodus 25–26 describes cherubim over the mercy seat and woven into sanctuary curtains.

These are genuine textual correspondences in imagery and spatial language. However, Exodus does not say, “The east gate is placed eastward because Eden's gate was guarded by cherubim.” No such explanatory statement appears in these passages.

**Finding:** The correspondence is worth investigating, but a direct historical or architectural derivation is not yet established.

## 6. Israel's camp — the east side and the sanctuary

Numbers 2:1–9 describes the arrangement of Israel's camp around the Tabernacle. The standard of Judah and the tribes associated with it are to pitch on the east side, toward the rising of the sun, by the ensign of their host.

Numbers 3:38 places Moses, Aaron, and his sons before the Tabernacle on the east side, toward the rising of the sun, keeping the charge of the sanctuary.

This establishes a deliberate eastward arrangement around the Tabernacle and a specific responsibility at the east side.

**Boundary:** The passage gives the camp's arrangement and the duties of those appointed. It does not explicitly explain the arrangement as a reenactment of Eden.

## 7. Solomon's Temple — cherubim and sanctuary imagery

### 7.1 The great cherubim — 1 Kings 6:23–28

Solomon makes two cherubim of olive tree for the inner house. Their wings extend across the house, and they are overlaid with gold.

The cherubim are large, fixed sanctuary figures. Their description differs from the smaller gold cherubim made for the mercy seat in Exodus 25.

### 7.2 Carved cherubim — 1 Kings 6:29–35

The walls of the house are carved with figures of cherubim, palm trees, and open flowers, both within and without. The doors and other surfaces also bear carved imagery.

The Temple is therefore decorated with cherubim imagery throughout specified areas.

### 7.3 The ark and the cherubim — 1 Kings 8:6–7

When the priests bring the ark into the oracle of the house, the wings of the cherubim cover the place of the ark and the staves thereof.

This continues the sanctuary association between the ark and cherubim.

### 7.4 What the Temple evidence establishes

- Cherubim are prominent in the Tabernacle and Temple.
- They are associated with the mercy seat, the inner sanctuary, and sanctuary decoration.
- Their presence is part of the commanded and constructed sacred environment.
- The Temple's cherubim imagery is related to, but not identical in physical form or placement with, the cherubim described in Genesis 3.

The Temple account does not say that the cherubim open the way to the tree of life. It does not say that the Temple restores Adam's access to Eden.

## 8. Ezekiel — cherubim, the throne, and the departing glory

### 8.1 The living creatures and the cherubim — Ezekiel 1 and 10

Ezekiel 1 describes living creatures in a vision associated with the glory of the LORD. Ezekiel 10 returns to the imagery and explicitly identifies the living creatures as cherubim (Ezekiel 10:20).

Ezekiel's vision includes wheels, wings, faces, movement, and the glory of the LORD. It must not be reduced to the simpler descriptions of the cherubim in Exodus.

### 8.2 The glory departs — Ezekiel 10:18–19; 11:22–23

Ezekiel sees the glory of the LORD depart from the threshold of the house and move toward the east gate. In Ezekiel 11:23, the glory of the LORD goes up from the midst of the city and stands upon the mountain on the east side of the city.

The eastward movement is explicit in the vision.

### 8.3 The glory returns from the east — Ezekiel 43:1–5

Ezekiel sees the glory of the God of Israel coming from the way of the east. The glory comes into the house, and the vision records that the glory of the LORD fills the house.

This is a striking narrative correspondence with the eastward departure in Ezekiel 10–11.

### 8.4 What Ezekiel contributes to the question

Ezekiel explicitly connects cherubim imagery with the throne and glory of the LORD, and depicts the glory moving eastward in departure and returning from the east in the restoration vision.

This provides a much stronger canonical context for the cherubim as associated with the divine throne and glory than Genesis 3:24 alone provides.

However, the exact relationship between Ezekiel's visionary cherubim and the cherubim crafted for the Tabernacle and Temple should not be flattened. The vision has details and functions specific to Ezekiel's prophetic experience.

## 9. Hebrews 9 — cherubim of glory and the way into the holiest

Hebrews 9:1–5 describes the first covenant's sanctuary and furnishings. Verse 5 refers to:

> “And over it the cherubims of glory shadowing the mercyseat...”

The KJV then notes that the writer cannot speak in detail of these things at that time.

Hebrews 9:6–10 describes the service of the priests and the high priest's entrance into the second tabernacle once every year, not without blood. It says the Holy Ghost signified that the way into the holiest of all was not yet made manifest while the first tabernacle was standing.

Hebrews 9:11–14 then turns to Christ as the High Priest of good things to come, entering by His own blood and obtaining eternal redemption.

### The critical connection

Hebrews explicitly relates the earthly sanctuary's arrangement and service to the question of access to God. It then presents Christ's priestly work as decisive.

This does not mean Hebrews explicitly identifies the cherubim of Genesis 3:24 with the cherubim of Exodus 25. It does mean the New Testament interprets the sanctuary's restricted access and priestly service in relation to Christ's work.

### Boundary

The phrase “the way into the holiest” in Hebrews 9:8 and “the way of the tree of life” in Genesis 3:24 are not the same phrase in the same language, nor does Hebrews 9 explicitly quote Genesis 3:24. Their comparison is a theological and canonical question, not an established direct quotation.

Hebrews 10:19–22 later speaks of boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, by a new and living way, which He hath consecrated for us. This is an explicit claim about access through Christ. The connection with Genesis' guarded way is a significant possible canonical comparison, but should be identified as comparison rather than direct textual citation.

## 10. Revelation — the throne, the tree of life, and the open city

### 10.1 Cherubim and throne imagery — Revelation 4

Revelation 4 describes four living creatures around the throne. Their imagery includes features comparable to Ezekiel's vision, and they continually give glory, honour, and thanks to the One who sits on the throne.

Revelation 4 does not use the word “cherubim” for these four living creatures. Ezekiel explicitly calls his living creatures cherubim in Ezekiel 10:20. A comparison is warranted, but the different wording must be preserved.

### 10.2 The tree of life returns — Revelation 22:1–5

Revelation 22 describes the river of the water of life and the tree of life. The servants of God see His face, His name is in their foreheads, and they reign for ever and ever.

This is a strong canonical return to the tree of life first described in Genesis 2 and guarded after the Fall in Genesis 3.

### 10.3 The gates are not shut — Revelation 21:25

Revelation 21 describes the holy city and says its gates shall not be shut at all by day, because there is no night there.

This is a notable contrast with the guarded way in Genesis 3:24. The final city is described with open gates, while Revelation 22 describes the tree of life and the servants seeing God's face.

The text does not explicitly say, “The cherubim have been removed from Eden's gate.” That would be an inferred narrative connection. What it does explicitly show is the final state of access, life, and divine presence.

### 10.4 No separate Temple — Revelation 21:22

John sees no Temple in the city, because the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the Temple of it.

This must remain distinct from the earlier sanctuary imagery. The final vision is not a return to the same arrangement of a guarded earthly sanctuary.

### Finding

Revelation brings the tree of life back into view and describes God's servants seeing His face. It depicts a final state that contrasts with the expulsion and guarded access in Genesis 3. The connection is canonical and strong, but the precise role of cherubim in the comparison remains an interpretive question because Revelation does not explicitly name them as guardians removed from the gate.

## 11. Eastward orientation — what is established and what remains open?

### Direct textual observations

- Genesis 2:8 says the Garden was planted eastward in Eden.
- Genesis 3:24 places the cherubim at the east of the Garden.
- Exodus 27:13–16 describes the east side of the court and its gate.
- Numbers 2:3 places Judah's camp on the east side, toward the rising of the sun.
- Numbers 3:38 places Moses, Aaron, and his sons on the east side, toward the rising of the sun.
- Ezekiel 10–11 depicts the glory departing toward the east.
- Ezekiel 43 depicts the glory returning from the east.

### What may be inferred cautiously?

There is a repeated eastward orientation in significant passages concerning the Garden, sanctuary, camp, and prophetic vision. Eastward movement and orientation deserve attention in a canonical study of sacred space.

### What is not yet established?

- That every eastward orientation has one identical symbolic meaning.
- That the Tabernacle gate is explicitly said to reproduce Eden's guarded entrance.
- That all sanctuary architecture was derived from a single Eden floor plan.
- That “east” by itself means sin, exile, judgment, or restoration in every passage.
- That the direction alone determines the meaning of a passage.

A valid interpretation must consider the immediate context, the direction of movement, the named actors, and the stated purpose in each passage.

## 12. Cherubim — a controlled comparison

| Passage | Description or placement | Stated role in the passage |

| Genesis 3:24 | Cherubim at east of Eden with a flaming sword | Keep the way of the tree of life |
| Exodus 25:18–22 | Two gold cherubim over the mercy seat | The LORD meets and speaks with Moses from above the mercy seat, between the cherubim |

### Comparison finding

Cherubim are associated in multiple passages with guarded sacred space, the mercy seat, sanctuary imagery, and the divine throne and glory.

The function is not identical in every passage. Genesis explicitly gives a guarding function; Exodus gives a sanctuary placement and identifies the meeting place; Ezekiel presents a prophetic throne vision. The recurring imagery is real, but each occurrence must retain its context.

## 15. Findings

1. Genesis 3:24 explicitly says cherubim are placed east of Eden to keep the way of the tree of life.
2. Genesis does not describe the cherubim's number, appearance, or later sanctuary function.
3. Exodus 25 places two crafted cherubim over the mercy seat and identifies the space between them as the place from which the LORD meets and speaks with Moses.
4. Exodus 26 includes cherubim in the Tabernacle curtains and veil.
5. Exodus 27 describes the court gate on the east side.
6. Numbers 2–3 records an east-side arrangement for the camp and sanctuary service.
7. 1 Kings 6–8 describes cherubim in Solomon's Temple, with forms and placements distinct from the mercy-seat figures.
8. Ezekiel 10:20 explicitly identifies the visionary living creatures as cherubim.
9. Ezekiel depicts the glory departing toward the east and later returning from the east in the restoration vision.
10. Hebrews 9 recalls the cherubim of glory over the mercy seat and relates the sanctuary's access restrictions to Christ's priestly work.
11. Hebrews 10 speaks of the new and living way through Christ; it does not explicitly quote Genesis 3:24.
12. Revelation 21–22 describes open gates, the tree of life, and God's servants seeing His face, but does not explicitly say cherubim were removed from Eden's gate.
13. Repeated cherubim imagery and eastward orientation create a meaningful canonical field of comparison, but do not prove identical function, physical identity, or direct architectural derivation.
14. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.
