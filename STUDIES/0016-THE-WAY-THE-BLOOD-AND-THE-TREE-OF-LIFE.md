# Study 0016 — The Way, the Blood, and the Tree of Life: From Genesis to Revelation

## 1. Central question

Does Scripture trace a movement from the guarded way to the tree of life in Genesis, through the sanctuary's restricted access, to access to God through Jesus Christ, and finally to the tree of life in Revelation?

A second question governs the study:

**Did Jesus merely show the way, or is He Himself the Way by which access to the Father and life are given?**

The answer must begin with Jesus' own words in John 14:6 and be tested against the surrounding context and the claims made in Hebrews and Revelation.

The study must not force every passage into a single metaphor. “Way,” “door,” “shepherd,” “blood,” “veil,” “life,” “tree,” and “access” have distinct contexts. Their relationships should be established from the text rather than assumed.

## 3. Genesis 2–3 — life present, access guarded

### 3.1 The tree of life is present before the Fall

Genesis 2:9 records that the LORD God made every tree grow that was pleasant to the sight and good for food, including the tree of life in the midst of the Garden and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

Genesis 2:16–17 records God's command concerning the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. The tree of life is named, but the passage does not state that Adam had already eaten from it.

### 3.2 The way is guarded after disobedience

Genesis 3:22 records the LORD God's stated concern that the man might take also of the tree of life, eat, and live for ever. Genesis 3:23 records the man's removal from the Garden. Genesis 3:24 records the cherubim and flaming sword appointed “to keep the way of the tree of life.”

### Direct observations

- The tree of life belongs to the Garden account before the Fall.
- After disobedience, the man is driven out.
- Cherubim and a flaming sword are placed at the east of the Garden.
- The stated purpose is to keep the way of the tree of life.

### Boundary

Genesis 3:24 does not say that the tree is destroyed. It says the way is guarded. Nor does Genesis itself explain the later work of Christ. Any connection to the New Testament must be established from the New Testament's own statements and then compared with Genesis.

## 4. John 10 — the door and the Good Shepherd

John 10:1–6 begins with the figure of the sheepfold, the door, the porter, the shepherd, and the sheep. Jesus distinguishes the one who enters by the door from the thief and robber who climbs up some other way. The sheep hear the shepherd's voice, and he calls his own sheep by name and leads them out.

John 10:7 records Jesus' statement:

> “I am the door of the sheep.”

John 10:9 continues:

> “I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.”

John 10:11 records:

> “I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.”

### What is explicit?

1. Jesus identifies Himself as the door of the sheep.
2. He says entry is “by me.”
3. He associates that entry with salvation and pasture.
4. He identifies Himself as the Good Shepherd.
5. He says the Good Shepherd gives His life for the sheep.
6. He distinguishes His own from thieves, robbers, and the hireling.

### Important contextual distinction

In John 10, “door” and “shepherd” are images used in the same discourse. Jesus identifies Himself with both, but their functions in the figure are not identical: the door concerns entry; the shepherd concerns care, calling, leading, protection, and giving His life.

The sheepfold is not explicitly identified as Eden, the Tabernacle, or the Temple. The passage should not be converted into a detailed architectural allegory without textual warrant.

### Finding

Jesus does not merely point out a door as an independent guide. He says, “I am the door,” and “by me” entry is given. The passage places access and salvation in relation to His own person and work.

## 5. John 14:1–6 — Jesus is the Way

John 14 begins with Jesus comforting His disciples. He speaks of His Father's house, many mansions, preparing a place, coming again, and receiving them unto Himself.

Thomas says that they do not know where Jesus is going and asks how they can know the way.

Jesus answers:

> “I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.” (John 14:6, KJV)

### Direct textual findings

- The question about the way is asked in the context of Jesus' departure and the disciples' destination.
- Jesus does not answer by giving directions independent of Himself.
- He identifies Himself as the Way.
- He identifies Himself as the Truth.
- He identifies Himself as the Life.
- He states that no man comes to the Father except by Him.

### What the verse does and does not say

It explicitly says that access to the Father is through Jesus Christ. It does not say that Jesus is merely one route among several, nor that He simply teaches a method which can be separated from His person.

It also does not say that the word “way” in John 14:6 is a direct quotation of Genesis 3:24. That connection is a canonical comparison to investigate, not a claim of direct quotation.

### Finding

The user's question can be answered at the level of the explicit statement: **Jesus does not merely show the way; He says that He is the Way.** The verse itself also joins Way, Truth, and Life and identifies Him as the sole access to the Father.

This conclusion rests on John 14:6, not on an inferred Eden analogy.

## 6. Hebrews 9 — the sanctuary, blood, and access

### 6.1 The first covenant sanctuary — Hebrews 9:1–10

Hebrews describes the first covenant's ordinances of divine service and a worldly sanctuary. It identifies the holy place and the holiest of all, the ark, the mercy seat, and the cherubim of glory shadowing the mercy seat (Hebrews 9:1–5).

The priests went continually into the first tabernacle, but the high priest went into the second once every year, not without blood (Hebrews 9:6–7).

Hebrews 9:8 says that the Holy Ghost signified that the way into the holiest of all was not yet made manifest while the first tabernacle was standing.

### 6.2 Christ's entrance and offering — Hebrews 9:11–14

Hebrews then presents Christ as the High Priest of good things to come. He enters by a greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands, and not by the blood of goats and calves, but by His own blood, having obtained eternal redemption.

The passage contrasts the blood of animal offerings with the blood of Christ and speaks of His offering as purifying the conscience from dead works to serve the living God.

### 6.3 The heavenly sanctuary — Hebrews 9:23–28

Hebrews says Christ did not enter holy places made with hands, which are figures of the true, but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us (verse 24).

It then contrasts repeated offerings with Christ's once-for-all appearance and offering. Verse 26 says He appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of Himself.

### Direct findings

- The first covenant had an appointed sanctuary and priestly service.
- Access to the holiest was restricted under that arrangement.
- The high priest entered with blood.
- Christ enters by His own blood.
- Christ's offering is distinguished from repeated animal sacrifices.
- Christ appears in the presence of God for us.
- Christ's sacrifice is presented as decisive and not a repeated annual offering.

### Boundary

Hebrews 9:8 uses “the way into the holiest,” but it does not explicitly quote Genesis 3:24. The shared subject of access is significant; it is not proof that the two passages use the same image in precisely the same sense.

## 7. Hebrews 10: the new and living way

Hebrews 10 develops the sufficiency of Christ's offering. The law is described as having a shadow of good things to come, not the very image of the things. The sacrifices offered year by year could not make those who came perfect (Hebrews 10:1).

Hebrews 10:10 says believers are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.

Hebrews 10:11–14 contrasts the priests, who stand daily offering the same sacrifices, with Christ, who after offering one sacrifice for sins for ever sat down on the right hand of God.

Then Hebrews 10:19–22 says:

> “Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus,  
> By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh;  
> And having an high priest over the house of God;  
> Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith...”

### What is explicit?

1. Believers have boldness to enter the holiest by the blood of Jesus.
2. The access is described as a new and living way.
3. The way is consecrated by Jesus.
4. The passage relates the way through the veil to His flesh.
5. Believers are called to draw near with a true heart and full assurance of faith.
6. The passage identifies Jesus as the great High Priest over the house of God.

### The central distinction

The access is not attributed to human construction, self-appointed authority, or an independent technique. Hebrews attributes it to Jesus' blood, His consecrated way, His flesh, and His priesthood.

### Connection with Genesis

Genesis 3:24 describes the way to the tree of life as guarded after the Fall. Hebrews 10 describes a new and living way into the holiest by the blood of Jesus.

These passages can be compared as part of Scripture's larger treatment of access and life. Hebrews does not explicitly say that its “new and living way” is a direct explanation of the guarded way in Genesis 3:24. The canonical connection is therefore a reasoned theological comparison, not a direct quotation.

## 8. Revelation 2:7 — the tree of life in the paradise of God

In the message to the church at Ephesus, Revelation 2:7 says:

> “To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life, which is in the midst of the paradise of God.”

### Direct findings

- The tree of life appears again in Revelation.
- It is associated with the paradise of God.
- The promise is given to the one who overcomes.
- The passage presents eating of the tree of life as a promised gift.

### Canonical comparison

Genesis 2 introduces the tree of life. Genesis 3 records the guarded way. Revelation 2:7 promises access to eat of the tree of life in God's paradise.

This is an explicit return to the tree-of-life subject. The relationship to the Genesis narrative is stronger than a general resemblance because the same named tree appears in both.

### Boundary

Revelation 2:7 does not explain the cherubim of Genesis 3:24 or explicitly state the mechanism by which the promise is fulfilled. It gives the promise; other passages must be considered for the broader account of access through Christ.

## 9. Revelation 21–22 — the final vision of life and presence

### 9.1 The dwelling of God — Revelation 21:1–4

Revelation 21 describes a new heaven and a new earth, the holy city, and the declaration that the tabernacle of God is with men, and He will dwell with them. God wipes away all tears; death, sorrow, crying, and pain are no more.

### 9.2 The water of life — Revelation 21:6

The One who sits upon the throne declares:

> “I will give unto him that is athirst of the fountain of the water of life freely.”

The gift is described as freely given to the thirsty.

### 9.3 The open gates — Revelation 21:25–27

The gates of the city are not shut by day, and there is no night there. The passage also describes what may and may not enter the city.

The open gates are a notable contrast with the guarded way of Genesis 3:24. However, Revelation does not explicitly say that these gates are Eden's original entrance or that the cherubim have been removed from that location.

### 9.4 The tree and river — Revelation 22:1–5

Revelation 22 describes the pure river of the water of life, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb. The tree of life is on either side of the river, bearing twelve manner of fruits and yielding fruit every month. Its leaves are for the healing of the nations.

The passage says there shall be no more curse. The throne of God and of the Lamb is in the city, His servants serve Him, they see His face, and His name is in their foreheads.

### 9.5 The final invitation and promise — Revelation 22:14, 17

In the KJV, Revelation 22:14 says:

> “Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city.”

Verse 17 records the invitation:

> “And the Spirit and the bride say, Come. And let him that heareth say, Come. And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely.”

The KJV reading of verse 14 is retained in this study. Textual differences among Greek manuscript traditions and modern translations exist at this verse; a full textual-critical investigation is outside the scope of this study. The reading used here should not be silently replaced by a different translation.

### Revelation findings

- The tree of life returns in the final vision.
- The river of the water of life proceeds from the throne of God and of the Lamb.
- The curse is no more.
- God's servants see His face.
- The city's gates are not shut.
- The KJV records a blessing associated with doing His commandments, right to the tree of life, and entrance through the gates.
- The final invitation offers the water of life freely.

### Boundary

Revelation 21–22 does not say that human beings independently regain access by their own power. The vision is framed by God's throne, the Lamb, God's dwelling, and the gift of life. The exact relationship between each image and the guarded Garden should be stated carefully rather than converted into a one-to-one architectural map.

## 11. The key question — Does Jesus merely show the way?

The answer must be divided by evidence level.

### Explicit answer from John 14:6

Jesus says, “I am the way.” He does not say only, “I will show you a way.” He joins Way, Truth, and Life and says that no one comes to the Father except by Him.

### Explicit answer from John 10

Jesus says, “I am the door,” and “by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved.” He also identifies Himself as the Good Shepherd who gives His life for the sheep.

### Explicit answer from Hebrews

Hebrews says that access to the holiest is by the blood of Jesus, through a new and living way He consecrated for us, and identifies Him as the great High Priest over the house of God.

### Theological synthesis

Taken together, these passages do not present Jesus as a detached signpost or merely an instructor who points toward a route independent of Himself. They identify His person and work as central to access to the Father and to life.

This conclusion does not require claiming that John 14:6 directly quotes Genesis 3:24. The canonical comparison is supported by the repeated subject of access and life, while the identity of Jesus as the Way rests on His explicit statement.

## 14. Findings

1. Genesis 2 names the tree of life as present in the Garden before the Fall.
2. Genesis 3:22–24 records the man's expulsion and the guarding of the way to the tree of life; it does not say the tree was destroyed.
3. Jesus explicitly identifies Himself as the door in John 10:7–9 and the Good Shepherd who gives His life in John 10:11.
4. Jesus explicitly identifies Himself as the Way, the Truth, and the Life in John 14:6 and says no one comes to the Father except by Him.
5. Hebrews 9 describes the first covenant sanctuary and restricted access, then contrasts it with Christ's entrance by His own blood.
6. Hebrews 10:19–22 explicitly describes boldness to enter the holiest by Jesus' blood, through a new and living way He consecrated.
7. Hebrews 10 does not explicitly quote Genesis 3:24. The comparison between the guarded way and the new and living way is a canonical and theological synthesis.
8. Revelation 2:7 promises the tree of life in the paradise of God.
9. Revelation 21–22 describes God's dwelling with His people, the water and tree of life, open gates, the removal of the curse, and God's servants seeing His face.
10. The KJV reading of Revelation 22:14 is retained; textual variants are acknowledged rather than silently harmonized.
11. The strongest direct answer to the central question comes from John 14:6: Jesus says He is the Way, not merely the one who points toward a way.
12. John 10 and Hebrews 9–10 reinforce that access and salvation are bound to Jesus' person and work.
13. The Genesis-to-Revelation connection is a canonical synthesis; it must not be misrepresented as a direct quotation or as proof that every image has identical meaning.
14. No GUA-W architecture change is proposed.
