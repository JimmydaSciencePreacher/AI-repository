# Study 0047 — John 3:3–8: Ye Must Be Born Again

**Status:** Initial investigation  
**Primary text:** John 3:3–8 (KJV)  
**Study question:** What does Jesus reveal to Nicodemus about the necessity, source, and operation of being born again?

## 1. Scripture under examination

> Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.  
> — John 3:3 (KJV)

> Jesus answered, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God.  
> — John 3:5 (KJV)

> That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.  
> — John 3:6 (KJV)

> Marvel not that I said unto thee, Ye must be born again.  
> — John 3:7 (KJV)

> The wind bloweth where it listeth, and thou hearest the sound thereof, but canst not tell whence it cometh, and whither it goeth: so is every one that is born of the Spirit.  
> — John 3:8 (KJV)

## 2. What the passage directly states

1. Jesus declares the new birth necessary: without it, a person cannot see the kingdom of God (verse 3).
2. Jesus distinguishes birth of water and Spirit from birth of flesh (verses 5–6).
3. Jesus states that flesh gives birth to flesh, while the Spirit gives birth to spirit (verse 6).
4. Jesus does not present the new birth as something Nicodemus can produce by natural understanding or human effort. He declares it as something that must occur.
5. Jesus uses the wind as an illustration: its sound can be heard, while its origin and destination are not determined by the hearer's observation (verse 8).
6. Jesus compares the one born of the Spirit with this illustration. The comparison must be read from the words Jesus actually gives; the illustration should not be expanded into claims the passage does not make.

## 3. The distinction Jesus makes

The passage identifies two different births and does not collapse them into one:

- Birth of the flesh: that which is born of flesh is flesh.
- Birth of the Spirit: that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.

The distinction originates in Jesus' statement, not in a framework supplied by the investigator. The passage places the source of spiritual birth with the Spirit. It does not describe the Spirit as a product of the person who receives this birth.

## 4. The wind illustration and the witness

Jesus identifies things that can be heard and things that remain beyond the hearer's determination. The sound of the wind is witnessed; the hearer does not thereby become the source of the wind or gain authority over its course.

This provides a limited observation about witnessing: a manifestation may be heard or observed without the witness originating or governing what is manifested.

**Boundary:** The wind is a natural phenomenon used by Jesus as an illustration. Atmospheric science can investigate wind, but that investigation does not, by itself, explain spiritual birth. The passage does not authorize the investigator to equate the Spirit with atmospheric wind or to turn the illustration into a scientific mechanism for regeneration.

## 5. Questions retained for further examination

- How does John 3:3–8 relate to John 1:12–13, where the birth is described as being of God rather than of blood, the will of the flesh, or the will of man?
- What does the immediate context of John 3 establish about the meaning of “water” in verse 5? This question remains open here; no interpretation is to be inserted without examining the surrounding passage and relevant Scripture.
- How does the passage distinguish seeing the kingdom (verse 3) from entering the kingdom (verse 5)?
- What does the comparison with the wind establish—and what does it leave unexplained?
- How does this passage relate to the Father's giving and the Son's receiving language elsewhere in John's Gospel?

## 6. Triple Constraint review

**Biblical compliance:** The observations above are tied to the wording of John 3:3–8 (KJV). Questions not settled by these verses remain questions.

**Scientific coherence:** The wind illustration refers to an observable natural phenomenon. Its physical investigation must remain distinct from the spiritual reality Jesus is describing. No scientific mechanism for the new birth is asserted.

**GUA-W consistency:** The witness may register what is manifested without claiming to be its originating cause. This is a comparison supported by the passage's illustration, not a new architectural placement or an addition to GUA-W.

## 7. Finding at this stage

Jesus teaches that being born again is necessary and that the birth He describes is of the Spirit, not an achievement produced by flesh. The wind illustration distinguishes witnessing an observable manifestation from determining or originating its source and course.

This study does **not** assign a new component, layer, operation, or position within GUA-W. The passage is recorded first. Its placement is not to be forced.

## 8. Unresolved

The meaning of “water” in John 3:5, the relation between seeing and entering the kingdom, and the fuller relationship between John 3:3–8 and John 1:12–13 require further Scripture examination.

**Method retained:** Observe the passage. Preserve what it says. Keep interpretation distinct from direct statement. Apply the Triple Constraint Law. Do not make the witness the Author, and do not make the investigator the architect.
