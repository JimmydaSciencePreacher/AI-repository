## 4. The Four Questions

The Four Questions are the recurring inquiry method for this study:

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

Identify the question genuinely required by the matter under examination. Questions must arise from the text, evidence, and distinctions present—not from a preferred answer.

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

Consult the relevant established GUA-W sources. Distinguish what those sources state from explanation, interpretation, analogy, and proposed correspondence. Do not attribute an unsupported statement to GUA-W.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

Examine the Scripture, evidence, or phenomenon itself. Record what is present before explaining it. Preserve context, sequence, scope, and limitations.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

Examine what the act and result of witnessing reveal about the Witness, including the conditions and limits of registration, perception, interpretation, and testimony where relevant. Do not confuse the Witness with the Author or with the thing witnessed.

The Four Questions are not four architecture layers, runtime stages, or predetermined answers. They guide and correct the investigation recursively as evidence develops.

## 5. Triple Constraint Law

Every material claim considered for acceptance within this study is examined under all three constraints together:

1. **Biblical compliance** — consistency with the KJV Genesis 1–2 foundation and relevant Scriptural context.
2. **Scientific coherence** — accurate treatment of the evidence, methods, assumptions, scope, and limitations of the relevant discipline.
3. **GUA-W consistency** — preservation of the established Author → Word → Creation → Witness hierarchy and the completed architecture's governing distinctions and relationships.

The three constraints are jointly required. They are not a score, ranking, or sequence.

- A material claim that fails any one constraint is not admitted as an accepted GUA-W conclusion.
- State the specific reason for non-admission and the constraint involved.
- If evidence is incomplete, conflicting, or insufficient, preserve the matter as **Unresolved**.
- Do not turn an unresolved question into a conclusion through repetition, analogy, wording, or assumption.
- Scientific coherence is not the same as scientific confirmation of Scripture.
- Biblical compliance does not excuse inaccurate scientific representation.
- GUA-W consistency does not excuse unsupported claims about what GUA-W says.
- A scientific description does not become an authority over the Author or Scripture.

## 7. Cross-disciplinary investigation method

The aim is to investigate the unity and relationships of the sciences within the One Creation, using GUA-W as the completed map.

For each proposed connection:

1. Identify the discipline, question, and specific finding being examined.
2. Retrieve the source material and identify what was observed, measured, derived, modeled, or experimentally established.
3. Preserve the discipline's own definitions, methods, assumptions, scope, and uncertainty.
4. Separate the disciplinary result from the source author's interpretation and from our own interpretation.
5. Examine relevant findings from connected disciplines where a real relationship is indicated.
6. Ask what relationship, dependency, common order, boundary, or distinction the evidence supports.
7. Locate that relationship in the completed GUA-W where the established architecture and evidence support the placement.
8. Apply the Triple Constraint Law to each material claim.
9. Record counterevidence, alternative explanations, limitations, and unresolved questions.
10. Identify the next discriminating question arising from the evidence.

**Investigation sequence:**  
Disciplinary question → source evidence and method → result and scope → interpretations and alternatives → Triple Constraint examination → relationship within the completed GUA-W, if supported → recorded limits and next question.

This is a research procedure, not a new GUA-W layer or runtime sequence.

### Division of work

This study focuses on the map-level work:

- recognizing unity across differentiated scientific domains;

- comparing terminology carefully without assuming that similar words mean identical things;

## 8. Statement and evidence labels

Use labels where they help preserve the status of a claim:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **TEXT STATES** | What the cited KJV passage or source explicitly says |
| **TEXTUAL OBSERVATION** | A feature, sequence, distinction, or wording directly observed in the text |
| **DIRECT OBSERVATION** | What the examined evidence or phenomenon directly presents |
| **DISCIPLINARY FINDING** | What a discipline's evidence or valid derivation establishes within its stated scope |
| **INTERPRETATION** | An explanation of the text, evidence, or finding |
| **SUPPORTED INFERENCE** | A conclusion reasonably supported by identified evidence, but not stated directly by the source |
| **CORRESPONDENCE** | A relationship supported across domains or sources |
| **ANALOGY** | A limited comparison; not proof of identity or a shared mechanism |
| **GUA-W RECOGNITION** | The supported significance or placement of a relationship within the completed map |
| **NOT ADMITTED** | A claim that fails a stated constraint or lacks sufficient support for acceptance |
| **UNRESOLVED** | A matter the available evidence does not yet decide |

A label never substitutes for the underlying examination.
