## 1. Question under examination

Genesis 2:5 follows the declaration that the heavens, earth, and all their host were finished (Genesis 2:1), God's ending and resting from His work (2:2), His blessing and sanctifying of the seventh day (2:3), and the *toledot* heading (2:4).

The immediate question carried forward from the Four-Question Study is:

**What does Genesis 2:5 mean by describing the plant of the field before it was in the earth and the herb of the field before it grew? Why does the verse give the absence of rain and the absence of a man to till the ground as its explanation? How does this description relate to Genesis 1:11–12, where the earth brought forth vegetation?**

The purpose is not to reopen or revise the completed GUA-W. It is to examine the KJV text and recognize what the passage contributes to the unified Genesis 1–2 account.

## 2. KJV text and immediate context

**Genesis 1:11–12 (KJV)**

>

**Genesis 2:1–5 (KJV)**

>

**Genesis 2:6–7 (KJV)**

> But there went up a mist from the earth, and watered the whole face of the ground.
>
> And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.

The immediate context must be preserved. Genesis 1:11–12 records the earth bringing forth vegetation. Genesis 2:5 then speaks specifically of the plant and herb **of the field**, describes a not-yet condition, and gives two stated circumstances: rain had not been caused upon the earth, and there was no man to till the ground. Verse 6 describes watering by a mist from the earth. Verse 7 records the LORD God's formation of man from the dust of the ground and the breath of life.

## 3. Direct textual observations

1. Genesis 2:5 speaks of “every plant of the field” and “every herb of the field.” The repeated qualifier “of the field” is part of the wording and must not be omitted.
2. The verse describes these field plants and herbs in relation to their being in the earth and growing. The KJV wording is retained rather than silently replaced with a modern paraphrase.
3. The verse gives two reasons or background conditions: the LORD God had not caused rain upon the earth, and there was not a man to till the ground.
4. Genesis 2:5 does not say that no vegetation of any kind existed anywhere. Genesis 1:11–12 has already recorded the earth bringing forth grass, seed-yielding herb, and fruit trees.
5. Genesis 2:6 distinguishes the watering described as a “mist” rising from the earth from the “rain” named in verse 5. The text uses different words; the Witness must not simply rename one as the other.
6. Genesis 2:7 immediately records the formation of man from the dust of the ground. The sequence places the absence of a man to till the ground before the account of man's formation.
7. Genesis 2:15 later records the man being placed in the Garden “to dress it and to keep it.” The later assignment is not to be imported as though it were already stated in verse 5.
8. The same Hebrew root commonly transliterated *‘ābad* is used for working/tilling in Genesis 2:5 and for the man's work in Genesis 2:15. This is a lexical connection; it does not alone prove that the tasks, locations, or phases are identical.
9. Genesis 2:5 does not literally state that God was holding seeds, storing seeds, or waiting to release seeds. Those formulations must not be presented as the verse's wording.
10. The passage does not provide a complete botanical classification, meteorological mechanism, or chronology for every plant on earth.

**Direct finding:** Genesis 2:5 describes a particular field-related not-yet condition and names the absence of rain and the absence of a man to work the ground. It does not erase the vegetation already recorded in Genesis 1:11–12.

## 4. Language and contextual examination

The GUA-W Hebrew audit of Genesis 2:4–6 records the following relevant cautions:

- The Hebrew noun *śîaḥ*, rendered “plant” in the KJV, is rare; proposed renderings include shrub, bush, or plant. Its exact botanical category is not established.
- The term *‘ēśeb*, rendered “herb”, is used elsewhere in Genesis 1:11–12 and 1:29–30. Its precise referent in Genesis 2:5 may be field vegetation or cultivated growth, but the exact botanical division is not settled.
- The term *ṭerem* carries a “before/not yet” sense. The verse therefore describes a condition in which the specified field growth had not yet appeared or grown at the relevant point in the account.
- The word *māṭār* refers to rain. The text explicitly names the absence of rain; it does not say that all water was absent.
- The verb *‘ābad* has a range including work and service. “Till” is appropriate to the ground in Genesis 2:5; the same root occurs in the Garden-work statement in Genesis 2:15.
- The precise meaning of *’ēd*, rendered “mist” in Genesis 2:6, is disputed. The KJV rendering is preserved, while a definitive modern hydrological identification is not asserted.

These lexical notes clarify the limits of the wording. They do not authorize a modern taxonomic classification or a physical mechanism absent from the text.
