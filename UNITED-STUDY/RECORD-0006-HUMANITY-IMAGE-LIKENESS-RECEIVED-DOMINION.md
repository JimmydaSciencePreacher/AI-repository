## 1. Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:26 establish when God says, “Let us make man in our image, after our likeness,” and appoints humanity to have dominion? What does this introduce that is distinct from the preceding accounts of vegetation and other living creatures?

The investigation must preserve:
- God as the speaker;
- the plural wording “Let us” and “our”;
- the singular collective term “man”;
- the terms “image” and “likeness”;
- the transition to “let them have dominion”;
- the named domains over which dominion is assigned.

Genesis 1:27–28 must be consulted as immediate context because the next verses record the creative act, identify male and female, and state the blessing and commission. They clarify the sequence without making Genesis 1:26 say more than it says.

## 2. KJV text and immediate context

### Genesis 1:26

> And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.

**TEXT STATES:** God speaks. The statement uses “Let us make man”, “in our image”, and “after our likeness”. It then says, “let them have dominion” over the listed domains.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The verse moves from God's statement about making man to the appointment of dominion. The wording moves from “man” to “them”. The list includes fish, fowl, cattle, all the earth, and every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.

The verse does not say that man makes himself, originates the appointment, or becomes the Creator. Dominion is assigned in the context of God's statement.

### Genesis 1:27

> So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.

**TEXT STATES:** The narrative explicitly says God created man in His own image. The image statement is repeated. The verse identifies male and female within the creation of humanity.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** Verse 27 confirms God's creative action and repeats “image”. It does not repeat the word “likeness”. That difference is observable, but it does not by itself prove that image and likeness are two separate technical operations or unrelated attributes.

### Genesis 1:28

> And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.

**TEXT STATES:** God blesses them and speaks to them. The instructions include fruitfulness, multiplication, replenishing the earth, subduing it, and dominion over the listed living creatures.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The dominion mentioned in verse 26 is followed by blessing and direct instruction in verse 28. The authority is received from God; the text does not describe humanity as independently originating it.

## 3. The transition within Genesis 1

The preceding accounts describe domains and creatures brought forth or made under God's command. Genesis 1:26 introduces a distinctive statement concerning humanity:

| Feature | Earlier creation accounts | Humanity, Genesis 1:26–28 |
|---|---|---|
| Divine speech | God commands and speaks. | God says, “Let us make man”. |
| Creation | Vegetation and living creatures are brought forth or made. | God explicitly creates man in His image (1:27). |
| Description | The text names domains and kinds. | The text uses image and likeness language. |
| Appointment | Creatures receive blessing and multiplication instructions in their respective accounts. | Humanity receives blessing, fruitfulness, replenishment, subduing, and dominion. |
| Authority | No creature is presented as the originating Author. | Humanity is assigned dominion; it does not become the originating Author. |

**SUPPORTED SYNTHESIS:** Humanity is not introduced as the originator of Creation, but as a created participant given a distinctive description and an appointment within Creation. The appointment is real, but it is received. The text places humanity within the created order and assigns responsibility over specified domains.

This is a distinction of position and appointment in the narrative. It does not yet define every aspect of the image, likeness, or dominion.

## 4. “Image” and “likeness”

The Hebrew terms associated with the KJV wording are:
- “image”: צֶלֶם (*tselem*), Strong's H6754;
- “likeness”: דְּמוּת (*demuth*), Strong's H1823.

Both occur in Genesis 1:26. Genesis 1:27 repeats “image” but not “likeness”.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The two words are distinguishable lexical forms, and both appear in the statement of verse 26. Their presence does not by itself establish two independent technical functions.

**INTERPRETIVE CAUTION:** Do not reduce image-bearing to physical resemblance alone, intelligence, consciousness, biological form, or a technical GUA-W component. Nor does the wording say that humanity becomes God or shares God's status as Original Cause.

Genesis 5:1–3 and Genesis 9:6 are relevant canonical passages for further examination of image and likeness. Their wording and contexts should be studied before a comprehensive theological definition is admitted.

**UNRESOLVED:** The full Scriptural relationship between image and likeness, and the precise meaning of each term in Genesis 1:26, require broader lexical and canonical investigation.

## 5. “Let us make man”: preserve the plural wording

Genesis 1:26 says “Let us make man in our image, after our likeness.” Genesis 1:27 then says, “So God created man in his own image”.

Both statements must be retained. The plural wording of verse 26 is not to be silently rewritten, and the explicit singular attribution of creation to God in verse 27 is not to be weakened.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** Genesis 1:26 uses plural pronouns in God's speech. Genesis 1:27 identifies God as the Creator. The immediate passage does not provide a complete explanation of the plural wording.

**UNRESOLVED:** The identity and full theological significance of “us” must be examined through broader canonical context. This record does not settle the question by choosing a theological explanation in advance.

## 6. Dominion: received authority within Creation

Genesis 1:26 assigns dominion over named domains. Genesis 1:28 repeats dominion and adds “subdue it”, within the blessing and instructions.

The passage distinguishes:
- God as Creator and speaker;
- humanity as created;
- dominion as an appointment given by God;
- the created domains over which that dominion is exercised.

**TEXT STATES:** God says “let them have dominion” (1:26), and God later says unto them “subdue it” and “have dominion” (1:28).

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** Dominion is received authority, not originating authority. Humanity is appointed to exercise authority within Creation, not to become the source of Creation or the authority that redefines its Author.

**LIMIT:** Genesis 1:26 alone does not specify every boundary, practice, or responsibility associated with dominion. Genesis 2:15 and other relevant passages must be examined before the nature of humanity's responsibility is fully described. The verse does not authorize treating every historical or present human exercise of power as faithful dominion.
