## 1. Question under examination

What does Genesis 1:31 establish when God sees everything He has made and declares it “very good”?

The investigation examines:
- the scope of “every thing that he had made”;
- the distinction between the earlier assessments of “good” and this collective assessment of “very good”;
- the relationship between God's making, God's seeing, and God's assessment;
- the closing designation of the sixth day;
- the transition from the sixth-day assessment in Genesis 1:31 to the statements concerning finishing, ending, and rest in Genesis 2:1–3.

The central question is not whether a human observer or scientific discipline can independently assign the same assessment. It is what the KJV text says about the Author's own assessment of the work He made, and what distinctions must be preserved when that statement is witnessed and reported.

## 2. KJV text

### Genesis 1:31

> And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

**TEXT STATES:**
- God saw.
- The object of His seeing is “every thing that he had made”.
- The verse says, “behold”.
- The assessment is “it was very good”.
- The evening and the morning designate the sixth day.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** God is identified as both Maker and the One who sees and assesses what He has made. The verse does not assign the assessment to humanity, to the created things themselves, or to a later scientific observer.

The KJV wording “every thing” is collective. The verse does not restrict the assessment to the last creature or to humanity alone. It points back to the work described in the preceding account.

## 3. Immediate sequence: from the parts to the whole

The sixth-day context includes:
- Genesis 1:25 — God makes the land creatures after their kinds, and sees that it is good.
- Genesis 1:26–27 — God speaks concerning man and creates humanity in His image, male and female.
- Genesis 1:28 — God blesses humanity and gives its commission.
- Genesis 1:29 — God identifies provision for humanity.
- Genesis 1:30 — God identifies provision for the other living creatures named.
- Genesis 1:31 — God sees everything He has made and declares it very good.

Earlier assessments in Genesis 1 include:
- Genesis 1:4 — God sees the light, that it is good.
- Genesis 1:10 — God sees that the dry land and gathered waters are good.
- Genesis 1:12 — God sees the vegetation is good.
- Genesis 1:18 — God sees the appointed work of the lights is good.
- Genesis 1:21 — God sees the living creatures are good.
- Genesis 1:25 — God sees the land creatures are good.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The earlier assessments are attached to particular parts or results within the account. Genesis 1:31 gathers the scope into the phrase “every thing that he had made” and gives the collective assessment “very good”.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** The narrative moves from descriptions and assessments of particular created works to a collective assessment of the whole work under consideration.

**LIMIT:** The text does not say that the earlier works were defective until verse 31. Nor does it define “very good” as a numerical score, a modern scientific measurement, or a technical description of every mechanism and relationship in Creation.

## 4. “God saw” — the One who assesses

The verse's wording preserves a clear relationship:

1. God made the things under consideration.
2. God saw everything that He had made.
3. God declared the whole “very good”.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The Maker is the One who sees and assesses His work. The assessment is not presented as an independent verdict produced by the created work or by the human Witness.

This preserves the governing distinction:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

The Author is not the Creation. The created work is not its own originating cause. The Witness may register and report what is given, but the Witness does not become the Author or the source of the Author's assessment.

**SUPPORTED GUA-W RECOGNITION:** Genesis 1:31 is consistent with the established Author–Creation distinction. The verse's immediate significance is a textual relationship: Maker, made work, seeing, and assessment. This record does not assign that relationship a new GUA-W layer, operator, runtime stage, or State term.

## 5. “Behold, it was very good”

The word “behold” calls attention to the assessment that follows. The KJV's “very good” must be retained rather than flattened into the earlier repeated wording “good”.

**TEXT STATES:** The collective assessment is “very good”.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The verse uses a strengthened expression in its collective assessment. The earlier statements of “good” and the final statement of “very good” are related, but the wording and scope are not identical.

The Hebrew expression commonly represented as *ṭôḇ meʾōḏ* (טוֹב מְאֹד) is rendered “very good” in the KJV. The adverbial intensifier *meʾōḏ* has a broad range including “very” or “exceedingly”, depending on context. This lexical note supports the ordinary force of the KJV rendering; it does not create a separate technical doctrine.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** The collective assessment is emphatic. It is God's assessment of the whole work He has made.

**LIMIT:** “Very good” is not identified here as a quantitative measurement, a scientific test result, or a technical formula for perfection in every later philosophical sense. The verse does not enumerate every possible implication of the phrase. Those broader claims require additional Scriptural examination and must not be smuggled into the wording.

## 6. The sixth day and the transition into Genesis 2

Genesis 1:31 closes:

> And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

This is the recurring day-designation formula used at the close of the preceding days. Here it identifies the sixth day.

The following verses must be kept distinct:

| Passage | KJV statement | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 1:31 | God sees everything He made, declares it very good, and the sixth day is designated. | Collective assessment and sixth-day closure. |
| Genesis 2:1 | The heavens, earth, and all their host are finished. | Statement concerning the finished whole. |
| Genesis 2:2 | God ends His work and rests on the seventh day. | Ending of work and seventh-day rest. |
| Genesis 2:3 | God blesses and sanctifies the seventh day. | Blessing and sanctification of the day. |

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** Genesis 1:31 does not say that God rested on the sixth day. The rest statement appears in Genesis 2:2 and is explicitly associated with the seventh day.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** Genesis 1:31 closes the sixth-day account with the collective assessment; Genesis 2:1–3 continues by describing the finishing of the heavens, earth, and all their host, God's ending of His work, His rest, and His blessing and sanctifying of the seventh day.

**LIMIT:** Assessment, finishing, ending, rest, blessing, and sanctification must not be treated as interchangeable words or collapsed into one undifferentiated event. Their exact relationship is examined in the following Genesis 2 records.
