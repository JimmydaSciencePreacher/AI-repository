## 1. Question under examination

What does Genesis 2:2 establish when it says that on the seventh day God ended His work which He had made and rested from all His work?

The investigation examines:
- “on the seventh day”;
- “God ended his work”;
- “which he had made”;
- “he rested”;
- “from all his work which he had made”;
- the relationship to Genesis 2:1;
- whether divine rest means exhaustion, cessation of the creative work, or cessation of all divine activity;
- how later Scripture describes God's continuing work and the sustaining of Creation.

**Central question:** What work does the passage say God ended and rested from, and what does it not say about God's continuing activity?

## 2. KJV text and immediate context

### Genesis 2:1

> Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them.

### Genesis 2:2

> And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.

### Genesis 2:3

> And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** Verse 1 says the heavens, earth, and all their host were finished. Verse 2 says God ended His work which He had made and rested on the seventh day from all His work. Verse 3 says God blessed and sanctified the seventh day because He had rested from His work.

These statements are connected. Their terms must not be collapsed.

## 3. “On the seventh day God ended his work”

The KJV wording is explicit and must be preserved.

The Hebrew clause begins וַיְכַל אֱלֹהִים בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי (*wayḵal ʾĕlōhîm bayyôm haššəḇîʿî*).

- וַיְכַל (*wayḵal*) — “and he finished/ended”, from כָּלָה (*kālāh*, Strong's H3615).
- אֱלֹהִים (*ʾĕlōhîm*) — God.
- בַּיּוֹם (*bayyôm*) — in/on the day.
- הַשְּׁבִיעִי (*haššəḇîʿî*) — the seventh.

The preposition ב (*b*) is expressed in the Hebrew as “in/on”. The KJV renders it “on”. Some modern translations render the first clause “by the seventh day”, interpreting the completion as already accomplished by the time the seventh day arrived. The NET notes acknowledge that the Hebrew is literally “on/in the seventh day” while explaining the translation “by”. The Hebrew wording and translation decision must both be reported accurately.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The KJV states, “on the seventh day God ended his work”.

**TRANSLATION OBSERVATION:** “By the seventh day” is an interpretive rendering adopted by some translators to express the relationship between Genesis 2:1's finished condition and the seventh-day statement.

**LIMIT:** Do not silently rewrite the KJV, call it erroneous without adequate evidence, or claim the translation question is settled merely because one rendering fits a preferred chronology.

Genesis 2:1 and 2:2 must be read together:
- Verse 1: the heavens, earth, and their host were finished.
- Verse 2: God ended His work which He had made and rested from all His work.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** The passage presents the created work as completed and identifies the seventh day with God's ending and resting from that work. The exact nuance of the temporal expression in the first clause remains a translation question worth preserving.

## 4. “His work which he had made”

The verse identifies the work as God's own work and repeats the phrase “which he had made”.

Verse 2 uses the reference to His work twice:
- “his work which he had made”;
- “all his work which he had made”.

Verse 3 again identifies it as work “which God created and made”.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The work belongs to God as its Maker. The created work is not identified as its own originating cause.

**GUA-W RECOGNITION:** This is consistent with the Author–Word–Creation–Witness distinction. The Author is the source; the work is what He made. The Witness reports the relationship and does not take the Author's place.

No new GUA-W layer or operator is established by the repeated phrase.

## 5. “And he rested” — שָׁבַת (*shābat*)

The Hebrew verb is וַיִּשְׁבֹּת (*wayyišbōt*), from שָׁבַת (*shābat*, Strong's H7673).

The root is associated with ceasing, desisting, or resting. In Genesis 2:2–3 it describes God's cessation from the work identified in the passage. Some translations therefore render the verb “ceased”; the KJV renders it “rested”.

The distinction matters: the word does not require the idea that God was exhausted and needed recovery.

### Isaiah 40:28 (KJV)

> Hast thou not known? hast thou not heard, that the everlasting God, the LORD, the Creator of the ends of the earth, fainteth not, neither is weary? there is no searching of his understanding.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** Isaiah explicitly says the Creator does not faint or become weary.

**SUPPORTED CONCLUSION:** Genesis 2:2 must not be interpreted as saying God rested because He was physically or mentally exhausted. That explanation conflicts with the explicit Scriptural description of the Creator in Isaiah 40:28.

### Exodus 20:11 (KJV)

> For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it.

Exodus connects God's six-day making, seventh-day rest, and the blessing and hallowing of the Sabbath.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** The Creation rest becomes the stated foundation for the Sabbath pattern in Exodus 20:11.

**LIMIT:** Genesis 2:2 itself does not give the full later Sabbath legislation. Exodus supplies that additional context.

## 6. Does God's rest mean He stopped all work?

This question must be handled carefully.

Genesis 2:2 says God rested from all His work which He had made. The immediate context is the work of Creation described in Genesis 1–2.

The verse does not say:
- God ceased to exist or be present;
- God became inactive in every respect;
- God ceased to govern Creation;
- God ceased to sustain what He had created;
- God became incapable of further action.

These are not statements found in Genesis 2:2.

### John 5:17 (KJV)

> But Jesus answered them, My Father worketh hitherto, and I work.

Jesus' statement prevents us from turning the seventh-day rest into the universal claim that God performs no work after Creation.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** Jesus speaks of the Father's continuing work.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** God's cessation from the Creation work in Genesis 2 must be distinguished from the continuing divine work described in John 5:17.

### Hebrews 1:3 (KJV)

> Who being the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person, and upholding all things by the word of his power, when he had by himself purged our sins, sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high;

This passage identifies the Son as upholding all things by the word of His power.

### Colossians 1:17 (KJV)

> And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.

These passages establish that the Son's relationship to Creation is not merely a past event. He upholds all things; by Him all things consist.

**CANONICAL RECOGNITION:** The cessation of the work of making described in Genesis 2:2 is not equivalent to the cessation of divine sustaining and governance. Hebrews 1:3 and Colossians 1:17 explicitly speak of the Son's sustaining relationship to all things.

**LIMIT:** Genesis 2:2 does not itself explain the mechanism of sustaining or provide a technical account of divine governance. Those details must not be inserted into the verse.

## 7. Distinguishing the statements

| Passage | Statement | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 2:1 | The heavens, earth, and host were finished. | Finished condition of the created whole. |
| Genesis 2:2 | God ended His work which He had made. | God's ending of His Creation work. |
| Genesis 2:2 | He rested from all His work. | Cessation/rest in relation to that work. |
| Genesis 2:3 | God blessed the seventh day. | Blessing of the day. |
| Genesis 2:3 | God sanctified it. | Setting the day apart as holy. |
| John 5:17 | The Father works hitherto. | Continuing divine work. |
| Hebrews 1:3 | The Son upholds all things. | Sustaining relationship to Creation. |
| Colossians 1:17 | By Him all things consist. | Continuing dependence of all things upon Him. |

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** Completion of the Creation work and continued divine sustaining are not contradictory categories. The first concerns the work of making described in Genesis; the latter is explicitly witnessed in the New Testament.

This distinction is important, but it must not be converted into an invented physical mechanism or a new GUA-W stage.
