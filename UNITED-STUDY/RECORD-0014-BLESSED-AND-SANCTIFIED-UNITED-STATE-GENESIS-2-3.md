## 1. Question under examination

What does Genesis 2:3 establish when God blesses and sanctifies the seventh day because He rested from all His work which He created and made?

The investigation examines:
- “God blessed the seventh day”;
- “and sanctified it”;
- “because that in it he had rested”;
- “from all his work which God created and made”;
- the relationship between the completed Creation work and the day God set apart;
- the connection to the later Sabbath command;
- the distinction between the completed Creation mapped by GUA-W and the map itself;
- how the completed map can aid investigation of relationships among modern scientific disciplines without replacing their evidence or methods.

**Central question:** What does the verse establish about God's blessing and sanctifying of the seventh day, and how does that fit the completed-work foundation that GUA-W maps?

## 2. KJV text and immediate sequence

### Genesis 2:1

> Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them.

### Genesis 2:2

> And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.

### Genesis 2:3

> And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:**
- Verse 1 declares the heavens, earth, and all their host finished.
- Verse 2 says God ended His work which He had made and rested from all His work.
- Verse 3 says God blessed the seventh day and sanctified it, giving His rest from all His created and made work as the reason.

The passage gives related but distinct statements. Finishing, ending, resting, blessing, and sanctifying must not be collapsed into one word or one undifferentiated action.

## 3. “God blessed the seventh day”

God is the subject. The seventh day is the object of His blessing.

**TEXT STATES:** God blessed the seventh day.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The blessing is attributed to God, not to the day itself, humanity, or a later observer. The verse does not itemize every effect of the blessing.

Exodus 20:11 later says:

> For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it.

**CANONICAL CONNECTION:** Exodus explicitly connects the Creation account, God's seventh-day rest, and the Sabbath day given in the commandment.

**LIMIT:** Genesis 2:3 itself is not phrased as a command to Adam or to all humanity to observe a weekly Sabbath. The later command and its covenantal context must be examined in their own setting.

## 4. “And sanctified it”

The verse says God sanctified the seventh day. The Hebrew root is קָדַשׁ (*qādash*, Strong's H6942), associated with being holy or setting apart as holy. The form in Genesis 2:3 is וַיְקַדֵּשׁ (*wayqaddēš*), “and he sanctified”.

**TEXT STATES:** God sanctified the seventh day.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The same object—the seventh day—is the object of both “blessed” and “sanctified”. God is the One who performs both actions.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** The day is set apart by God's own action. Its holiness is not self-originated and is not conferred by a human observer.

**LIMIT:** The lexical meaning does not, by itself, specify every later Sabbath obligation or every theological application of sanctification. Those require the relevant Scriptural passages.

## 5. “Because that in it he had rested”

The word “because” supplies the reason given by the verse for God's blessing and sanctifying of the seventh day: He had rested in it from all His work.

This preserves the direction of the stated relationship:

1. God ended His Creation work.
2. God rested from that work on the seventh day.
3. God blessed the seventh day.
4. God sanctified the seventh day.
5. The verse expressly connects the blessing and sanctification to His rest.

Isaiah 40:28 says the Creator does not faint or become weary. Therefore, the rest is not recovery from exhaustion.

John 5:17 says, “My Father worketh hitherto, and I work.” Hebrews 1:3 describes the Son as “upholding all things by the word of his power”, and Colossians 1:17 says, “by him all things consist”.

**CANONICAL RECOGNITION:** God's rest from the Creation work must not be confused with the cessation of all divine activity or sustaining. The completed Creation remains dependent upon its Author; the New Testament explicitly speaks of the Son upholding all things.
