## 1. Question under examination

What does Genesis 2:8–9 establish about the LORD God planting a garden eastward in Eden, placing the man He had formed there, and causing trees to grow out of the ground? What distinctions does the text make between Eden and the Garden, the general provision of trees and the two named trees, and God's actions and man's received place?

## 2. KJV text and immediate context

### Genesis 2:7

> And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.

### Genesis 2:8

> And the LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden; and there he put the man whom he had formed.

### Genesis 2:9

> And out of the ground made the LORD God to grow every tree that is pleasant to the sight, and good for food; the tree of life also in the midst of the garden, and the tree of knowledge of good and evil.

### Genesis 2:10

> And a river went out of Eden to water the garden; and from thence it was parted, and became into four heads.

The immediate narrative gives these distinct statements:
1. God formed man and breathed into him the breath of life.
2. God planted a garden eastward in Eden.
3. God put there the man whom He had formed.
4. God caused trees to grow out of the ground.
5. The trees are described as pleasant to sight and good for food.
6. The tree of life and the tree of knowledge of good and evil are named.
7. The next verse describes a river going out of Eden to water the Garden.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The LORD God is the acting subject of the planting, placing, and causing-to-grow statements. The man is the one placed; the trees are what God causes to grow.

The sequence is narrated, but the verses do not provide a complete calendar, duration, or physical mechanism for every action.

## 3. Genesis 2:8 — “The LORD God planted a garden”

The verb translated “planted” is נָטַע (*nāṭaʿ*), meaning to plant or establish by planting.

The subject is the LORD God. The object is “a garden”.

**TEXT STATES:** The LORD God planted a garden.

**LIMIT:** The verb does not specify a modern horticultural procedure, tools, dimensions, or a complete landscape design.

The word for garden is גַּן (*gan*). It identifies the planted place but does not, by itself, define its exact size, boundaries, or full arrangement.

### “Eastward in Eden”

The expression translated “eastward” is מִקֶּדֶם (*miqqedem*). Its sense is context-dependent, with associations including east, the front, or antiquity. The KJV renders it directionally as “eastward” here.

Eden is עֵדֶן (*ʿēḏen*), the named setting in which the Garden is placed.

**DIRECT OBSERVATION:** The verse describes a garden “eastward in Eden”. Its wording distinguishes the Garden from the named setting Eden.

Genesis 2:10 further says that a river went out of Eden to water the Garden. This supports retaining the distinction between the named setting and the Garden within it.

**NOT ESTABLISHED:** The verse does not give coordinates, distances, modern country names, the Garden's exact boundaries, or Eden's complete geographical extent.

**UNRESOLVED:** The precise geographical reference for “eastward”, the location of Eden, and the modern identification of the Garden, if any.

## 4. Genesis 2:8 — “There he put the man whom he had formed”

The verb translated “put” is from שׂוּם (*śîm*), meaning put, set, or place according to context.

The LORD God is the subject. The man is the object. “There” refers to the Garden just described.

The phrase “whom he had formed” points back to Genesis 2:7 and its verb יָצַר (*yāṣar*), to form or fashion.

**TEXT STATES:**
- God put the man in the Garden.
- The man placed there is the man God had formed.

**SUPPORTED OBSERVATION:** The man does not plant the Garden or place himself there. God is the subject of both actions.

**LIMIT:** Genesis 2:8 does not say that the man was formed inside the Garden. It does not state the elapsed time between formation, planting, and placement, nor does it describe every event between them.

Genesis 2:15 later describes God taking the man and putting him into the Garden to dress and keep it. The later verse supplies the purpose of that placement; that purpose should not be imported as though it were fully stated in Genesis 2:8 itself.

## 5. Genesis 2:9 — God causes the trees to grow

The verb translated “made ... to grow” is וַיַּצְמַח (*wayyaṣmaḥ*), from צָמַח (*ṣāmaḥ*), to sprout or grow. The form is causative: God causes the trees to grow.

The phrase “out of the ground” uses אֲדָמָה (*ʾăḏāmâ*), the same ground term present in the account of man's formation from the dust of the ground in Genesis 2:7.

**TEXT STATES:** The LORD God caused the trees to grow out of the ground.

**SUPPORTED TEXTUAL CONNECTION:** The same ground word appears in the adjacent accounts of man's formation and the trees' growth.

**LIMIT:** Shared use of *ʾăḏāmâ* does not make human formation and plant growth the same process, nor does it establish a shared physical mechanism.

The verse does not give a botanical growth mechanism, timescale, or modern biological explanation. It attributes the growth to the LORD God.

## 6. The trees — general description and named distinctions

The verse first describes “every tree” as:
- pleasant to the sight;
- good for food.

The Hebrew expressions are:
- נֶחְמָד לְמַרְאֶה (*neḥmād le-marʾeh*) — desirable or pleasant to sight.
- טוֹב לְמַאֲכָל (*ṭōḇ le-maʾăkāl*) — good or suitable for food.

These are two distinct qualities in the description: visual pleasantness and food suitability.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The verse does not reduce the trees to one attribute. It names both appearance and food.

**LIMIT:** Visual pleasantness is not identical to moral goodness, and food suitability does not override the specific command that God gives later in Genesis 2:16–17.

### The tree of life

עֵץ הַחַיִּים (*ʿēṣ haḥayyîm*) — “the tree of life”.

Genesis 2:9 explicitly locates the tree of life “in the midst of the garden”.

**TEXT STATES:** The tree of life is named and located in the midst of the Garden.

Genesis 3:22–24 later refers to the tree of life in connection with living for ever and the guarding of access after the Fall. Revelation 22:1–2 also includes the tree of life in the vision of the river and the renewed order.

**LIMIT:** Genesis 2:9 alone does not explain every aspect of the tree's operation, the conditions of access, or its full canonical significance.

### The tree of knowledge of good and evil

עֵץ הַדַּעַת טוֹב וָרָע (*ʿēṣ haddaʿat ṭōḇ wā-rāʿ*) — “the tree of the knowledge of good and evil”.

The name refers to knowledge of good and evil. It is not “the tree of evil”.

Genesis 2:9 names the tree but does not yet state the command concerning it. Genesis 2:16–17 supplies the command; Genesis 3 narrates the later encounter and eating.

**TEXT STATES:** The tree of knowledge of good and evil is named in the Garden account.

**LIMIT:** Verse 9 does not fully explain the nature of the knowledge, the tree's operation, or the later human response. The command and Fall account must be examined in their own textual sequence.

### Location distinction

The verse explicitly says that the tree of life is “in the midst of the garden”. It does not separately specify the precise position of the tree of knowledge in the same words.

Both trees belong to the Garden description, but the location wording must be reported accurately rather than expanded beyond what the verse says.

The verse does not say the two named trees were the only trees in the Garden.

## 7. The sequence: formation, planting, placement, provision

Genesis 2:7–9 records distinct actions:

1. God formed man from the dust of the ground.
2. God breathed into the man's nostrils the breath of life.
3. Man became a living soul.
4. God planted a Garden eastward in Eden.
5. God put the man He had formed there.
6. God caused trees to grow out of the ground and named two significant trees.

**SUPPORTED OBSERVATION:** God's actions precede and establish the man's received place and provision in the narrative. The man is not the originating cause of his formation, the Garden, or the trees.

**IMPORTANT LIMIT:** The text's order does not by itself settle every chronological detail. The exact elapsed time and full relation among each action remain unspecified.

Genesis 2:15–17 must be read next to establish the man's assigned work, the command, permission, and prohibition. Purpose, provision, permission, and prohibition are related but distinct; they must not be collapsed into one undifferentiated concept.
