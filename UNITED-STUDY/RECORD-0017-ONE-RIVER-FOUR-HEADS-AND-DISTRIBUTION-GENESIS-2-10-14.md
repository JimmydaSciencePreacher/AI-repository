## 3. Genesis 2:10 — One river, watering, and division

The verse presents a grammatical and narrative sequence:

1. “A river” — singular.
2. It “went out of Eden”.
3. Its stated function is “to water the garden”.
4. “From thence” it “was parted”.
5. It “became into four heads”.

The Hebrew wording includes:

| KJV | Hebrew | Basic sense |
|---|---|---|
| River | נָהָר (*nāhār*) | River or stream |
| Went out | יֹצֵא (*yōṣēʾ*) | Going out / proceeding |
| Of Eden | מֵעֵדֶן (*mēʿēḏen*) | From Eden |
| To water | לְהַשְׁקוֹת (*lehašqôt*) | To water / give drink |
| Garden | הַגָּן (*haggan*) | The garden |
| From thence | מִשָּׁם (*miššām*) | From there |
| Was parted | יִפָּרֵד (*yippārēḏ*) | Is parted / divides |
| Four heads | אַרְבָּעָה רָאשִׁים (*ʾarbāʿâ rāʾšîm*) | Four heads |

### The singular river

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The account begins with one river, not four rivers entering Eden.

The narrative then describes that river being parted into four heads. We must preserve the stated sequence rather than reverse it.

**NOT ADMITTED:** “Four independent rivers originally entered Eden.” That is not what this verse says.

### The source and watering function

The river is said to go out of Eden and to water the Garden. Genesis 2:8 had placed the Garden eastward in Eden; Genesis 2:10 distinguishes the named setting Eden from the Garden that receives the water.

**TEXT STATES:** Eden is the river's stated point of departure in the narrative, and the Garden is the stated object of its watering function.

**LIMIT:** The verse does not say that the river waters the whole earth, all of Eden, or every region named in verses 11–14. It does not specify channels, irrigation works, flow volume, gradient, or the exact way water reaches the Garden.

### “From thence”

The Hebrew *miššām* means “from there”. The sentence places it after the river's movement out of Eden and its watering of the Garden.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The division follows the river's described movement and watering function.

**UNRESOLVED:** The exact spatial point referred to by “from thence”. The verse does not give a measured location for the division or enough detail to draw a precise map.

### “Was parted” and “four heads”

The verb *yippārēḏ* comes from פָּרַד (*pāraḏ*), to divide, separate, or part. “Four heads” translates *ʾarbāʿâ rāʾšîm*.

The text identifies the number four and calls the resulting features “heads”. It does not provide a modern hydrological classification that conclusively determines whether these should be called tributaries, distributaries, branches, source streams, or another arrangement.

**DIRECT FINDING:** The passage describes one river, followed by a division into four heads.

**NOT ADMITTED:** Assigning a modern river-network topology to the four heads without resolving the textual and geographical questions.

## 4. Genesis 2:11–12 — Pison and Havilah

The first head is named פִּישׁוֹן (*Pîšôn*), Pison.

It is described as the river that “compasseth the whole land of Havilah”. The verb is related to סָבַב (*sāḇaḇ*), with a range including going around, surrounding, or encompassing.

The text then associates Havilah with:
- gold;
- gold described as “good”;
- bdellium;
- the onyx stone.

### What is established

**TEXT STATES:**
- Pison is the first named head.
- It is described in relation to the whole land of Havilah.
- Gold is present there and described as good.
- Bdellium and the onyx stone are named.

### What is not established

The text supplies no coordinates, borders, measured river course, geological deposit description, assay, or extraction method.

- “Compasseth” does not by itself prove a perfectly closed loop.
- “Good” does not provide a modern purity percentage or carat grade.
- The exact ancient identity of *bĕḏōlaḥ* (bdellium) is uncertain.
- The exact mineralogical identification of *šōham* (KJV: onyx) should not be assumed from the modern English name alone.
- The land of Havilah is not precisely located by these verses.

Numbers 11:7 also uses *bĕḏōlaḥ* in describing the appearance of manna. That comparison is relevant to biblical usage but does not, by itself, settle the material's modern identity.

**UNRESOLVED:** Pison's modern identity, Havilah's precise location and extent, the exact meaning of “compasseth” in geographical terms, and the material identities of bdellium and onyx.

## 5. Genesis 2:13 — Gihon and Cush

The second river is גִּיחוֹן (*Gîḥôn*), Gihon.

The KJV calls the associated land “Ethiopia”. The Hebrew name is כּוּשׁ (*Kûš*), conventionally transliterated Cush.

The same “compasseth” wording used for Pison is used for Gihon. This establishes a textual parallel: both rivers are described in relation to a whole land using the same expression.

**TEXT STATES:** Gihon is the second river and is described as encompassing the whole land of Cush (KJV: Ethiopia).

**LIMITS:**
- The KJV's historical rendering “Ethiopia” must not automatically be equated with the modern nation-state's present borders.
- The verse gives no coordinates or defined borders for Cush.
- The verse does not identify Gihon with the Nile.
- The verse does not identify it with the Gihon spring associated with Jerusalem merely because the name is shared.
- The repeated verb does not prove that Gihon and Pison have identical courses or geographical configurations.

Other biblical references to Cush and to a Gihon water source must be examined in their own contexts. A shared name alone is insufficient to establish geographical identity.

**UNRESOLVED:** Gihon's modern identity, the precise geographical scope of Cush in this description, and the exact route implied by “compasseth”.

## 6. Genesis 2:14 — Hiddekel, Assyria, and Euphrates

The third river is חִדֶּקֶל (*Ḥiddeqel*), Hiddekel.

It is described as going “toward the east of Assyria”. The directional expression supplies a relationship to Assyria but not a surveyed course, coordinates, or complete route.

Hiddekel is conventionally identified with the Tigris in biblical and historical geography. Daniel 10:4 also uses the name Hiddekel. This conventional identification is relevant context, but it does not reconstruct the full four-river system or prove the location of Eden.

The fourth river is named פְּרָת (*Pĕrāṯ*), Euphrates. It is familiar as an ancient river name, but Genesis 2:14 supplies no further course description for it in this sentence.

**TEXT STATES:**
- Hiddekel is third.
- Hiddekel goes toward the east of Assyria.
- Euphrates is fourth.

**CONTEXTUAL IDENTIFICATION:** Hiddekel is conventionally identified with the Tigris; Euphrates is conventionally identified with the Euphrates river.

**NOT ESTABLISHED:**
- That the modern courses of the Tigris and Euphrates are unchanged from the setting described.
- That the present rivers alone establish Eden's location.
- That the verse supplies the exact source, length, direction, or route of Euphrates.
- That the four named heads can be placed on a single modern map without additional evidence.

## 7. Preserve the differences in the descriptions

The four descriptions are not uniform:

| Head | Textual description | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| Pison | Encompasses Havilah; gold, bdellium, onyx named | River identity, Havilah's extent, material identities |
| Gihon | Encompasses Cush (KJV: Ethiopia) | River identity, historical geography, precise route |
| Hiddekel | Goes toward the east of Assyria | Exact course and spatial reference |
| Euphrates | Named as fourth | Its specific role and course in this four-head reconstruction |

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** Scripture does not provide the same type or amount of geographical description for every head.

We must not manufacture uniformity where the passage itself gives different descriptions.
