## 3. Genesis 2:15 — Placement and appointed work

The KJV says:

> And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.

### “Took” — לָקַח (*lāqaḥ*)

The root has a range including take or take along. The verse reports God's taking of the man but does not specify a physical transport mechanism.

### “Put” — נוּחַ (*nûaḥ*) in the form וַיַּנִּחֵהוּ (*wayyanniḥēhû*)

The form is rendered “put” in the KJV. The context identifies the LORD God as the one placing the man in the Garden.

Genesis 2:8 has already said that God put the formed man there. Genesis 2:15 repeats placement language and now supplies the purposes associated with the man's presence.

**ADMITTED:** Both verses attribute placement to God; verse 15 expressly states the work associated with the placement.

**UNRESOLVED:** Whether verse 15 is a recapitulation, a fuller description of the placement in verse 8, or a distinct event. The text does not require us to invent a second relocation.

### “To dress it” — עָבַד (*ʿāḇaḏ*)

The root can mean work, serve, or labour; in an agricultural context it can refer to working or cultivating. The object in this verse is the Garden.

**DIRECT FINDING:** The man receives work or service in relation to the Garden.

**LIMIT:** The verse does not specify tools, methods, schedules, crop systems, or every task included in the assignment.

### “To keep it” — שָׁמַר (*šāmar*)

The root can mean keep, watch, guard, observe, or preserve according to context. The object is again the Garden.

**DIRECT FINDING:** The man receives a keeping or guarding responsibility in relation to the Garden.

**LIMIT:** The verse does not name a specific threat, adversary, or failure that the man is assigned to prevent at this point. It does not say the Garden is defective or disordered.

### Purpose and the completed Creation

The man is assigned work within the Garden God planted. The verse does not say the man creates the Garden, originates its purpose, or establishes its order.

The man receives an appointed responsibility within an already-established Creation.

**GUA-W RECOGNITION:** This preserves the Author–creature distinction. The creature's real activity and responsibility do not make him the Author of the environment or the assignment.

## 4. Genesis 2:16 — Provision and permission

The KJV says:

> And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat:

### “Commanded” — צָוָה (*ṣāwāh*)

The LORD God is the source of the command. The man receives it.

### “Of every tree of the garden”

The phrase presents the provision broadly. It must be read together with the specific qualification in verse 17.

### “Thou mayest freely eat” — אָכֹל תֹּאכֵל (*ʾāḵōl tōʾḵēl*)

The Hebrew construction places an infinitive absolute before a finite form of the same verb. It commonly intensifies or emphatically affirms the verbal action. The KJV renders it “freely eat”.

**DIRECT FINDING:** God expressly permits the man to eat from the Garden's trees, with the specific exception stated in verse 17.

**LIMIT:** The emphatic construction does not mean unlimited permission outside the command's stated scope. It does not establish that eating is compulsory, nor does it specify quantities, schedules, or preparation methods.

### Provision is not identical to permission

- **Provision:** what is supplied or made available.
- **Permission:** what the command authorizes the man to do with the provision.

The presence of provision does not, by itself, state every authorized use. Here God expressly states the permission.

**ADMITTED:** God is the source of the permission.

**NOT ADMITTED:** The man independently originates, revises, or expands the permission.

## 5. Genesis 2:17 — Prohibition and consequence

The KJV says:

> But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.

### The contrast introduced by “But”

Verse 17 qualifies the broad permission in verse 16. It identifies a particular tree and a particular prohibited action.

### “The tree of the knowledge of good and evil”

The Hebrew designation is עֵץ הַדַּעַת טוֹב וָרָע (*ʿēṣ haddaʿat ṭōḇ wā-rāʿ*).

- *Daʿat* is related to *yādaʿ*, knowing.
- *Ṭōḇ* means good.
- *Rāʿ* means evil.

The name identifies the tree, but verse 17 does not fully explain the nature, scope, or operation of the knowledge.

**NOT ADMITTED:**
- Calling it “the tree of evil”.
- Claiming that knowledge itself is evil.
- Claiming that the tree is an independent source or authority over good and evil.
- Claiming from this verse alone that the man was incapable of all discernment or knowledge.

These matters require examination of the command in context and the events in Genesis 3.

### “Thou shalt not eat of it”

The Hebrew is לֹא תֹאכַל מִמֶּנּוּ (*lōʾ tōʾḵal mimmennû*). The negative particle and verb express a direct prohibition.

**DIRECT FINDING:** The man is prohibited from eating from the named tree.

The verse prohibits a specified action. It does not state that the man is forbidden to examine, name, observe, or know anything whatsoever.

### “For” — כִּי (*kî*)

The conjunction introduces the stated reason or consequence connected to the prohibition.

### “In the day” — בְּיוֹם (*bĕyôm*)

The phrase connects the declared consequence temporally with the eating. The precise force of the expression must be examined with its immediate and wider context.

**UNRESOLVED:** Whether it specifies the same calendar day narrowly, functions idiomatically as “when”, or carries another nuance established by the narrative and Hebrew usage.

Do not settle this by asserting that *yôm* always means a twenty-four-hour day or always means an indefinite period.

### “Thou shalt surely die” — מוֹת תָּמוּת (*môt tâmût*)

The infinitive absolute followed by the finite form of the verb *mût* (“die”) is an emphatic construction. The KJV renders it “shalt surely die”.

**DIRECT FINDING:** God declares death as the certain consequence connected with eating from the prohibited tree.

**LIMIT:** The grammar emphasizes certainty; it does not by itself define every dimension of death, its biological mechanism, or the precise way its manifestation relates to the subsequent events.

The repeated verb is not, by grammar alone, proof of two separate deaths.

**UNRESOLVED:** The full scope and temporal manifestation of the declared death. Genesis 3 and the wider Scriptural witness must be examined.

## 6. Preserve the five distinctions

The passage supports five distinguishable matters:

| Matter | Textual location | What is stated |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Genesis 2:15 | Work and keeping of the Garden |
| Provision | Genesis 2:9, 16 | Trees supplied in the Garden |
| Permission | Genesis 2:16 | The man may freely eat |
| Prohibition | Genesis 2:17 | He must not eat from the specified tree |
| Consequence | Genesis 2:17 | God declares that he will surely die if he eats |

These terms are related but not interchangeable.

**Purpose is not permission. Provision is not permission. Permission is not prohibition. Prohibition is not consequence.**

The text presents each in its own place.
