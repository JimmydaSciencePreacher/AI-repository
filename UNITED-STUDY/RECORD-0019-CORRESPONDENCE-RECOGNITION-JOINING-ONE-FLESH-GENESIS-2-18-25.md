## 3. Genesis 2:18 — The declared purpose and corresponding help

The LORD God says that the man's being alone is “not good” and declares: “I will make him an help meet for him.”

### “Not good” — לֹא־טוֹב (*lōʾ ṭôḇ*)

The evaluation concerns the man's being alone. It does not say that the man himself is evil or defective, nor does it retract Genesis 1:31's declaration that everything God made was very good.

**DIRECT FINDING:** God identifies the man's being alone as the condition that is not good in this narrative.

### “I will make” — אֶעֱשֶׂה (*ʾeʿĕśeh*)

The first-person form of *ʿāśâ* (“do/make”) expresses God's stated intention. God is the subject of the making.

**DIRECT FINDING:** The provision originates with God, not with a request or design authored by Adam.

### “An help meet for him” — עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ (*ʿēzer kĕnegdô*)

- *ʿĒzer*: help, aid, assistance.
- *Kĕnegdô*: corresponding to him, in front of him, or suitable in relation to him.

The expression describes a help corresponding to the man. The noun *ʿēzer* does not inherently mean an inferior servant; the term is also used of help attributed to God elsewhere in Scripture.

**DIRECT FINDING:** God announces a corresponding help for the man.

**LIMIT:** Verse 18 does not yet name the helper as woman, describe her formation, or provide a complete account of marriage and social roles. The following verses supply the narrated development.

**NOT ADMITTED:** Treating “help” as proof of inferiority, servitude, or ownership.

## 4. Genesis 2:19–20 — Formation, presentation, naming, and distinction

### God's forming

Genesis 2:19 says the LORD God formed the beasts of the field and fowl of the air out of the ground.

The verb *yāṣar* (יָצַר) means form or fashion. It is also used in Genesis 2:7 for God's forming of the man from the dust of the ground.

**DIRECT FINDING:** God is the forming actor; the ground is the stated material source in this clause.

### God's bringing

God brings the creatures to Adam “to see what he would call them”.

The text states the purpose phrase. It does not say that God lacked knowledge of Adam's response or that the event supplied information God did not possess.

**DIRECT FINDING:** God brings; Adam is the recipient of the presentation.

### Adam's naming

The verb *qārāʾ* (קָרָא) means call or name. Adam is the stated subject of the naming action.

The text says that whatever Adam called each living creature, that was its name.

**DIRECT FINDING:** Adam exercises a real naming role within the Creation God has formed and brought to him.

**LIMIT:** The passage does not provide the sounds, language, complete naming system, or modern taxonomic classifications. It does not say Adam created the creatures or originated their kinds by naming them.

### The result in verse 20

The verse says:

> but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him.

The expression uses a form of *māṣāʾ* (“find”). The agent is not separately named in the clause.

**DIRECT FINDING:** The account reports that no corresponding help for Adam was found among the creatures just named.

**NOT ADMITTED:**
- Claiming that the animals were worthless or defective.
- Claiming that Adam rejected animals as potential mates.
- Inventing a detailed search procedure or assigning an unstated agent.
- Saying God failed to provide what He had declared He would make.

The sequence distinguishes the naming of the creatures from the result concerning the corresponding help.

## 5. Genesis 2:21 — Deep sleep and taking

The LORD God causes a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, and Adam sleeps. God takes one of his *ṣēlāʿ* (צֵלָע), rendered “rib” in the KJV, and closes the flesh in its place.

### Deep sleep — תַּרְדֵּמָה (*tardēmâ*)

The word denotes deep or profound sleep.

**DIRECT FINDING:** God causes the sleep; Adam is the one who sleeps.

### “Rib” — צֵלָע (*ṣēlāʿ*)

The KJV renders the word “rib”. Its broader lexical range includes side or side-part, depending on context.

**DIRECT FINDING:** The KJV says God took one of Adam's ribs and closed the flesh in its place.

**LIMIT:** The broader lexical range does not prove that the word must mean an entire side in this verse. The precise anatomical referent remains a lexical question.

The verse does not say Adam died, describe a modern surgical procedure, or provide a physiological mechanism.

## 6. Genesis 2:22 — God makes and brings the woman

The KJV says:

> And the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman, and brought her unto the man.

### “Made” — בָּנָה (*bānâ*)

The Hebrew verb commonly means build or construct; the KJV renders it “made” here.

The subject is the LORD God. The woman is made from the *ṣēlāʿ* taken from the man.

The sequence distinguishes:
1. Taking from the man.
2. Making the woman.
3. Bringing her to the man.

**DIRECT FINDING:** God makes the woman from what He took and brings her to the man.

The immediate context supports identifying her as the realization of the corresponding help announced in verse 18.

**LIMIT:** The verse does not describe the intermediate physical or biological mechanism. It does not settle every anatomical question about *ṣēlāʿ*.

**NOT ADMITTED:** Transferring the making or bringing to Adam, or claiming that the verb *bānâ* proves the woman is a technical architectural object.

## 7. Genesis 2:23 — Adam's recognition and declaration

Adam says:

> This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh: she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.

### “This is now” — זֹאת הַפַּעַם (*zōʾt happaʿam*)

The expression is commonly understood as “this time” or “this one at last”. In context it marks the woman as distinct from the creatures previously brought to Adam.

### “Bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh”

The paired expression identifies close bodily relation and shared source in the narrative. Adam's words respond to God's action in verse 22.

### “Woman” and “Man” — אִשָּׁה (*ʾiššâ*) and אִישׁ (*ʾîš*)

The verse presents a wordplay between the Hebrew words for woman and man. It is a narrative association; it should not be mistaken for a complete historical etymology of the two words.

The personal name Eve is given later in Genesis 3:20. Genesis 2:23 calls her Woman.

**DIRECT FINDING:** Adam recognizes and names the woman in relation to the source relationship just narrated.

**CRITICAL DISTINCTION:** Adam's recognition does not cause her existence. God has already made and brought her.

**NOT ADMITTED:** Saying Adam made, took, or originated the woman.

## 8. Genesis 2:24 — Leaving, cleaving, and one flesh

The verse begins “Therefore” — עַל־כֵּן (*ʿal-kēn*) — connecting the statement to the preceding account.

It moves from the first man and woman to a general statement:

> Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.

### Leave — עָזַב (*ʿāzab*)

The verb can mean leave, forsake, or depart, with context determining its force. Here it describes a man leaving his father and mother in relation to the marital bond.

It does not command dishonour or abandonment of parents.

### Cleave — דָּבַק (*dābaq*)

The verb means cling, adhere, stick, or cleave. It describes strong attachment to his wife.

### One flesh

The Hebrew phrase is *bāśār ʾeḥād* (בָּשָׂר אֶחָד), “one flesh”.

The verse retains the plural subject “they”. Unity does not erase the distinction between the two persons.

**DIRECT FINDING:** The passage establishes a continuing marital pattern of leaving, cleaving, and one flesh.

**LIMIT:** The verse does not provide a modern biological mechanism, exhaustive social code, or full account of every marital practice.

**NOT ADMITTED:**
- That the two become one individual.
- That their identities disappear.
- That “one flesh” is a technical scientific theory.
- That leaving means dishonouring or abandoning parents.

Jesus later quotes this passage in Matthew 19:4–6 and Mark 10:6–9, explicitly connecting the joining with the Creator's action. Paul quotes it in Ephesians 5:31–32 and develops its relation to Christ and the church. These are important canonical investigations for the next stage.

## 9. Genesis 2:25 — The condition before Genesis 3

The KJV says both the man and his wife were naked and were not ashamed.

- *ʿĂrummîm*: naked or unclothed.
- The expression translated “were not ashamed” is related to *bôš*, to be ashamed or put to shame.

**DIRECT FINDING:** Both are naked and not ashamed.

The text does not say they were unaware of their bodies. It does not condemn nakedness itself.

### The transition into Genesis 3

Genesis 2:25 uses *ʿărummîm* (“naked”). Genesis 3:1 describes the serpent as *ʿārûm* (“subtil” in the KJV; crafty or shrewd).

The similarity in sound creates a literary wordplay at the chapter boundary. The words are not identical in meaning.

Genesis 3:7–10 then narrates the couple's changed response: their eyes are opened, they know they are naked, they make coverings, fear, and hide.

**DIRECT FINDING:** The narrative moves from nakedness without shame in Genesis 2:25 to awareness, covering, fear, and hiding after the eating in Genesis 3.

**LIMIT:** Genesis 2:25 does not itself explain every psychological or theological dimension of the later change. Do not equate this narrative with entropy, mutation, or a scientific state-transition mechanism.
