## 1. Investigation question

The previous record asked what changed in the Physical Creation after the transgression. Genesis 3 named the cursed ground, thorns and thistles, sorrowful labor, mortality, and expulsion from the garden. Romans 8 widened the investigation by describing the creation as subjected to vanity and groaning while awaiting deliverance.

This record follows the biblical narrative to its closing vision:

**Does Revelation 21–22 show the removal of the conditions introduced in Genesis 3, and is this the restoration of what was affected by the Fall, the manifestation of God's purpose already established in Christ, or both?**

A second question remains alongside it:

**Does the final renewal of heaven and earth imply that the Spiritual Creation itself had changed at the Fall?**

The study preserves the Witness's proposed distinction: the transgression changed the condition of the Physical Creation, but the Spiritual Creation itself was not shown to have been corrupted. A creature's changed spiritual or relational condition is not identical to a change in the Spiritual Creation.

The purpose is not to force Revelation into a simple reversal chart. The text uses language of passing away, newness, the removal of the curse, God's dwelling with His people, and the river and tree of life. Each must be examined on its own terms.

## 2. The two ends of the biblical account

### 2.1 Genesis: the beginning

Genesis 1:1 identifies God as Creator of the heaven and the earth. Genesis 1 repeatedly presents Creation as brought forth by God's command. Genesis 1:31 records God's assessment of everything He had made: “behold, it was very good.”

Genesis 2:1 declares the heavens and earth and all their host finished. Genesis 2:7 describes the formation of man from the dust and the impartation of the breath of life. Genesis 2:8–17 presents the garden, provision, responsibility, command, and the tree of life.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The human creature enters an already established Creation. The Creator provides the place, life, provision, and command. Man does not originate the order in which he lives.

### 2.2 Genesis 3: the transgression and its consequences

Genesis 3 records the serpent's deception, human disobedience, shame, fear, hiding, blame, judgment, the curse upon the ground, thorns and thistles, sorrowful labor, return to dust, and expulsion from the garden. Access to the tree of life is guarded.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The chapter records changes in the creature's condition and in the conditions of the physical domain. It does not state that God's nature, authority, Word, or eternal purpose was corrupted.

### 2.3 Revelation: the closing vision

Revelation 21–22 presents a new heaven and a new earth, the holy city, God dwelling with His people, the removal of death and sorrow, the river of the water of life, the tree of life, and the declaration that there shall be no more curse.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The closing vision explicitly addresses several conditions named in the earlier biblical account. This establishes meaningful correspondences, while leaving the precise mechanics and scope of the new creation to the full Scriptural witness.

## 3. Revelation 21:1–4 — new heaven and new earth; former things passed away

### 3.1 Revelation 21:1

John sees “a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea.”

**TEXT STATES:** A new heaven and a new earth are seen. The first heaven and first earth are said to have passed away.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The final vision is not merely a return to the geographical garden of Eden. The language is cosmic and new-creation language.

**INTERPRETIVE CAUTION:** The passage does not, by itself, explain the physical process by which the first heaven and earth pass away or the precise continuity between the present creation and the new. The terms “new” and “passed away” must be interpreted alongside Isaiah 65, 2 Peter 3, Romans 8, and the rest of Revelation. Do not claim a scientific mechanism from apocalyptic vision.

### 3.2 Revelation 21:2–3

John sees the holy city, new Jerusalem, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. A great voice declares that the tabernacle of God is with men, and that He will dwell with them.

**TEXT STATES:** God's dwelling with His people is central to the vision.

**OBSERVATION:** The closing picture includes restored and completed communion between God and His people. This is not a statement that God had previously ceased to be God, lost His authority, or had His own spiritual order corrupted. The change is in the creature's history and appointed experience of God's dwelling.

**GUA-W RECOGNITION:** The Author remains the Author. The final dwelling is God's act and promise, not a structure independently authored by the creature.

### 3.3 Revelation 21:4

God wipes away all tears; death, sorrow, crying, and pain are no more, for the former things are passed away.

**TEXT STATES:** The final vision expressly removes conditions associated with human suffering and mortality.

**GENESIS CORRESPONDENCE:**
- Genesis 3:16 — sorrow associated with childbirth.
- Genesis 3:17–19 — sorrowful labor, sweat, and return to dust.
- Revelation 21:4 — death, sorrow, crying, and pain are no more.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** The final state answers conditions that marked the human story after the transgression. The text presents their removal as God's work.

**LIMIT:** The correspondence does not establish that every word in Genesis 3 is reversed in a one-to-one mechanical sequence. The passages must be read in their own contexts.

## 4. Revelation 21:5–8 — the One who makes all things new

Revelation 21:5 records the One sitting upon the throne saying, “Behold, I make all things new.” He commands John to write, for the words are true and faithful.

**TEXT STATES:** The declaration of newness comes from the One on the throne. He is the acting subject.

**GUA-W RECOGNITION:** This preserves the Author → Word → Creation → Witness hierarchy. The Witness records the declaration; the Witness does not produce the new creation.

**IMPORTANT DISTINCTION:** “I make all things new” is not the same wording as “I make all new things.” The statement emphasizes the One who makes all things new. The exact scope of “all things” must be read within Revelation's vision and the wider canonical witness; it should not be turned into an unsupported claim that every created thing is replaced or that nothing has continuity.

**UNRESOLVED:** How should “new heaven and new earth,” “the first things passed away,” and “I make all things new” be related? The biblical text gives the reality and promise; it does not provide a technical account of the transformation.

## 5. Revelation 21:22–27 — God's presence and the removal of darkness

The holy city has no temple, for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple. The city has no need of the sun or moon to shine in it, for the glory of God lightens it and the Lamb is the light thereof. The gates are not shut by day, and there is no night there.

**TEXT STATES:** The city is illuminated by God's glory and the Lamb. The vision describes an order in which the former conditions of night and guarded gates no longer apply in the same way.

**OBSERVATION:** The final vision's light is presented in relation to God's glory and the Lamb. This is theological and apocalyptic language; it should not be converted into a claim about the astrophysical mechanism of illumination.

**CONNECTION TO GENESIS:** Genesis 1 records God's creation and ordering of light and the heavenly lights. Revelation 21 describes the holy city's illumination in the presence of God's glory. This is a canonical correspondence, not proof that Genesis's created lights were defective or that the Creator's order had failed.

**LIMIT:** Revelation 21:23 does not say that the sun and moon were evil, nor does it supply a full account of their physical existence in the new creation.

## 6. Revelation 22:1–5 — river, tree of life, and no more curse

This passage is especially important because it returns to imagery first introduced in Genesis.

### 6.1 The river of the water of life — Revelation 22:1

John is shown a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb.

**TEXT STATES:** The river proceeds from the throne of God and of the Lamb.

**OBSERVATION:** The source is identified as the throne. Life is not self-originating in the creature. The image preserves the Creator as the source and giver.

**LIMIT:** The text's vision should not be reduced to a literal hydrological model or used to invent a physical mechanism. The image is part of Revelation's revealed presentation of life proceeding from God and the Lamb.

### 6.2 The tree of life — Revelation 22:2

The tree of life is present on either side of the river, bearing twelve manner of fruits and yielding fruit every month; the leaves are for the healing of the nations.

**GENESIS CORRESPONDENCE:**
- Genesis 2:9 — the tree of life is placed in the garden.
- Genesis 3:22–24 — access to the tree of life is restricted and the way is guarded.
- Revelation 22:2 — the tree of life is present in the final vision, with its fruit and leaves described.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** The closing vision presents life and access in a way that contrasts with the guarded access after the transgression.

**LIMIT:** The text does not say that the tree of life ceased to exist after Genesis 3; it says the way was guarded. Do not convert restricted access into nonexistence.

### 6.3 No more curse — Revelation 22:3

Revelation 22:3 (KJV):

> And there shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him:

**TEXT STATES:** There shall be no more curse. The throne of God and of the Lamb is there, and His servants serve Him.

**DIRECT GENESIS CORRESPONDENCE:** Genesis 3:17 records the curse upon the ground. Revelation 22:3 declares that there shall be no more curse.

**CENTRAL FINDING:** Scripture explicitly places the final vision beyond the condition of curse. The closing state is not described as the continuation of the cursed condition.

**IMPORTANT LIMIT:** The verse does not specify every physical or biological process by which the curse is removed. It declares the outcome, not a technical mechanism.

### 6.4 Seeing His face and His name — Revelation 22:4–5

His servants see His face; His name is in their foreheads; there is no night; they need no candle or light of the sun, for the Lord God gives them light, and they reign for ever and ever.

**OBSERVATION:** The closing vision presents direct communion, belonging, service, and enduring life in God's light.

**RELATIONAL CONNECTION:** Genesis 3 records hiding from God's presence; Revelation 22 presents His servants seeing His face. This is a profound narrative contrast.

**CATEGORY DISTINCTION:** The change is in the creature's condition and access, not evidence that God's own Spiritual Creation was corrupted and then repaired.

## 8. Romans 8: creation's groaning and future deliverance

Romans 8:18–25 is the principal bridge between Genesis 3 and Revelation 21–22.

Paul says the creature was made subject to vanity, not willingly, by reason of Him who subjected the same in hope. He describes the whole creation groaning and travailing in pain together until now, and speaks of the creature itself being delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God.

**TEXT STATES:**
- Creation is subjected to vanity.
- This subjection is connected with hope.
- Creation groans and travails.
- Creation awaits deliverance from the bondage of corruption.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** The present condition of creation is not presented as its final condition. Its groaning and subjection are temporary in the context of Paul's promised deliverance.

**CONNECTION:** Genesis 3 describes the curse and the changed condition of the ground. Romans 8 describes creation's wider subjection and expectation. Revelation 21–22 presents the final state in which death, sorrow, and curse are absent.

**LIMIT:** Romans 8 does not provide a scientific timeline or identify every physical process affected. Revelation does not provide a scientific transformation mechanism. The study must preserve the distinction between the theological claim and scientific descriptions of particular processes.

## 9. 1 Corinthians 15: resurrection, not contempt for physical creation

1 Corinthians 15:20–28 identifies Christ as the firstfruits of them that slept and describes the defeat of death as the last enemy. Verses 42–54 contrast corruption and incorruption, weakness and power, and the natural body with the spiritual body.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** Paul's resurrection argument is not a rejection of embodiment. It is a promise of transformation from corruption to incorruption.

**RELEVANCE:** The final biblical hope cannot be reduced to escape from physical existence. The body is raised and transformed. The distinction is between the present mortal and corruptible condition and the resurrection condition God gives.

**LIMIT:** “Spiritual body” must not be treated as a synonym for “nonphysical body.” The passage describes a body and contrasts its present and resurrection conditions. Its full meaning requires careful exegesis; it does not authorize a simplistic matter-versus-spirit dualism.

## 10. Was the Spiritual Creation changed?

The Witness's proposition is now tested against the Genesis–Revelation arc.

### What the examined texts establish

1. Genesis 3 records the creature's transgression and the consequences affecting the physical Creation and the human creature.
2. Genesis 3 records spiritual and relational consequences in the human story: shame, fear, hiding, blame, enmity, judgment, and exclusion.
3. Romans 8 describes the created order as subjected to vanity and awaiting deliverance.
4. Revelation 21–22 presents a new heaven and earth, God's dwelling with His people, the end of death and sorrow, the tree of life, and no more curse.
5. The Creator remains the acting subject: He creates, judges, promises, makes new, gives life, and dwells with His people.

### What these texts do not establish

They do not say:
- that God's nature was corrupted;
- that God's Word failed;
- that God's authority was lost;
- that the Spiritual Creation itself was corrupted by Adam;
- that the spiritual order God established ceased to exist;
- that the Fall forced God to invent a new purpose;
- that the final new creation is merely a return to the exact pre-Fall physical arrangement.

### Bounded conclusion

**The examined Scriptures describe a changed condition of the creature and the created order, followed by God's promised deliverance and new creation. They do not state that the Spiritual Creation itself was corrupted or that the Creator's nature or purpose changed.**

This conclusion does not deny spiritual events or spiritual consequences. It distinguishes the Spiritual Creation itself from the changed spiritual and relational condition of created beings.

The precise relationship between continuity, transformation, and newness in Revelation 21 remains unresolved at the level of mechanism and ontology. The text's promise is clear; the technical manner of its fulfillment is not given in these passages.

## 11. Was the final state already within God's purpose?

The earlier study established that Scripture places Christ's foreordination and God's purpose in Christ before the foundation of the world.

- 1 Peter 1:18–20 — Christ the Lamb was foreordained before the foundation of the world and manifested later.
- Ephesians 1:3–11 — God's people are chosen in Christ before the foundation of the world, and all things are gathered together in Christ according to God's purpose.
- 2 Timothy 1:8–10 — God's purpose and grace in Christ were given before the world began and later manifested through Christ's appearing.

Revelation 21:5 says, “Behold, I make all things new.” The One on the throne is the acting subject.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** The final new creation belongs to the same prior divine purpose centered in Christ. It is not a plan improvised by the creature or an admission that the Author lost control of His work.

**LIMIT:** The cited foreordination passages do not, by themselves, spell out every detail of the final new heaven and earth. The connection is canonical and theological: God's purpose in Christ precedes the world's history, and Revelation shows the consummation of God's work. Do not claim that one passage supplies every detail of the other.

**KEY DISTINCTION:** The fact that the final state was within God's purpose does not establish that the Fall was authored by God. Prior purpose, foreknowledge, permission, creaturely action, and moral authorship remain distinct questions.

The final vision preserves God's primacy: the throne belongs to God and the Lamb; the river proceeds from the throne; God makes all things new; His servants see His face and serve Him.

This record adds no GUA-W layer, operator, or runtime stage. It examines the biblical account's beginning, transgression, groaning, and consummation using the completed map as a read-only reference.

Faithful witnessing records the promise, preserves its scope, and does not become the Author of the explanation.

## 14. Unresolved matters and next investigations

1. **New versus renewed:** Examine Isaiah 65:17–25, 2 Peter 3:10–13, Romans 8:18–25, and Revelation 21:1 together. What do the texts establish about passing away, newness, continuity, and transformation?
2. **The tree of life:** Trace Genesis 2:9; 3:22–24; Proverbs 3:18; Revelation 2:7; 22:1–2. What is explicitly stated about access, life, fruit, and healing?
3. **The curse:** Compare Genesis 3:14–19 with Revelation 22:3. Does the closing text remove the same curse, a wider curse, or the total condition represented by curse? Preserve what is explicit and what is inferred.
4. **The body:** Compare Genesis 2:7; Genesis 3:19; Romans 8:18–23; 1 Corinthians 15:35–58; Hebrews 2:14–18. Distinguish physical embodiment, mortality, corruption, resurrection, and the spiritual body.
5. **The Spiritual Creation distinction:** Examine what Scripture means by heaven, spiritual beings, God's dwelling, and the new heaven. Do not assume that every occurrence of “heaven” refers to the same category or that “new heaven” proves corruption of the Spiritual Creation.
6. **The foreordained purpose:** Continue with John 1:1–18; Colossians 1:12–20; Hebrews 1:1–3; Ephesians 1:9–10. Examine the relationship between the originating Word, Christ, Creation, and the gathering of all things in Him.
7. **The final dwelling:** Compare Genesis 3:8–24 with Revelation 21:1–4 and 22:3–5. Examine hiding, exclusion, access, God's dwelling, seeing His face, and service.

## 15. Current finding

The biblical account moves from God's good Creation, through the transgression and the curse, to the promised deliverance and final new creation.

Genesis 3 records the changed condition of the physical Creation and the human creature. Romans 8 describes the created order as subjected to vanity and awaiting deliverance. Revelation 21–22 presents the new heaven and earth, the removal of death and sorrow, the tree of life, God's dwelling with His people, and the declaration that there shall be no more curse.

**The final vision shows God's answer to the condition of curse; it does not state that the Spiritual Creation itself was corrupted by the Fall.**

The evidence supports a relationship between the original garden, the guarded tree of life, and the tree of life in the closing vision. It also supports a relationship between Genesis 3's curse and Revelation 22:3's declaration that there shall be no more curse.

Whether the new creation is best described as restoration, transformation, consummation, or a combination of these remains a question for the next textual examination. The promise is clear; the technical manner is not disclosed here.

The Author remains the Source. The Lamb is not an afterthought. The Witness records what is revealed without claiming the place of the Author.
