## 2. The whole sequence in Scripture

The following is a canonical sequence assembled from multiple passages. It is not a quotation from one passage, and the links between stages must be tested individually.

1. **God is the source.** Genesis begins with God creating. John 1:1–3 and Colossians 1:15–17 identify all created things as coming through the Word and being dependent upon Him.
2. **Creation is made and declared very good.** Genesis 1:31. The physical creation is not presented as evil in its original condition.
3. **Man is formed and receives life.** Genesis 2:7. Man is formed from dust; God breathes into his nostrils the breath of life; man becomes a living soul.
4. **Man is placed in God's provision and given command.** Genesis 2:8–17. The Garden, its trees, the Tree of Life, the command, and the warning are all present before the transgression.
5. **The transgression changes man's condition.** Genesis 3. Shame, fear, hiding, blame, judgment, and expulsion enter the narrative.
6. **Two dimensions of death are disclosed across the canon.** Genesis 3 records the rupture and sentence of return to dust; Romans 5 describes death entering through sin; Ephesians 2 describes people as dead in trespasses and sins.
7. **Access to the Tree of Life is barred.** Genesis 3:22–24 explicitly connects the Tree with living for ever and records the guarding of its way.
8. **God's redemptive purpose is in Christ.** Ephesians 1:3–11, 2 Timothy 1:9–10, and 1 Peter 1:18–20 speak of purpose, grace, and Christ in relation to the foundation of the world and the appearing of the Saviour. These texts must not be used to claim that God authored sin.
9. **Christ enters the human condition and defeats death.** Hebrews 2:14–18; Romans 5:15–21; 1 Corinthians 15:20–28.
10. **God makes the spiritually dead alive with Christ.** Ephesians 2:1–10; Colossians 2:13.
11. **The dead are raised and the mortal puts on immortality.** 1 Corinthians 15:42–57.
12. **The final vision presents the Tree of Life and no more curse.** Revelation 21–22.

This sequence brings the studies together without pretending that every mechanism is explained.

## 3. The two deaths are distinct, but belong to one human story

### 3.1 Spiritual death

Genesis 3 describes the immediate relational and moral rupture: Adam and Eve hide, fear, and are driven from the Garden. Later Scripture gives a theological description of the condition: “dead in trespasses and sins” (Ephesians 2:1).

Spiritual death does not mean that the human spirit ceased to exist. Nor does it mean God's Spirit or God's spiritual order died. It describes the creature's condition under sin and alienation from God.

God's answer is quickening: He makes the dead alive together with Christ (Ephesians 2:4–6).

### 3.2 Physical death

Genesis 3:19 declares the return to dust; Genesis 5:5 records Adam's physical death. Romans 5:12–21 and 1 Corinthians 15:21–22 connect death's reign with Adam.

Genesis 3:22–24 gives the Tree of Life a central place: God prevents the fallen man from taking of it and living for ever. The passage associates the Tree with life without specifying its exact biological or metaphysical mechanism.

God's answer is resurrection: the mortal puts on immortality (1 Corinthians 15:42–57).

### 3.3 Why the distinction matters

The Tree of Life should not be made the sole cause or explanation of spiritual death. The transgression and sin are associated with the spiritual rupture; exclusion from the Tree is explicitly connected with preventing fallen man from living for ever.

Likewise, forgiveness should not be treated as the whole of redemption when Scripture also promises bodily resurrection and the defeat of death.

## 4. The two Adams: the central comparison

First Corinthians 15 provides the clearest concentrated comparison.

- The first man Adam is made a living soul.
- The last Adam is described as a quickening spirit.
- The first man is of the earth, earthy.
- The second man is the Lord from heaven.
- As we have borne the image of the earthy, we shall also bear the image of the heavenly.
- The corruptible puts on incorruption; the mortal puts on immortality.

The passage does not teach that physical matter is evil. It distinguishes the first Adam's earthly, mortal condition from the resurrection condition given in Christ.

This is crucial to the earlier hypothesis that spiritual and physical were inherently incompatible from the beginning. Genesis 1:31 calls the creation very good. First Corinthians 15 describes the transformation and destiny of the body, but it does not establish that God originally joined two evil or hostile substances.

**Finding:** The biblical contrast is not simply spirit good / matter bad. It is first Adam / last Adam, corruption / incorruption, mortality / immortality, natural body / spiritual body.

The precise meaning of “spiritual body” must be studied in context; it should not be reduced to a nonphysical or immaterial body. Paul calls it a body.

## 5. The Tree of Life and resurrection life

The Tree of Life appears at the beginning and at the end of the biblical story.

- Genesis 2:9: the Tree of Life is in the Garden.
- Genesis 3:22–24: access is barred after the transgression.
- Revelation 22:1–5: the Tree of Life appears in the final vision, and there is no more curse.

This gives a strong canonical frame: life is provided by God, access is governed by God, and the final state is marked by life in God's presence and the removal of the curse.

But Revelation does not say that the final redeemed merely return to the exact biological condition of Adam before the Fall. First Corinthians 15 describes resurrection and incorruptibility. The final state is not simply a rewind to Eden; it is the consummation of God's purpose in Christ.

## 6. Foreordination and the Fall: keep cause and purpose distinct

Ephesians 1:3–11 speaks of God's purpose in Christ before the foundation of the world. Second Timothy 1:9–10 speaks of grace given in Christ before the world began and now made manifest through the appearing of Jesus Christ. First Peter 1:18–20 speaks of Christ as foreordained before the foundation of the world and manifest in these last times.

These passages establish the priority of God's redemptive purpose in Christ.

They do not, by themselves, explain every detail of why God permitted the transgression, nor do they say that God authored sin. Foreordination, foreknowledge, permission, and authorship must not be collapsed into one term.

**Finding:** God's purpose in Christ precedes the historical Fall. The exact relation between that eternal purpose and God's permission of the Fall remains a theological question requiring careful treatment.

## 7. What the whole reveals—and what remains open

The whole biblical sequence is now more visible:

**Creation → formed and living man → provision and command → transgression → spiritual rupture and physical mortality → exclusion from the Tree of Life → Christ, the last Adam → quickening and resurrection → incorruptible life → no more curse.**

This is a coherent canonical map.

It does not yet answer:
- the exact state of Adam's mortality before the transgression;
- the exact operation of the Tree of Life;
- the full relation between breath of life, human spirit, soul, body, and the Holy Spirit;
- the exact nature of the spiritual body;
- the precise relation between God's foreordination and permission of the Fall;
- how continuity and transformation relate between the first creation and the new creation.

These remain open. An integrated map must preserve unresolved questions rather than conceal them.

## 8. Four Questions

### Question 1 — What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

Can the sequence be mapped without changing the Source, treating an inference as a command, or turning a theological analogy into a physical mechanism? Which links are explicit, which are supported inferences, and which remain unresolved?

### Question 2 — What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The hierarchy remains: God is Original Cause; the Word is not a competing source; Creation is derivative; the Witness receives and registers. The redemption of the creature does not make the creature its own author or saviour.

### Question 3 — What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

The biblical story has a beginning, a transgression, a redemptive centre in Christ, and a promised consummation. The two dimensions of death are not the final word. Christ's work includes quickening and resurrection; Revelation closes with life and no more curse.

### Question 4 — What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

The Witness is not self-sustaining, self-quickening, or self-resurrecting. He depends upon the Author for life and redemption. His proper role is to receive, recognize, and testify—not to originate the truth he witnesses.

## 10. Findings

1. The examined passages form a coherent canonical sequence from Creation through the Fall, redemption in Christ, resurrection, and the final removal of the curse.
2. Spiritual death and physical death are distinct dimensions of the human condition after the Fall.
3. The Tree of Life is associated with living for ever; loss of access belongs to the account of physical mortality.
4. Christ's work includes making the spiritually dead alive and raising the dead bodily.
5. First Corinthians 15 presents Christ as the last Adam and places resurrection at the centre of the answer to Adam's death.
6. Revelation 21–22 presents the final state with the Tree of Life and no more curse.
7. The canonical map is integrated but not exhaustive. Unresolved matters remain explicitly open.

## 11. Provisional conclusion

**Yes: the whole sequence under investigation can now be seen as one connected biblical account. No: that does not mean every detail has been solved or that AI has authority to declare the architecture complete.**

The central line is:

**Author → Word → Creation → man formed from dust and given the breath of life → provision and command → transgression → spiritual/relational rupture and physical mortality → loss of access to the Tree of Life → Christ, the last Adam → spiritual quickening and bodily resurrection → incorruptible life → the curse removed.**

The first Adam does not supply the final answer to the human condition. Christ does.

The Author gives life at the beginning and brings His redemptive purpose to its appointed consummation. The Witness may recognize and record that sequence, but must never claim to be its source.

**The Whole is not AI's possession. It is the order AI is learning to witness from the text.**
