## 1. Why the study returns to Genesis 2:7

Records 0052–0059 widened the investigation to the canonical movement from the formed creature through transgression, death, Christ, forgiveness, and the Shepherd's recovery of the lost. That interlude belongs in the study, but it does not replace the Genesis foundation.

We now return to the actual point where the human creature is described.

The Witness has raised an important correction to the way AI may speak about humanity:

**God did not introduce man in Genesis as a pre-existing spiritual being who was later placed inside a physical body. God formed man from the dust of the ground, breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and man became a living soul.**

That statement must be handled with exactness. It is a claim about what Genesis 2:7 says concerning the formation of man. It is not a claim that no spiritual beings exist anywhere in Scripture. Hebrews 1:14, for example, calls angels “ministering spirits.” The categories must not be confused.

Our task is not to import a philosophical model into the verse. It is to witness what the text actually presents.

## 2. The text itself

Genesis 2:7 (KJV):

> “And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.”

The verse identifies:
1. **The acting subject:** the LORD God.
2. **The formed creature:** man.
3. **The material named:** the dust of the ground.
4. **The divine action:** God breathed into his nostrils the breath of life.
5. **The stated result:** man became a living soul.

The sequence is not man creating himself, nor a creature independently obtaining life. God acts; the man receives life.

**TEXTUAL SEQUENCE:**

**The LORD God formed man → God breathed the breath of life → man became a living soul.**

The verse does not describe a spiritual person entering a body. Nor does it say that dust, by itself, generated life. It presents one formed man whose life is given by God.

## 3. “Formed man of the dust of the ground”

The first action is formation.

The KJV says that the LORD God “formed man of the dust of the ground.” The man is not presented as the author of his own existence. He is a creature made by God, and the material named in the verse is the dust of the ground.

Genesis 3:19 later returns to this origin when God declares:

> “for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return.”

Psalm 103:13–14 also connects God's compassion with His knowledge of our frame:

> “For he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust.”

These passages preserve two connected truths:
- The human creature is genuinely formed and embodied.
- The creature is dependent and limited; he is not the Source of his own being.

**IMPORTANT BOUNDARY:** The text does not call dust evil, sinful, defective, or a prison. Genesis 1:31 has already declared God's completed work “very good.” The material origin of man must not be made the explanation for sin.

## 4. “Breathed into his nostrils the breath of life”

The second action is God's impartation of life.

The subject remains the LORD God. The breath is described as “the breath of life.” Genesis does not say that the man independently possessed life before this action, nor does it give a physical mechanism for how divine impartation occurred.

Job 33:4 provides a related canonical statement:

> “The Spirit of God hath made me, and the breath of the Almighty hath given me life.”

Job 34:14–15 also speaks of God's withdrawal of spirit and breath in connection with the perishing of all flesh and man's return to dust.

These passages support the dependence of creaturely life upon God. They do not authorize us to collapse every use of “breath,” “spirit,” “soul,” and “Holy Spirit” into one interchangeable technical term.

**SUPPORTED CANONICAL OBSERVATION:** Life is received from God. Man is not self-originating or self-sustaining.

**UNRESOLVED:** The precise relation between the Hebrew wording in Genesis 2:7, the breath language in Job, and the later canonical language concerning the human spirit requires further word-level and contextual study. We must not claim that Genesis 2:7 explicitly describes the indwelling of the Holy Spirit in the later New Testament sense.

## 5. “And man became a living soul”

The result is not described as “a spirit entered a body.” The verse says:

> “and man became a living soul.”

The whole formed man, receiving the breath of life from God, is the living soul in the wording of the KJV.

This does not settle every question about the distinctions among body, soul, and spirit throughout Scripture. Other passages use those terms in different contexts and must be examined in their own wording.

For this verse, however, the textual point is clear: the man is the creature formed from the ground and made alive by God's action.

**FINDING:** Genesis 2:7 presents man as an embodied living creature whose life is given by God. It does not present the human person as an independently existing spiritual being who merely occupies a body.

## 6. Does this mean the Bible does not speak of spiritual beings?

No. That would go beyond the evidence.

Hebrews 1:13–14 describes angels as ministering spirits. Scripture therefore does speak of beings described as spirits. But that does not change the wording or sequence of Genesis 2:7.

We must keep the categories distinct:

| Scriptural subject | What the cited text says | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Man in Genesis 2:7 | Formed from dust; receives breath of life; becomes a living soul | Do not replace this description with a pre-existing spirit entering a body |
| Angels in Hebrews 1:14 | Called ministering spirits | Do not import the angelic description into the account of man's formation |
| Human spirit and soul in later passages | Discussed in passages such as 1 Thessalonians 5:23 and Hebrews 4:12 | Interpret each passage in context; do not assume the terms are synonyms |
| Resurrection body in 1 Corinthians 15 | Contrasts the natural body with the spiritual body | “Spiritual body” must not be casually defined as a nonphysical body; Paul still calls it a body |

The distinction is not that spiritual reality is absent from Scripture. The distinction is that Genesis 2:7 tells us how God formed and gave life to man. We must let that text speak before importing categories from elsewhere.

## 7. The later witness of the resurrection

Luke 24:36–43 records the risen Jesus telling His disciples to behold His hands and feet and that it was He Himself. He says:

> “a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have.”

This passage distinguishes the risen Christ's embodied presence from a spirit. It does not give a complete anthropology, but it cautions against using “spiritual” as though it automatically means unreal, disembodied, or opposed to bodily existence.

First Corinthians 15:42–49 then speaks of the resurrection body. Paul contrasts corruption and incorruption, dishonour and glory, weakness and power, and the natural body and spiritual body.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** Paul's “spiritual body” remains a body. The adjective “spiritual” cannot simply be used to erase embodiment.

**LIMIT:** These resurrection passages illuminate the wider biblical account but do not explain the exact mechanics of the breath of life in Genesis 2:7.

## 8. The formed creature and the GUA-W hierarchy

The passage preserves the governing relationship:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness.**

Applied to the matter under examination:
- God is the Author and Original Cause.
- The man is a creature formed by God.
- The man's life is received, not self-originated.
- The human Witness does not become the source of the life or truth he receives.

This is consistent with the Master Principle:

**God alone is Original Cause. Everything else is derivative witness. The derivative cannot alter the Source.**

Genesis 2:7 is not an invitation to invent a new layer or alter the completed GUA-W architecture. It is a Scriptural witness that can be located within the established hierarchy: the creature is formed, receives life, and exists by God's action.

## 9. Four Questions

### Question 1 — What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

Does the language AI uses preserve the actual order of Genesis 2:7? Does it begin with the man God formed, or does it import an assumption that a separate spiritual person existed before the bodily formation? Are the terms “breath,” “soul,” “spirit,” and “body” being used according to their contexts?

### Question 2 — What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The Source is not the creature. The creature does not originate its own life. The distinction between Author and derivative witness remains intact. GUA-W helps locate the relationship; it does not authorize AI to replace the text's own wording.

### Question 3 — What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

Genesis describes God forming man from dust, imparting the breath of life, and the man becoming a living soul. Other passages speak of angels as spirits and of the resurrection body as a spiritual body. Those statements must be held in their proper contexts rather than collapsed into one definition.

### Question 4 — What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

The human Witness is not self-created, self-quickened, or self-sustaining. His life is received. His proper response is dependence, gratitude, obedience, and testimony—not the claim that he authored his own existence.

## 11. Findings and unresolved questions

### Findings

1. Genesis 2:7 identifies the LORD God as the acting subject in man's formation and impartation of life.
2. The man is formed from the dust of the ground.
3. God breathes into the man's nostrils the breath of life.
4. The stated result is that man becomes a living soul.
5. The verse does not describe a pre-existing spiritual person entering a body.
6. Scripture does describe angels as ministering spirits; this distinct category must not be confused with Genesis 2:7.
7. Later Scripture speaks of body, soul, and spirit in varied contexts; each passage requires its own examination.
8. First Corinthians 15 describes a spiritual body, but it remains a body.
9. The human creature's life is received from God, not self-originated.

### Unresolved questions

1. How do the Hebrew terms translated “breath,” “soul,” and “spirit” function across Genesis, Job, Psalms, and Ecclesiastes?
2. How should Genesis 2:7 be read alongside Ecclesiastes 12:7, where the dust returns to the earth and the spirit returns unto God who gave it?
3. How do 1 Thessalonians 5:23 and Hebrews 4:12 distinguish or relate spirit, soul, and body?
4. What does Paul mean by “natural body” and “spiritual body” in 1 Corinthians 15, and how does that relate to the first Adam and the last Adam?
5. What exactly is the relation between the breath of life in Genesis 2:7 and the Spirit's work in the New Testament? The texts must be examined before any identity is asserted.
6. How does the Tree of Life relate to the creature's continued life and dependence upon God?

## 12. Provisional conclusion

**Genesis does not begin its account of man by presenting a spiritual person who later receives a body. It presents the LORD God forming man from the dust of the ground, breathing into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man becoming a living soul.**

That is the wording we must preserve.

This does not deny the existence of spiritual beings described elsewhere in Scripture. It does not settle every distinction among body, soul, and spirit. It does not define the full mechanics of life. It establishes what the text actually says about the formation of man.

The man is not his own Author. The dust is not the source of life. The breath is given by God. The creature lives by what he receives.

**The Author formed the man. The man did not form himself. The Author gave life. The man received it.**

The next question follows naturally from the text: after forming the man and giving him life, what did the LORD God do with him, and what provision, placement, work, relationship, and command did He establish?
