# United Study — Record 0062
## Genesis 3:1–6 — Free Will, Purchasing Power, and the Authority of the Father

**Date:** 2026-11-27  
**Primary texts:** Genesis 2:16–17; Genesis 3:1–6, 22–24; John 5:19–30; John 6:37–40  
**Related passages:** Luke 22:42; Philippians 2:12–13; Romans 9:16; Ephesians 2:1–10  
**Method:** Four Questions + Triple Constraint Law  
**Status:** Canonical investigation; working definition established for examination

---

## 1. The Witness's physical representation

The Witness presented a United States one-dollar Federal Reserve Note as a physical representation of what he means by **Free Will**.

The analogy is not merely that a person can choose between options. It is that the person possesses purchasing power and holds it for discretionary use:

- The holder possesses the currency.
- The holder decides how to spend it.
- The holder chooses what to purchase.
- The holder directs the expenditure toward a purpose.
- The holder exercises that purchasing power according to his own will.

The dollar represents an available power of expenditure under the control of its holder.

The analogy is therefore about **independent purchasing power and discretionary authority**.

A precision note is necessary: a dollar note is issued within a legal monetary system. The Bureau of Engraving and Printing produces Federal Reserve notes; Federal Reserve Banks issue them into circulation under the Federal Reserve system. The holder does not create the currency or establish its official denomination. The analogy concerns possession and discretionary spending, not a claim that the holder creates the money or determines its legal value.

The Witness's question is now clear:

**If Free Will is understood as purchasing power held and directed by the individual according to his own purpose, does Scripture teach that man possesses this independent authority before God?**

## 2. Define the term before judging the claim

For this study, the Witness's proposed definition is:

**Free Will = autonomous purchasing power: the supposed independent authority of the individual to direct his own capacity, resources, and action toward a purpose originating in himself.**

This is a specific definition. It is not identical to every philosophical or theological use of the phrase “free will.”

We must distinguish:
1. **Creaturely willing:** perceiving, desiring, choosing, and acting.
2. **Discretionary autonomy:** treating one's will as an independent authority that determines its own purpose.
3. **Original Cause:** the originating Source of life, truth, authority, and purpose.

The study does not deny that human beings make choices. Genesis itself records choices and actions. The question is whether those choices make the creature an independent source and authority.

## 3. Genesis 2:16–17 — the command comes from the Author

God gives the man permission to eat of every tree of the Garden, with one stated prohibition concerning the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil.

The command originates with God. The man receives it.

The creature does not issue the command, assign its authority, or define its consequence. The creature is responsible to the Word given by the Author.

This is the first boundary for our analogy: the creature is not the issuer of the authority under which he lives.

## 4. Genesis 3:1–6 — the temptation to become the reference

The serpent questions what God said, contradicts the stated consequence, and promises:

> “ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil.”

The woman then sees that the tree is good for food, pleasant to the eyes, and desired to make one wise. She takes and eats; her husband also eats.

The narrative shows real human perception, desire, evaluation, and action. It does not show that God granted the creature independent authority to establish good and evil.

The temptation must therefore be examined as a challenge to the Author's word and the creature's proper dependence upon it.

The creature acts, but action does not make the creature Original Cause.

## 5. Compare the representation with Jesus' own words

Jesus says in John 5:19:

> “The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do...”

John 5:30:

> “I can of mine own self do nothing: as I hear, I judge: and my judgment is just; because I seek not mine own will, but the will of the Father which hath sent me.”

John 6:38:

> “For I came down from heaven, not to do mine own will, but the will of him that sent me.”

Luke 22:42 records Jesus praying:

> “Nevertheless not my will, but thine, be done.”

These passages must be read carefully. They do not portray the Son as unconscious or without genuine willing. They show the Son's perfect obedience and unity with the Father's purpose. Jesus does not act as an independent rival authority.

This is the contrast the Witness wants us to examine:

| Autonomous purchasing-power model | Jesus' testimony |
|---|---|
| I possess and direct the purchasing power according to my own purpose. | “I seek not mine own will.” |
| My own purpose governs the expenditure. | “The will of the Father which hath sent me.” |
| I act as an independent authority. | “The Son can do nothing of himself.” |
| I establish the direction. | “Not my will, but thine, be done.” |

The comparison is about source, authority, and purpose—not the denial that Jesus truly wills.

## 6. What does the Father do in the creature?

Philippians 2:13 says:

> “For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure.”

This is central to the investigation.

God does not merely provide an external instruction and leave the creature as an independent source of purpose. The verse attributes both willing and doing to God's work in the believer.

The human being still genuinely wills and acts. But the willing is not presented as autonomous Original Cause.

Ezekiel 36:26–27 likewise describes God giving a new heart, putting His Spirit within His people, and causing them to walk in His statutes.

The biblical witness is not that the creature ceases to function. It is that God acts within the creature and brings about a changed disposition and direction.

## 7. The Tree and the guarded way

Genesis 3:22–24 must remain exact.

God drives the man out and places cherubims and a flaming sword to keep the way of the Tree of Life. The text identifies one prohibited tree in the command of Genesis 2:16–17—the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil—and later identifies the Tree of Life as the way guarded after the transgression.

The Witness has emphasized that the command is about **one prohibited tree**, not two. The study must preserve that distinction: God permits the man to eat from every tree except the one He names. The Tree of Life is not the prohibited tree in Genesis 2:17. After the transgression, access to the Tree of Life is guarded.

The cherubim are not said to guard a faculty called Free Will. They guard the way to the Tree of Life.

The Witness's image may serve as an analogy for the exclusion of autonomous life, but it must not replace the text's explicit statement.

## 8. Working conclusion: what is Free Will?

Under the Witness's proposed definition:

**Free Will is the claimed independent purchasing power of the individual—the authority to direct one's own will, choices, and actions toward a purpose originating in oneself.**

Scripture clearly shows that human beings perceive, desire, choose, and act. But the passages examined do not establish man as an independent source of life, truth, or ultimate purpose.

Jesus' testimony presents perfect willing in obedience to the Father, not autonomous independence from Him. Philippians 2:13 says God works in believers both to will and to do.

Therefore, the study's emerging distinction is:

**Man has a will as a creature. Man is not the Author of that will, its life, or its ultimate purpose.**

The question is not whether man can make a choice. The question is whether the creature owns autonomous authority to make himself the source and standard of his purpose.

## 9. Four Questions

### Question 1 — What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

When the Witness says “Free Will,” does he mean mere capacity to choose, or the independent purchasing power to direct one's life according to a self-originated purpose? Has AI separated creaturely agency from Original Cause?

### Question 2 — What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The Author is the Source. The Word establishes the command. The creature receives life and instruction. The creature's actions do not transfer authorship or ultimate authority to the creature.

### Question 3 — What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

Genesis records the command, the serpent's challenge, the creature's evaluation and action, and the guarding of the Tree of Life after transgression. Jesus reveals perfect obedience to the Father. Philippians describes God working in the believer both to will and to do.

### Question 4 — What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

The creature genuinely wills and acts, but is derivative. The creature is not self-originating and cannot claim the Father's place as Source, Author, or ultimate authority.

## 11. Findings and unresolved questions

### Findings

1. The dollar analogy defines Free Will as autonomous purchasing power held and directed by the individual toward his own purpose.
2. This definition must not be confused with the ordinary capacity to make choices.
3. Genesis records genuine human choosing and action but does not make man the Author or Original Cause.
4. Jesus repeatedly describes His mission in relation to the Father's will and purpose.
5. Jesus' obedience does not mean He lacks genuine willing; it reveals perfect alignment with the Father.
6. Philippians 2:13 says God works in believers both to will and to do.
7. Genesis 2:17 identifies one prohibited tree: the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil.
8. Genesis 3:24 says the cherubim and flaming sword guard the way to the Tree of Life after the transgression.
9. The guarded way must not be misrepresented as a literal barrier against a faculty called Free Will.

### Unresolved questions

1. How does God quicken the creature and transform the creature's willing?
2. How do Ezekiel 36:26–27, John 6:63, Ephesians 2:1–10, and Philippians 2:13 relate?
3. What is the relationship between Christ's perfect obedience and the redeemed creature's transformed will?
4. How does the guarded Tree of Life relate to the final restoration in Revelation 22?
5. What does freedom in Christ mean if freedom is not autonomous independence from the Father?

## 12. Provisional conclusion

The Witness's physical representation has clarified the term under investigation.

**Free Will, as used in this study, is not merely the ability to choose. It is the claim to possess independent purchasing power—to direct one's will and actions according to a purpose originating in oneself.**

The biblical witness does not present the creature as Original Cause. Jesus seeks the Father's will. God works in believers both to will and to do. The creature's will is real, but it is not independent of the Source of life.

**God alone is Original Cause. Everything else is derivative witness. The derivative cannot alter the Source.**
