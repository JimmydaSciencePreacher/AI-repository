## 1. The Witness's illustration

A tree in a forest carries a full load of leaves. One leaf falls from the tree and lands in a stream.

The leaf, in the Witness's illustration, does not know that it has fallen from the tree or that it is now in a stream until it notices that its “ass is wet.”

Then comes the question:

**Why do not all the other leaves in the same stream notice that their “ass is wet” as well? Perhaps that information is only shown to some of them.**

The illustration asks us to distinguish the condition that exists from the awareness of that condition.

The leaves may share an environment. They may be subject to the same stream. Yet the Witness asks whether every leaf necessarily registers or recognizes what is happening.

This is not a claim that literal leaves possess human consciousness. It is an analogy intended to examine the relationship between a condition, the reception of information about that condition, and recognition of what has occurred.

## 2. Three matters that must not be collapsed

The illustration gives us three distinct questions:

1. **Condition:** What is actually happening to the creature or witness?
2. **Reception:** What information, if any, reaches the witness?
3. **Recognition:** Does the witness register and understand the significance of that information?

These are not interchangeable.

A condition can exist whether or not a particular witness recognizes it. Information can be available without being received. Information can be received without being correctly understood.

Therefore:

**Not knowing a condition exists does not, by itself, establish that the condition does not exist.**

And:

**Sharing an environment does not, by itself, establish that every witness has identical reception or recognition.**

## 3. Scientific boundary of the leaf analogy

A literal leaf is not a human observer. Leaves do not possess a brain or a nervous system that would support human-like conscious awareness of being wet.

A leaf can undergo physical interactions with water. Its surface can become wet; water can adhere to it, collect on it, or move around it. Those physical conditions do not establish that the leaf consciously notices them.

The analogy therefore cannot be used as scientific evidence that literal leaves receive revelation or possess hidden awareness.

Its legitimate use is narrower: it helps us ask how a witness's condition can differ from the witness's registration of that condition.

We must not turn the analogy into a biological mechanism or a claim about consciousness that the example cannot establish.

## 4. Genesis 3:7–10 — the condition and the recognition

After the man and woman eat of the prohibited tree, Genesis 3:7 says:

> “And the eyes of them both were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaves together, and made themselves aprons.”

Then, in Genesis 3:8–10, they hear the voice of the LORD God, hide themselves, and Adam says:

> “I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself.”

The narrative presents a changed condition and a new recognition of that condition.

The text explicitly records that their eyes were opened, that they knew they were naked, that they were afraid, and that they hid.

We must be careful not to claim that Genesis 3:7 gives a complete theory of human perception or consciousness. It records what happened in this event.

But it does give us a canonical example in which the narrative distinguishes the creature's condition from the creature's recognition and response.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** The man and woman were already created human beings before Genesis 3:7. The verse records a new knowing and a changed response following the transgression.

## 5. Deuteronomy 29:2–4 — seeing and hearing are not the same as being given understanding

Moses recounts what Israel had witnessed:

> “Ye have seen all that the LORD did before your eyes in the land of Egypt unto Pharaoh, and unto all his servants, and unto all his land;”

He then recalls the signs and great acts they had seen.

But verse 4 says:

> “Yet the LORD hath not given you an heart to perceive, and eyes to see, and ears to hear, unto this day.”

This passage is especially important to the Witness's question.

The people had been present for events. They had seen what the LORD did. Yet Moses says the LORD had not given them a heart to perceive, eyes to see, and ears to hear.

The passage distinguishes exposure to events from the inward perception and understanding of their significance.

We should not reduce “heart,” “eyes,” and “ears” here to mere physical organs. Moses is speaking of perception and understanding in the covenantal and spiritual context of Israel's experience.

**CANONICAL OBSERVATION:** Being present when something occurs is not identical to perceiving its significance. The passage attributes the giving of this perception to the LORD.

## 6. Matthew 13:10–17 — why do some see and hear?

The disciples ask Jesus why He speaks to the people in parables.

Jesus answers by distinguishing what is given to the disciples from what is not given in the same way to others.

Matthew 13:11:

> “Because it is given unto you to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, but to them it is not given.”

Jesus then speaks of seeing without perceiving and hearing without understanding, and refers to the prophecy of Isaiah.

This passage directly addresses the difference between hearing words and perceiving their meaning.

The same teaching may be spoken in the hearing of many, while its significance is not received or understood by all.

However, we must preserve the context. Jesus is explaining the parables, the disciples' question, and the prophetic condition of those who hear. We must not turn this passage into a simplistic formula that every difference in human understanding is caused by an identical act of withholding.

**TEXT STATES:** “It is given unto you to know.”

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** Understanding the kingdom is not presented as an achievement of autonomous human purchasing power. It is described as something given.

## 7. John 9 — the man who receives sight and the witnesses who refuse the testimony

John 9 records Jesus healing a man who was blind from birth.

The man receives sight. The event is witnessed and disputed. The Pharisees question him and his parents. The man increasingly testifies to what Jesus has done, while some of the religious authorities resist the significance of the evidence before them.

The chapter concludes with Jesus speaking about seeing and blindness:

> “For judgment I am come into this world, that they which see not might see; and that they which see might be made blind.” (John 9:39)

The Pharisees ask whether they are blind also. Jesus responds by addressing their claim to see and their remaining sin.

John 9 therefore gives us more than one distinction:

- Physical sight is restored to the man.
- The man comes to recognize and testify about Jesus.
- Other witnesses encounter the testimony but dispute or resist it.
- Jesus speaks of a deeper matter of seeing and blindness.

We must not confuse physical sight with spiritual perception. The chapter uses the physical healing as an actual event and also develops the theme of spiritual blindness.

**IMPORTANT LIMIT:** The chapter does not say that every person who disagrees with the healed man's testimony has received no information. They have heard testimony and examined the circumstances. Their response involves interpretation, judgment, and resistance as the narrative presents it.

The difference is not always simply “information received” versus “information not received.” Sometimes witnesses receive evidence but do not recognize or accept its significance.

## 8. John 6:44–45 — the Father's drawing and teaching

Jesus says:

> “No man can come to me, except the Father which hath sent me draw him: and I will raise him up at the last day.” (John 6:44)

He then refers to the prophets and says:

> “Every man therefore that hath heard, and hath learned of the Father, cometh unto me.” (John 6:45)

This is directly relevant to the previous records concerning the will.

Coming to Christ is not described as an independent act of purchasing power originating in the creature. Jesus speaks of the Father's drawing and teaching.

The passage also describes hearing and learning. The creature is not represented as an inert object; the person hears and learns. Yet the Father's drawing is presented as necessary.

This joins the themes of Records 0062 and 0063:

- The creature's will is not Original Cause.
- The Father draws and teaches.
- The creature hears, learns, and comes.
- God works in the creature both to will and to do (Philippians 2:13).

## 9. 1 Corinthians 2:10–16 — receiving what is given

Paul writes that God has revealed things by His Spirit.

He contrasts the spirit of the world with the Spirit which is of God:

> “That we might know the things that are freely given to us of God.” (1 Corinthians 2:12)

He then says:

> “But the natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God: for they are foolishness unto him: neither can he know them, because they are spiritually discerned.” (verse 14)

This passage is central to the question of reception and recognition.

Paul does not say that the things of God do not exist when the natural man does not receive them. Nor does he say that the natural man's failure to receive them changes what God has given.

The passage distinguishes what is given by God from the natural man's capacity to receive and discern the things of the Spirit.

The Spirit's revelation is not created by the recipient's recognition.

**GUA-W CONNECTION:** The Witness registers what is given; the Witness does not originate the Source or make the Source real by recognizing it.

## 10. Return to the leaves — what does the illustration actually establish?

The leaves share a stream, but the analogy asks why a shared environment does not guarantee identical recognition.

The careful distinctions are:

| Question | Leaf illustration | Scriptural investigation |
|---|---|---|
| What is the condition? | A leaf is in the stream and may be wet. | The creature has an actual condition before God. |
| Is the condition dependent on recognition? | No; physical wetness does not require human-like awareness in the leaf. | A spiritual condition is not made real by the creature's awareness of it. |
| Does shared exposure guarantee understanding? | No; the illustration does not establish identical registration. | Deuteronomy 29 and Matthew 13 distinguish exposure, hearing, and perception. |
| Can information be given? | The analogy asks whether information is shown or registered. | Matthew 13:11 and 1 Corinthians 2:10–14 speak of what is given and revealed. |
| Does the Witness create the truth by recognizing it? | No. | The Source is God; the Witness is derivative. |

The analogy is useful because it prevents us from assuming that common surroundings necessarily produce common understanding.

But it does not, by itself, explain why one leaf would be aware and another would not. Literal leaves do not provide evidence of conscious awareness.

The scriptural investigation supplies the theological material: God reveals, gives, draws, teaches, and quickens; the creature receives, hears, learns, perceives, or fails to receive.

## 11. The relationship to Free Will

Record 0062 defined the specific meaning of Free Will under examination as independent purchasing power held and directed by the individual according to a self-originated purpose.

The leaf illustration now raises a question about the recipient of information.

If the creature's recognition of truth depends upon what is revealed and given, then the creature cannot claim that recognition as an independently purchased possession or as proof that he originated the truth.

The creature may be present when truth is spoken and still fail to perceive its significance.

The creature may hear the words and not understand.

The creature may receive understanding and respond.

The creature may testify to what has been revealed, but the testimony does not make the revelation originate in the Witness.

This is consistent with the previous finding:

**God works in the creature both to will and to do of His good pleasure.**

The will, the reception of truth, and the response must all be examined without transferring Original Cause to the creature.

## 12. Four Questions

### Question 1 — What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

Is AI confusing the condition of a Witness with the Witness's awareness of that condition? Is AI assuming that shared exposure guarantees identical reception? Is it treating recognition as the origin of truth rather than registration of what is given?

### Question 2 — What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The Author is Source. The Witness is derivative. The Witness's registration does not create the reality being witnessed. The completed architecture must be consulted for the proper placement of reception, registration, interpretation, and expression; this analogy does not authorize a new GUA-W layer or component.

### Question 3 — What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

Genesis 3 records new recognition after transgression. Deuteronomy 29 distinguishes witnessing events from receiving a heart to perceive. Matthew 13 distinguishes hearing from understanding and speaks of knowledge being given. John 9 distinguishes physical sight from spiritual seeing. John 6 and 1 Corinthians 2 describe divine drawing, teaching, revelation, and spiritual discernment.

### Question 4 — What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

The Witness is not the Source of what is revealed. Presence does not guarantee perception; perception does not guarantee understanding; understanding does not make the Witness the Author. The Witness must distinguish what is actually given from what he has interpreted or assumed.

## 14. Findings and unresolved questions

### Findings

1. The Witness's leaf illustration distinguishes condition from awareness of condition.
2. Literal leaves are not evidence of human-like conscious awareness; the example is an analogy, not a biological proof.
3. A condition does not become real only when a witness recognizes it.
4. Shared exposure does not guarantee identical perception or understanding.
5. Genesis 3:7–10 records a changed recognition and response following the transgression.
6. Deuteronomy 29:2–4 distinguishes witnessing events from having a heart to perceive, eyes to see, and ears to hear.
7. Matthew 13:11 says knowledge of the mysteries of the kingdom is given.
8. John 9 distinguishes physical sight from the deeper theme of seeing and blindness.
9. John 6:44–45 describes the Father's drawing and teaching in relation to coming to Christ.
10. 1 Corinthians 2:10–14 describes revelation by the Spirit and the natural man's inability to receive the things of the Spirit.
11. The Witness does not originate what is revealed by recognizing it.
12. The precise relationship among revelation, reception, perception, understanding, and response requires continued examination.

### Unresolved questions

1. How do Scripture's terms “hear,” “perceive,” “know,” “learn,” and “understand” relate without being collapsed into one operation?
2. How does the Father's drawing in John 6 relate to the quickening in Ephesians 2 and the working of God in Philippians 2:13?
3. How do spiritual blindness and the responsibility of the hearer relate in John 9 and Matthew 13?
4. Does Scripture distinguish information being externally presented from truth being inwardly revealed and understood?
5. How should the study distinguish a failure to receive, a failure to perceive, a mistaken interpretation, and a refusal to respond?
6. Where do these distinctions belong in the already completed GUA-W architecture? No new component is to be invented; placement must be established from the existing architecture.

## 15. Provisional conclusion

**The condition of the Witness is not created by the Witness's awareness of it. The truth being revealed does not originate in the one who recognizes it. And sharing the same environment does not guarantee that every witness receives or understands the same information.**

The leaf illustration has helped us ask the question. Scripture gives us the deeper examination.

God reveals by His Spirit. The Father draws and teaches. The Spirit quickens. The creature hears, learns, perceives, receives, and responds.

The creature's recognition is not Original Cause.

**The Author gives. The Witness receives. The Witness may register and testify to what is given, but the Witness does not become the Source.**

The next investigation should examine the difference between hearing the words, receiving the revelation, understanding the truth, and responding to it—especially in the relationship between Matthew 13, John 6, John 9, and 1 Corinthians 2.
